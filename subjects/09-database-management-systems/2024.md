First normal form requires atomic attribute values rather than composite/multivalued attributes in the usual relational model. It does not require a single-column key: several atomic columns may form one key. Foreign keys and multiple candidate keys are also allowed. Thus A and C. **Official answer:** A;C;D

**Derived answer:** A;C;D

## Solution

Two-phase locking first acquires locks and then, after the first release, only releases locks. The no-new-locks shrinking-phase rule proves C. Ordering transactions by their lock points proves conflict serializability, hence A. It does not release every lock immediately after each operation, and deadlock is possible: T1 holds X and waits for Y while T2 holds Y and waits for X. Thus A, C, D.

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

## Solution

Rename three copies P1, P2, P3 of Person and take their product using two binary cross products. Select equal cities and pairwise distinct pids, then project the city. This identifies exactly cities with at least three residents. With only one cross product, selection/projection/rename can expose at most two independently chosen source tuples, which cannot witness a third distinct person. Thus the minimum is 2, option B, in the stated algebra without aggregation or other operators.

# GATE 2024 CS2: Q56

**Official answer:** 50 to 50

**Derived answer:** 50

## Solution

For each of four attributes choose membership in X, membership in Y, or neither: 3⁴ assignments enforce disjointness. Exclude assignments with X empty (2⁴) and Y empty (2⁴), then restore the all-neither assignment counted twice. The number is 3⁴−2⁴−2⁴+1=81−16−16+1=50. This counts possible syntactic dependencies, not dependencies that necessarily hold on a particular instance.
