**Independently derived answer:** Maximum8, minimum0; A only with referential integrity

## Solution

In the GFG reproduction Student.rollno is the underlined key, while Enroll has the composite key(rollno,courseno). Each of the8 enrollment tuples can match at most one Student tuple, so the maximum join size is8. With only the stated keys, all enrollment roll numbers may be absent from Student, giving minimum0; no listed pair is then correct. If the intended additional constraint is Enroll.rollno referencing Student.rollno, every enrollment matches exactly once and both maximum/minimum are8, A. This foreign-key constraint is not stated and is not inferred from matching attribute names alone.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** B

## Solution

The candidate keys are{name,courseNo} and{rollNo,courseNo}. Thus name,rollNo,courseNo are prime attributes, and grade is non-prime. Dependencies from either key to grade satisfy3NF. The non-key determinants name and rollNo determine only another prime attribute, so these dependencies also satisfy3NF. But name→rollNo and rollNo→name violate BCNF because neither determinant determines courseNo. Highest listed normal form is3NF.

**Independently derived answer:** D

## Solution

The left expression selects all female names. The right expression finds a female student paired with a male student whose marks m satisfy female marks≤m, and projects that female name. Subtracting these removes every girl who fails to score strictly more than at least one boy. Remaining girls therefore have marks greater than EVERY boy. The primary-key name makes the projected subtraction unambiguous, and the stated existence of boys avoids a vacuous comparison.

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** D

## Solution

WHERE sex=M filters the outer rows to male employees before grouping by department. HAVING compares each such group’s average salary with the uncorrelated subquery SELECT AVG(salary) FROM Employee, which averages all employees company-wide. Therefore it returns departments whose male employees’ average exceeds the overall company average. The inner query has no male filter and is not restricted to the same department.
