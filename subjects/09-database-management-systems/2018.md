**Paraphrased problem context:** R is a many-to-one relation E1→E2. Both entity sets participate totally and |E1|>|E2|. Which holds? A each E1 entity has exactly one E2 partner; B some E1 entity has multiple E2 partners; C each E2 entity has exactly one E1 partner; D each E2 entity has at most one E1 partner.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

Treat the relation as a mapping from E1 into E2. “Many-to-one” imposes an upper bound of one on each E1 entity’s partners, and “total participation” imposes a lower bound of one. Together they force exactly one. On the E2 side total participation makes the map surjective, while the larger source cardinality invokes the pigeonhole principle: at least one E2 entity receives two or more partners.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Many-to-one permits at most one E2 target per E1 entity. Total participation of E1 supplies at least one, hence exactly one. Total participation of E2 and |E1|>|E2| ensure some E2 target has multiple preimages, not exactly one each. Thus A.

**Paraphrased problem context:** For Book(isbn,bname) and Stock(isbn,copies), all queries select B.isbn,S.copies and join on equal isbn. Queries 1–4 respectively use INNER, LEFT OUTER, RIGHT OUTER and FULL OUTER JOIN. Which result necessarily contains the other three? A 1; B 2; C 3; D 4.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

Divide possible output rows into matched pairs, unmatched Book rows and unmatched Stock rows. An inner join emits only the first group. A left join additionally emits unmatched Book rows with null Stock columns; a right join analogously preserves unmatched Stock rows. A full join includes all three groups. Even after the common projection, every row generated by a narrower join still occurs in the full join, with at least the same multiplicity.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

The full outer join includes every matched pair plus all unmatched rows from each side padded by nulls. The inner and either one-sided outer join produce subsets of those rows after the same projection. Thus Query4, D; this remains a superset under SQL bag multiplicities.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Relations r(A,B),s(B,C) have no nulls; s.B is primary and r.B foreign key to s.B. Q=r⋈σ_(B<5)(s). Which differs? A σ_(B<5)(r⋈s); B σ_(B<5)(r LOJ s); C r LOJ σ_(B<5)(s); D σ_(B<5)(r) LOJ s.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

An enforced non-null foreign key means each r tuple initially has exactly one matching s tuple. Consequently an unfiltered left outer join has no unmatched left tuples and behaves like an inner join. Applying the filter on r first also guarantees all its retained tuples match. Filtering only the right input before an outer join changes this: left tuples whose matches were filtered out still survive with null extension. This is why moving a predicate across an outer join requires more care than across an inner join.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Every r tuple has a matching s tuple by the non-null foreign key. Thus the unfiltered natural left join equals the inner join, and filtering B<5 before or after it gives the same matched rows. In C, filtering s first removes matches for r rows with B≥5, but the left join preserves those r rows with null-extended C. Q discards them instead. For example r={(a, 6)}, s={(6, c)} makes Q empty while option C retains(a, 6, NULL). Hence C alone is not equivalent.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Schemas: I R(rollno,courses), courses set-valued, rollno→courses; II R(rollno,courseid,email), rollno,courseid→email and email→rollno; III R(rollno,courseid,marks,grade), rollno,courseid→marks,grade and marks→grade; IV R(rollno,courseid,credit), rollno,courseid→credit and courseid→credit. Which is 3NF but not BCNF? A I; B II; C III; D IV.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

BCNF requires every nontrivial dependency’s determinant to be a superkey. 3NF allows a second possibility: its right-hand attribute is prime, meaning it belongs to some candidate key. For II, email together with courseid determines rollno and all attributes, while rollno together with courseid determines email. Neither email nor rollno alone determines courseid. Thus email→rollno violates BCNF but is permitted by 3NF because rollno is prime. Merely inspecting the designated primary key would miss the alternate candidate key.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

In schema II, candidate keys are {rollno, courseid} and {email, courseid}. All three attributes are prime, so each listed dependency satisfies 3NF. But email→rollno has a determinant that is not a superkey, violating BCNF. Schema I has a set-valued non-atomic attribute under the intended 1NF definition. Schema III has marks→grade with a non-superkey determinant and non-prime right side; schema IV has the analogous courseid→credit violation. Hence only schema II, B. The listed nontrivial dependencies are understood as a generating set, rather than literally excluding dependencies implied by them.
