**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** B

## Solution

S1 has T2→T1 from r2(x) before w1(x), but T1→T2 from w1(y) before w2(y), so it cycles. S2 has only T2→T1: both its reads and final write of y precede T1’s conflicting writes. S3 has only T1→T2, as w1(x) and w1(y) precede T2’s accesses. S4 cycles for the same opposing x and y directions as S1. Therefore S2 and S3 are conflict-serializable, B.

# GATE 2009: Q45 (printed CS Q45)

**Question:** [Official-source archive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view), member CS/CS2009.pdf, physical page 10. Consult original wording and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A

## Solution

I begins with candidate(a,b) pairs from r and removes those for which some c in s is missing from r, exactly relational division r÷s. II expresses the same condition: for every tuple u in s there exists a tuple v in r matching both u’s c and the candidate(a,b). Thus I and II are equivalent, A, including empty s because both restrict candidates to the projection of r. III reverses the quantifiers and requires all r tuples to share the candidate pair; IV is only an existential matching join, not universal division.

# GATE 2009: Q55 (printed CS Q55)

**Question:** [Official-source archive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view), member CS/CS2009.pdf, physical page 13. Consult original wording and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** D

## Solution

The innermost query finds non-blue part IDs. The middle query finds supplier IDs for catalog rows whose part is not in that set, i.e. suppliers of a blue part under the intended referential-integrity and non-NULL color assumptions. The outer NOT IN therefore returns names of suppliers who supply no blue parts, including suppliers with no catalog rows. The intended option is D, but its printed wording is imprecise: “have not supplied only blue parts” logically also includes mixed-color suppliers, whereas the SQL excludes them. A asks for any non-blue supply and also includes mixed suppliers; B and C concern suppliers with no non-blue supply / only blue supply, the opposite condition. Thus no option states the exact result under a literal reading. Do not present D as an officially verified or exact semantic equivalence.

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A

## Solution

The given primary keys are Suppliers.sid, Parts.pid, and Catalog.(sid,pid); Suppliers additionally has candidate key (sname,city). The question explicitly rules out functional dependencies other than those implied by primary and candidate keys. Consequently every nontrivial FD has a superkey as determinant, exactly the BCNF condition. All three relations are in BCNF, option A. Do not infer street→city from an address or infer sname alone is a key: the stipulated uniqueness is within a city and (sname,city) is explicitly the candidate key.
