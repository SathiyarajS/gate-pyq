

# GATE 2026 CS1: Q30

**Topic:** Functional-dependency implications.

**Question:** Consult [the original master question, page 22](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2026/QPs/CS1.pdf#page=22) for the full wording and options. This entry supplies an original solution, not a complete transcription.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 1 mark.

**Official and derived answer:** C;D

## Solution

C follows by augmentation and union: P determines R and Q determines S, so PQ determines both R and S. D is augmentation: adding Q to the determinant cannot destroy P’s determination of R. A fails, for example with tuples (P,Q,R)=(0,0,0),(1,0,1): P determines R, hence PQ does too, but Q does not. B fails in a relation with R equal to XOR(P,Q) over all four binary input pairs: the pair PQ determines R, while neither P nor Q alone does. An arbitrary fixed S can be appended to both counterexamples. Hence only C and D always hold.

## Solution

The two existentially quantified variables represent joined coordinates: v is the second coordinate of R and the first coordinate of S. The join condition is therefore R.Q=S.X. The output keeps only u, the first coordinate of R, namely attribute P. Joining on that equality and projecting P gives option B. Reversing the written operand order of the join does not alter this coordinate equality. The other options equate the wrong coordinates.

## Q31 — Database Management Systems: dependency preservation and normal forms

Paper page 23; key page 1. MSQ, 1 mark. **Answer: A, B, C.**

1. The 3NF synthesis construction can always produce a dependency-preserving 3NF decomposition for a relation and its functional dependencies. It starts from a minimal cover, groups appropriate determinants with their dependents, and includes a candidate-key relation if needed for losslessness. Thus A is true.
2. Since a relation in 3NF also satisfies 2NF and 1NF, the same dependency-preserving decomposition supplies existence witnesses for both lower normal forms. Hence B is true and the assertion of possible failure at 2NF in D is false.
3. BCNF does not always permit dependency preservation. For an explicit witness, consider R(A,B,C) with AB → C and C → B. Its candidate keys are AB and AC. The dependency C → B violates BCNF because C does not determine A.
4. Any BCNF decomposition must use proper subrelations rather than retaining all of ABC. Among proper subrelations, the only essential nontrivial projected dependency is C → B on BC; the projections on AB and AC contain no nontrivial dependencies. Thus the union of all available proper projections cannot imply AB → C: under that union, the closure of AB never acquires C. No collection of those BCNF components can preserve the original dependencies. This proves C.

**Common error:** A lossless BCNF decomposition does not automatically preserve every dependency. Losslessness and dependency preservation are separate requirements.

# GATE 2026 CS1: Q65

**Question:** [Official master paper page 46](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2026/QPs/CS1.pdf#page=46); consult original wording and options.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 6 to 6

## Solution

Every superkey contains some candidate key. Supersets of AB are AB,ABC,ABD,ABCD, giving four; supersets of AC are AC,ABC,ACD,ABCD, also four. The overlap consists of ABC and ABCD, so inclusion-exclusion gives 4+4-2=6 superkeys. Since the premise says these are the only candidate keys, no additional superkey can arise without containing AB or AC. The six distinct sets are AB,AC,ABC,ABD,ACD,ABCD.
