**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C

## Solution

Query1 counts B rows with A.balance≤B.balance, so a customer tied with t customers at its balance receives the count of higher-balance customers plus t, instead of plus 1. For two tied maximum balances it gives 2, not 1. Query2 uses A.balance<B.balance and adds 1 to the count, but its inner join produces no group for maximum-balance customers; those customers disappear. Thus neither query is correct (statement 4). They agree on the empty database but differ on every nonempty database because Query1 includes the maximum group and Query2 does not, establishing statement 1 under the allowed empty-database case. Hence statements 1 and 4, option C. Statement 5 is not a general performance guarantee.

# GATE 2006: Q70 (printed CS Q70)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005203119/GATE2006.pdf#page=11), physical page 11. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C and D as reproduced

## Solution

Apply AB→CD, AF→D, DE→F, C→G, F→E, G→A until closure stops. (CF)+={A,C,D,E,F,G}: C gives G then A; F gives E; AF gives D. (BG)+={A,B,C,D,G}: G gives A then AB gives C,D; neither E nor F is derivable. (AF)+={A,D,E,F}: AF gives D and F gives E, but nothing yields C or G. (AB)+={A,B,C,D,G}: AB gives C,D, C gives G; no E or F follows. Thus C is false and D is also false because its printed right side includes F. A and B are true. There is no unique single false option in these reproductions.
