

# GATE 2010: Q16 (printed CS Q6)

**Question:** [Official-source archive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view), member CS/CS2010.pdf, physical page 2. Consult original wording and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** Find the minterm expansion of f(P,Q,R)=PQ+Q¬R+P¬R. Use P,Q,R as the most-significant to least-significant bits of each minterm index.
Options: (A) m₂+m₄+m₆+m₇; (B) m₀+m₁+m₃+m₅; (C) m₀+m₁+m₆+m₇; (D) m₂+m₃+m₄+m₅.

## Solution

The expression is PQ+Q R̄+P R̄, with variable order P,Q,R. PQ covers minterms 6 and 7; Q R̄ covers 2 and 6; P R̄ covers 4 and 6. Taking their union gives m₂+m₄+m₆+m₇, option A. Repeated minterm 6 is included only once.

Teaching detail: To convert products to minterms, include missing variables: PQ=PQ¬R+PQR, Q¬R=¬PQ¬R+PQ¬R, and P¬R=P¬Q¬R+PQ¬R. Their binary assignments are 110,111; 010,110; and 100,110. OR is idempotent, so duplicate 110 is not counted again. The resulting truth vector in index order 0 through 7 is 00101011.

# GATE 2010: Q18 (printed CS Q8)

**Question:** [Official-source archive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view), member CS/CS2010.pdf, physical page 2. Consult original wording and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** A signed 16-bit integer P has two’s-complement hexadecimal representation F87B. What is the 16-bit two’s-complement representation of 8P?
Options: (A) C3D8; (B) 187B; (C) F878; (D) 987B.

## Solution

The 16-bit pattern F87B represents 0xF87B−0x10000=−1925. Multiplication by 8 gives −15400, which is within the signed 16-bit range. Its two’s-complement encoding is 65536−15400=50136=0xC3D8, A; equivalently shift F87B left three positions and retain 16 bits.

Teaching detail: The top hexadecimal digit F sets the sign bit, so interpreting F87B as an unsigned positive number without subtracting 2^16 gives the wrong signed value. The range check −32768≤−15400≤32767 confirms that multiplying by eight does not overflow here. Hexadecimal shifting also gives F87B×8=7C3D8, whose low four digits are C3D8.

# GATE 2010: Q19 (printed CS Q9)

**Question:** [Official-source archive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view), member CS/CS2010.pdf, physical page 3. Consult original wording and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** A 4-to-1 multiplexer has data inputs I₀=R, I₁=¬R, I₂=¬R, I₃=R. The select inputs are S₁=P and S₀=Q (S₁ is the more significant selection bit). Find its output f.
Options: (A) ¬(P⊕Q⊕R); (B) P⊕Q⊕R; (C) P+Q+R; (D) ¬(P+Q+R).

## Solution

The select bits are S₁=P and S₀=Q. Inputs 0 and 3 equal R, while inputs 1 and 2 equal R̄. Thus f=R when P=Q and f=R̄ when P≠Q, exactly f=P⊕Q⊕R, option B.

Teaching detail: The four select combinations are PQ=00→R, 01→¬R, 10→¬R, 11→R. XOR with P⊕Q flips R exactly in the middle two cases, establishing the answer for both possible R values. For example P=Q=R=0 yields f=0, immediately excluding the complemented-XOR and complemented-OR choices.

# GATE 2010: Q41 (printed CS Q31)

**Question:** [Official-source archive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view), member CS/CS2010.pdf, physical page 7. Consult original wording and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** A combinational circuit consists entirely of 2-input NOR gates. Define NOR(x,y)=¬(x+y). Its wiring is a=NOR(P,Q), b=NOR(Q,R), c=NOR(P,R), d=NOR(Q,R), u=NOR(a,b), v=NOR(c,d), and f=NOR(u,v). What is f?
Options: (A) ¬(Q+R); (B) ¬(P+Q); (C) ¬(P+R); (D) ¬(P+Q+R).

## Solution

The top second-level NOR gives complement(complement(P+Q)+complement(Q+R))=(P+Q)(Q+R)=Q+PR. The bottom gives (P+R)(Q+R)=R+PQ. The final NOR complements their sum: f=complement(Q+PR+R+PQ)=complement(Q+R), since PR is absorbed by R and PQ by Q. Thus A.

Teaching detail: De Morgan’s law turns a NOR of two complemented OR expressions into an AND of the original OR expressions. The identity (X+Y)(X+Z)=X+YZ then simplifies the two intermediate branches. Finally Q+PQ=Q and R+PR=R remove the terms containing P entirely, so the output is independent of P despite P appearing in the wiring.

# GATE 2010: Q42 (printed CS Q32)

**Question:** [Official-source archive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view), member CS/CS2010.pdf, physical page 7. Consult original wording and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** Two positive-edge-triggered T flip-flops have both T inputs tied to logic 1. The external clock drives the first, whose output is Q₀; Q₀ directly clocks the second, whose output is Q₁. Starting from Q₁Q₀=00, what are the next four settled values after successive external positive clock edges?
Options: (A) 11,10,01,00; (B) 10,11,01,00; (C) 10,00,01,11; (D) 11,10,00,01.

## Solution

Both T inputs are tied to 1, so each flip-flop toggles on a positive clock edge. Q₀ toggles on every external edge; Q₁ is clocked by Q₀ and therefore toggles only on Q₀ transitions 0→1. Starting at Q₁Q₀=00, the settled states after successive external edges are 11,10,01,00. Option A; the ripple clock is not a common synchronous clock.

Teaching detail: Trace the clock-sensitive events: on edge 1, Q₀ rises 0→1 and triggers Q₁, giving 11. On edge 2, Q₀ falls 1→0, so Q₁ stays 1, giving 10. Edge 3 raises Q₀ and toggles Q₁ back to 0, giving 01. Edge 4 only lowers Q₀, returning to 00. Ripple propagation delays are ignored when listing settled states.
