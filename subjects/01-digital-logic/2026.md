# GATE 2026 CS1: Q22

**Status:** Partial subject-year coverage. Independently derived and matched against the published official key; independent second derivation review passed; final-key revision check remains pending.

**Question summary (paraphrased):** For 8-bit sign-magnitude X=10110100 and Y=01001100, identify overflowing operations among X+Y, X-Y, -X+Y, and -X-Y.

Operations in the listed order are options A, B, C, D.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 1 mark. Paper variant CS1 (forenoon); the official key labels the exam session 3.

**Official answer:** B;C

**Derived answer:** B;C

## Solution

Seven magnitude bits give the representable interval [-127,127]. X has sign 1 and magnitude binary 0110100 = 52, hence X=-52; Y=76. The four mathematical results are 24, -128, 128, and -24, respectively. Only the middle two fall outside the interval. Therefore B and C overflow. Sign-magnitude is essential: treating these words as two’s-complement integers would solve a different problem.

## Q21 — Digital Logic: XOR identity

Paper page 16; key page 1. MSQ, 1 mark. **Answer: A, C.**

1. Let x=NOT P and y=Q. The given expression becomes (x OR y) XOR (x AND y).
2. If both x and y are zero, both inner expressions are zero and the result is zero. If exactly one is one, the OR is one and the AND zero, so the result is one. If both are one, both inner expressions are one and the result is zero. Thus the expression is x XOR y.
3. Substituting back gives (NOT P) XOR Q, exactly option C. Complementing one XOR input complements the XOR result, so this is also NOT(P XOR Q), option A.
4. B is P XOR Q, the opposite function. D complements both XOR inputs and therefore equals P XOR Q as well, so neither is equivalent to the given expression.

**Truth-table check:** For (P,Q)=00,01,10,11, the output is respectively 1,0,0,1. This is XNOR of P and Q.

**Visual check:** Option A's bar covers the whole XOR expression; C complements P only; D complements each input separately. Those distinctions were confirmed in the rendered source.

## Q37 — Digital Logic: saturating counter next-state logic

Paper page 26; key page 1. MCQ, 2 marks. **Answer: B.**

1. A D flip-flop takes its next state from its D input. Thus D1 and D0 must equal the corresponding next-state bits in all eight input/current-state rows.
2. Use variable order (P,Q1,Q0). The rows where D1=1 have binary indices 1,2,3,7. Adjacent pairs (1,3), (2,3) and (3,7) yield the implicants (NOT P)Q0, (NOT P)Q1 and Q1Q0. Therefore D1=(NOT P)Q1 OR (NOT P)Q0 OR Q1Q0.
3. The rows where D0=1 have indices 0,2,3,6. Adjacent pairs (0,2), (2,3) and (2,6) yield (NOT P)(NOT Q0), (NOT P)Q1 and Q1(NOT Q0). Therefore D0=(NOT P)(NOT Q0) OR (NOT P)Q1 OR Q1(NOT Q0).
4. These two expressions are the pair in B. Each of the other offered pairs fails at least one row: A gives the wrong D0 at 000; C gives the wrong D1 at 000; D gives the wrong D1 at 100.

**Computational check:** Evaluated B on all eight assignments and compared the resulting two-bit value with min(3,current+1) for P=0 and max(0,current−1) for P=1. All eight matched.

**Visual check:** Inspected both the full next-state table and each complement bar in the four expression pairs.

## Q48 — Digital Logic: minimum sum of products

Paper page 36; key page 2. MSQ, 2 marks. **Answer: B, D.**

1. There are exactly six prime implicants. In bit-pattern notation using variable order (P,Q,R,S), where a dash is a don't-care coordinate, they are:
   - 0--1: (NOT P)S, covering minterms 1,3,5,7.
   - -10-: Q(NOT R), covering 4,5,12,13.
   - 001-: (NOT P)(NOT Q)R, covering 2,3.
   - -010: (NOT Q)R(NOT S), covering 2,10.
   - 1-10: PR(NOT S), covering 10,14.
   - 11-0: PQ(NOT S), covering 12,14.
2. The first two prime implicants are essential: minterm 1 can only be covered by 0--1, and minterm 4 only by -10-. Together they leave 2,10,14 uncovered.
3. No single remaining prime implicant covers all three residual minterms. Therefore at least two further terms are required, for a minimum of four product terms overall. Each additional prime has three literals, so the minimum covers have ten literals in total.
4. Option B adds 001- and 1-10: these cover 2,3 and 10,14, completing the function. Option D adds 11-0 and -010: these cover 12,14 and 2,10, also completing it. Both achieve the four-term, ten-literal minimum.
5. Option A leaves minterm 14 uncovered. Option C leaves minterm 2 uncovered. Hence neither even represents the target function.

**Completeness check:** Exhaustively enumerated all 81 four-variable cubes, retained cubes containing no zero minterm, and extracted the six maximal cubes above. Enumerating prime-implicant covers found minimum size four. There is a third minimum cover using the two essential implicants plus -010 and 1-10; it is not one of the offered choices. The answer does not claim that B and D are the only possible minimal expressions.

**Visual check:** Confirmed the minterm list and all complement bars in options A–D directly from rendered page 36.
