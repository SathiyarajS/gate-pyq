**Official answer:** 3.0 : 4.0

**Derived answer:** 3

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2016.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

The repeating output sequence has six distinct phases. Its three occurrences of 0 require different future successors1, 2, 3, so they cannot share one deterministic internal state. At least six states require ceil(log₂6)=3 flip-flops; a six-state cycle with output decoding attains this. The official accepted interval3.0 : 4.0 is preserved even though the minimum derived here is 3.

# GATE 2016 CS2: Q17 (printed CS Q7)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 27](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** -1.0 : -1.0

**Derived answer:** −1; sum-only timing may also tie at127

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2016.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

With A=00000001 and B=11111111 (−1), the low-order1+1 generates a carry that propagates through all seven higher positions, giving the standard full-ripple worst case and official−1. If only the eight sum outputs matter and final carry-out is excluded, B=01111111 (127) also propagates the carry into the sign position and can tie the longest sum-settling delay under a conventional equal-delay full-adder model. Thus uniqueness depends on the precise output/timing model; −1 is a valid worst-case operand but need not be the only one.
