Options:
(A) x′+y′
(B) x+y
(C) x+y′
(D) x′+y

## Solution

**Paraphrased problem context:** A decimal digit is encoded in four-bit BCD: 0=0000,…,9=1001. A combinational circuit must output 1 iff the digit is at least 5. Using only AND,OR,NOT gates, what is the minimum number of gates?
Options:
(A) 2
(B) 3
(C) 4
(D) 5

## Solution

1. Write the BCD inputs A,B,C,D with weights8,4,2,1. For valid digits0–9, the threshold predicate digit≥5 is A OR[B AND(C OR D)].

2. This uses three gates: one OR for C+D, one AND with B, and one OR with A. All four inputs are essential on valid BCD pairs (0/8,1/5,4/6,4/5 respectively), and a two-gate AND/OR/NOT network cannot express both the independent A case and the nested B∧(C∨D) case.

3. Invalid BCD encodings10–15 are unspecified/don’t-care, as the source produces a digit.

4. Teaching check: Use digit weights 8,4,2,1. Digits 8 and 9 are detected by the 8-bit; digits 5,6,7 require the 4-bit and at least one low bit. Values 10–15 are not decimal digits supplied by this source, so they are don’t-care cases rather than additional truth-table requirements.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A two-input mux selects data input X when control Z=0, and Y when Z=1. Which connections realize f=T+R without extra hardware?

(A) X=R,Y=1,Z=T
(B) X=T,Y=R,Z=T
(C) X=T,Y=R,Z=0
(D) X=R,Y=0,Z=T

## Solution

1. A two-input mux computes Z′X+ZY. Set X=R,Y=1,Z=T: output=T′R+T=T+R by absorption.

2. When T=0 it passes R; when T=1 it outputs1. These are precisely the two cases of OR, with no additional gate.

3. Teaching check: Mux select is a Shannon expansion: f=Z′X+ZY. Taking Z=T splits OR into f|T=0=R and f|T=1=1. This supplies the data connections directly, without trying every arbitrary wiring pattern.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A synchronous two-bit counter uses T flip-flops with outputs Q2 (MSB) and Q1 (LSB), driven by a common clock. Its required cycle is 0→2→3→1→0. The figure wires XNOR(Q1,Q2) to T2; the unknown X drives T1. What is X?
Options:
(A) Q2′
(B) Q2+Q1
(C) (Q1⊕Q2)′
(D) Q1⊕Q2

## Solution

1. Use state order Q2Q1=00→10→11→01→00. Since a T flip-flop toggles exactly when T=1, the LSB excitation Q1 XOR Q1(next) takes values0,1,0,1 at states00,10,11,01.

2. This is Q1 XOR Q2. The MSB excitation is its complement, agreeing with the pictured XNOR feedback into T2.

3. Thus X=Q1⊕Q2.

4. Teaching check: For each present state, calculate toggle=present bit XOR next bit. At 00,10,11,01 the LSB toggles 0,1,0,1 and the MSB toggles 1,0,1,0. These complementary sequences explain the given XNOR and required XOR together.

**Independently derived answer:** B under conventional parallel complemented-signal implementation

**Paraphrased problem context:** A 4-bit carry-lookahead adder is built only from AND,OR,NOT,NAND,NOR gates. Inputs are supplied in true and complemented forms. Each gate has unit delay, and the carry network is a two-level AND–OR implementation. What is the overall propagation delay? The exact complement-generation/netlist convention is not specified.
Options:
(A) 4 time units
(B) 6 time units
(C) 10 time units
(D) 12 time units

## Solution

1. With input complements supplied, pi=ai XOR bi and its complement can each be formed in two gate levels, while gi=ai bi needs one. Expanding each carry as an OR of generate/propagate products adds two levels after the propagate signals, so carries and their complements can be ready at time4 (parallel OR/NOR outputs).

2. A sum si=pi XOR ci can then be realized in two further AND–OR levels using these true/complement signals, giving time6. This is the conventional gate-delay model behind B; it assumes sufficient fan-in for the stipulated two-level carry network and parallel generation of needed complements.

3. The wording does not specify a unique netlist or minimum-area implementation; adding serial inversion or a three-level XOR implementation can produce a different delay, so6 is not a delay guaranteed for every allowed circuit.

4. Teaching check: The critical path goes through propagate formation, the expanded carry network and sum formation. Under the conventional parallel true/complement implementation these cost 2+2+2 levels. A statement about this implementation is different from a universal claim about every legal gate netlist.

Options:
(A) 11000100
(B) 10011100
(C) 10100101
(D) 11010101

## Solution

Step 1: 11111010 represents 250−256=−6 and 00001010 represents 10. Their product is −60, which fits in eight signed bits.

Step 2: Its encoding is 256−60=196=11000100, option A. A full sixteen-bit product is its sign extension, 1111111111000100; the options clearly request the eight-bit representation.

Step 3: Teaching check: Negating 11111010 by invert-and-add-one gives magnitude 00000110=6, so the signed operand is −6. The product −60 fits in [−128,127], avoiding overflow. Check the chosen output by decoding 11000100 as 196−256=−60.
