

# GATE 2006: Q8 (printed CS Q8)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005203119/GATE2006.pdf#page=1), physical page 1. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A under the intended two-phase storage model

**Paraphrased problem context:** A free-running clock has 50% duty cycle; waveform f changes only at its falling edges. Which two-stage clocked-D circuit delays f by 180°? Textual circuit options specify (stage-1 D input, stage-1 clock, stage-1 output wired to stage-2 D, stage-2 clock); every final output is stage-2 Q. The source does not resolve whether “clocked D flip-flops” means level-sensitive stages or edge-triggered devices.
Options:
(A) (¬f, ¬clk, Q̄, clk)
(B) (¬f, clk, Q, ¬clk)
(C) (f, clk, Q, ¬clk)
(D) (f, ¬clk, Q̄, clk)

## Solution

1. In A the first D storage stage is enabled by inverted clock, receives ¬f, and exposes its complemented output, restoring f. The second stage is driven by the original clock.

2. Under a level-sensitive two-phase interpretation, a change of f at the falling edge propagates through the first stage during the low half-cycle and becomes visible through the second at the next rising edge: delay T/2, or 180 degrees of the clock. D gives the wrong polarity; B and C use the opposite phase order.

3. If these are literal falling-edge-triggered flip-flops, f changes at the sampling edge and setup/hold timing is unspecified, so reliable half-cycle delay is not guaranteed. The reproduction says clocked D flip-flops without resolving this distinction.

4. Teaching check: Distinguish signal inversion from delay: feeding ¬f and then using Q̄ restores f’s polarity. The complementary clocks create the intended half-period separation. Reliable edge-triggered sampling additionally requires setup/hold margins, which the statement does not supply for f changing exactly at a clock edge.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** Two 2:1 multiplexers are cascaded. The first has input 0=x, input 1=¬y and select=z. Its output drives input 0 of the second; second input 1=x and select=y. A select value of 0 chooses input 0. Which expression represents output f(x,y,z)? Here + means OR and juxtaposition AND.
Options:
(A) x¬z+xy+¬yz
(B) x¬z+xy+¬(yz)
(C) xz+xy+¬(yz)
(D) xz+x¬y+¬yz

## Solution

1. The first mux outputs x¬z+¬yz. The second selects that output when y=0 and x when y=1, giving f=¬y(x¬z+¬yz)+xy=x¬y¬z+¬yz+xy.

2. By absorption/consensus this equals x¬z+xy+¬yz, option A. The first mux’s lower input is complemented y, not y.

3. Teaching check: For a 2:1 mux use output=¬select·input 0+select·input 1. Apply this formula first to z, then to y. Keeping the complemented lower input of the first mux is essential; overlooking that inversion changes the truth table.

# GATE 2006: Q36 (printed CS Q36)

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** Two three-bit unsigned words a₂a₁a₀ and b₂b₁b₀ are added with carry-in c. Which Boolean expression is the block carry-generate function (outgoing carry generated independently of c)? Here + means OR, juxtaposition AND, and ⊕ XOR. The expanded alternatives below follow the clearer GateForum typesetting.
Options:
(A) a₂b₂+a₂a₁b₁+a₂a₁a₀b₀+a₂a₀b₁b₀+a₁b₂b₁+a₁a₀b₂b₀+a₀b₂b₁b₀
(B) a₂b₂+a₂b₁b₀+a₂a₁b₁b₀+a₁a₀b₂b₁+a₁a₀b₂+a₁a₀b₂b₀+a₂a₀b₁b₀
(C) a₂+b₂+(a₂⊕b₂)(a₁+b₁+(a₁⊕b₁)(a₀+b₀))
(D) a₂b₂+¬a₂a₁b₁+¬(a₂a₁)a₀b₀+¬a₂a₀¬b₁b₀+a₁¬b₂b₁+¬a₁a₀¬b₂b₀+a₀¬(b₂b₁)b₀

## Solution

1. Let gi=ai bi and pi=ai+bi. The carry generated independently of c0 is G=g2+p2g1+p2p1g0.

2. Expanding gives a2b2+a2a1b1+b2a1b1+a2a1a0b0+a2b1a0b0+b2a1a0b0+b2b1a0b0, the seven terms of A. This tests whether the three-bit sum generates an outgoing carry when c0=0, not whether an incoming carry propagates.

3. Teaching check: At one bit, carry-out is gᵢ+pᵢ·carry-in, where gᵢ=aᵢbᵢ and the OR-based propagation pᵢ=aᵢ+bᵢ is sufficient. Substitute this recurrence across bits 0,1,2 and collect the terms independent of the external carry. Those terms form the block generate function.

# GATE 2006: Q38 (printed CS Q38)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005203119/GATE2006.pdf#page=5), physical page 5. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** Proven cover given; option letter unresolved

**Paraphrased problem context:** For f(w,x,y,z)=Σm(5,7,11,12,13,15), only one input changes at a time. A transition between adjacent ON minterms must never cause a momentary zero. Which cube cover avoids such static-1 hazards? The options below explicitly use GateForum p11; its D begins wzy, while the corresponding GFG term is too blurred to establish. This question therefore retains a source discrepancy rather than silently repairing an option.
Options:
(A) {¬wxz, wx¬y, x¬yz, xyz, wyz}
(B) {wxy, ¬wxz, wyz}
(C) {wx·overline(yz), xz, w¬xyz}; the printed bar spans yz, retaining the source bar-scope ambiguity
(D) {wzy, wyz, wxz, ¬wxz, x¬yz, xyz}

## Solution

1. For a two-level AND-OR cover to avoid static-1 hazards on single-input changes, every adjacent pair of ON minterms must share a cube. The adjacent pairs are(5,7),(5,13),(7,15),(11,15),(12,13),(13,15).

2. The cover{xz,wyz,wx¬y} covers all six and exactly the ON set. Equivalently, the six edge cubes are{¬wxz,x¬yz,xyz,wyz,wx¬y,wxz}.

3. Option A lacks a cube spanning 13↔15; option C leaves 12↔13 unbridged if its first term is read as wx¬y¬z. GateForum instead has a continuous bar spanning yz: under the grouped-complement reading wx¬(yz), C also includes the OFF minterm 14 and is not a cover of f.

4. Neither reading makes C valid. The first term of GFG option D is too blurry to verify as wx¬y, while GateForum prints wzy, duplicating wyz and omitting minterm 12; its option B is also not a cover of the stated ON set.

5. Thus a corrected D containing wx¬y would work, but neither reproduction warrants silently claiming that printed D is correct. Original wording/key are needed to resolve the letter.

6. Teaching check: Ordinary truth-table equivalence does not by itself guarantee hazard freedom. For every adjacent ON pair, one product term must remain asserted throughout the changing input. The independently derived valid cover {xz,wyz,wx¬y} can be verified by checking both its ON set and its coverage of all six ON edges.

# GATE 2006: Q39 (printed CS Q39)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005203119/GATE2006.pdf#page=5), physical page 5. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** B only under separate-literal complement reading; printed grouped bar is ambiguous

**Paraphrased problem context:** An ordinary binary adder adds two n-bit two’s-complement numbers aₙ₋₁…a₀ and bₙ₋₁…b₀. Its result bits are cₙ₋₁…c₀ and carry-out is c_out. Which expression detects signed overflow? Let a=aₙ₋₁,b=bₙ₋₁,c=cₙ₋₁. Both reproductions print a continuous bar over ab in the second term of B; its scope must not be silently normalized.
Options:
(A) c_out·¬(a⊕b)
(B) ab¬c+overline(ab)c [continuous source bar; intended separate-literal reading: ab¬c+¬a¬bc]
(C) c_out⊕c
(D) a⊕b⊕c

## Solution

1. Signed overflow occurs when both operand sign bits agree and the result sign differs: V=ab¬c+¬a¬bc. For example, two positive operands overflowing the positive range give a=b=0,c=1.

2. GFG physical page 5 and GateForum physical page 11 both display a continuous bar spanning ab in B’s second term. Read literally as a grouped complement, the printed expression is ab¬c+¬(ab)c, not the standard overflow expression.

3. A counterexample is four-bit −8+1=−7: a=1,b=0,c=1, with no signed overflow, but ¬(ab)c=1. Therefore printed B works only if its spanning bar is intended to mean separately complemented literals. That intended reading must be stated explicitly; the original official wording and key are unavailable.

4. The other expressions are not general overflow detectors. With four-bit −1+1=0, c_out=1,c=0 and C incorrectly signals overflow. D fails on −8+(−1), whose sign bits are a=b=1,c=0: D=0 although overflow occurs. A misses positive overflow 7+1 because c_out=0.

5. Teaching check: Compute overflow from operand and result signs, then check the scope of every complement. Carry-out is an unsigned carry indicator, not signed overflow. The retained B answer is conditional on the separate-literal reading, not a verified literal-source or official-key answer.

Options:
(A) g₀=Σm(1,2,3,6,10,13,14,15) [GateForum; GFG: 1,2,5,6,10,13,14,15]
(B) g₁=Σm(4,9,10,11,12,13,14,15)

2. As cross-checks, g0 has{0,1,6,7,10,11,12,13}, g1 has{1,2,3,6,10,13,14,15}, and g3 has{4,9,10,11,12,13,14,15}; the other options attach these sets to wrong output bits.

3. Teaching check: Gray-code minterm indices are the current Gray words, not the underlying binary n values. For example n=0 gives h=0000 and next g=0001, so the present-state index zero belongs to g₀’s ON set. This immediately distinguishes some mislabeled alternatives.
