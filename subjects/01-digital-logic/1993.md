**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005204139/GATE1993.pdf#page=4), physical page 4. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** B: exclusive NOR

**Essential problem context (paraphrased):** Let t=NOR(x,y),u=NOR(x,t),v=NOR(y,t),f=NOR(u,v). Identify the function.

## Solution

The first output is t=¬x¬y. Then u=¬(x+t)=¬x·y and v=x·¬y. These are the two unequal-input cases. The final NOR complements their OR, giving f=¬(¬x·y+x·¬y)=xy+¬x¬y. Hence f is one exactly when x=y, the exclusive-NOR function. Evaluating 00,01,10,11 yields 1,0,0,1, distinguishing it from XOR and from a single NOR gate.

**Essential problem context:** Recovered diagram: S1 loops for a=1 and goes to S2 for a=0; S2 loops for b=1 and goes to S1 for b=0. Which input restriction makes the machine have a stable state? A ab=1; B a+b=1; C ¬a+¬b=0; D ¬(ab)=1; E ¬(a+b)=1. Here + means Boolean OR. The section permits multiple correct alternatives.

**Independently derived answer:** Conditional on recovered diagram: B is the exact condition; A,B,C are sufficient restrictions under the literal MSQ reading.

**Official answer:** No official key located.

## Solution

Use the recovered third-party state diagram, not the unrelated NAND-controller question numbered 19 elsewhere. The arrows give S1→S1 when a=1 and S1→S2 when a=0; S2→S2 when b=1 and S2→S1 when b=0. Hold the inputs constant and test each pair. At (a,b)=(0,0), both states leave themselves, so there is no fixed point. At (0,1), S2 is stable and S1 reaches it. At (1,0), S1 is stable and S2 reaches it. At (1,1), both states are stable. Thus at least one stable state exists exactly when a∨b=1, giving B as the necessary-and-sufficient, least restrictive condition. A and C both force a=b=1 and hence are also sufficient restrictions, though not necessary. Because the original section allows multiple correct alternatives and the wording does not explicitly ask for the least restrictive condition, A,B,C is defensible under a merely sufficient-condition reading. D permits (0,0), while E requires (0,0), so neither guarantees stability. **Essential problem context (paraphrased):** Three common-clock JK stages use J/K as a signal and its complement; stages shift Q1→Q2→Q3, and the first receives ¬Q3. Starting at 000, what function is performed?

## Solution

With complementary J and K inputs, each JK stage copies the signal on J at the next edge. The transition is therefore (Q1,Q2,Q3)→(¬Q3,Q1,Q2). Starting at 000 gives 100,110,111,011,001,then 000. The six distinct states form the cycle, so it is a three-stage Johnson counter with modulus six. It uses shift-register structure, but its specified closed-loop function from the given initial state is the modulo-6 count; the unused states are not part of that cycle.

# GATE 1993: Q44 (printed II-B Q9)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005204139/GATE1993.pdf#page=10), physical page 10. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** descriptive, 5 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** Universal with a fixed logic-1 input; false for constant-free combinational half-adder networks.

**Essential problem context (paraphrased):** Only half adders are available. Show how any binary Boolean function can be implemented using them. The source does not specify whether fixed logic inputs are allowed.

## Solution

A half adder on inputs x,y produces S=x XOR y and C=xy. Its carry output directly implements AND. If a fixed logic 1 is allowed as an input, its sum output on (x,1) is NOT x. Thus half adders provide the functionally complete basis {AND,NOT}. For example OR follows from De Morgan: x OR y = NOT((NOT x) AND (NOT y)). A concrete four-half-adder OR network uses H1(x,1).sum=nx, H2(y,1).sum=ny, H3(nx,ny).carry=z, H4(z,1).sum=x OR y; unused outputs may be ignored. Fan-out and tied inputs are permitted wiring operations, not additional gates.

To implement any two-input truth table f, form the minterms NOT x AND NOT y, NOT x AND y, x AND NOT y, and x AND y. OR together exactly those minterms whose truth-table rows have output 1. Each minterm uses available NOT/AND networks, and each OR uses the construction above. Empty selection gives constant 0, obtainable as H(x,x).sum; all four selected give constant 1. The same sum-of-products argument works for any finite number of Boolean inputs.

There is an essential qualification: without an independent logic-1 supply (or another non-zero-preserving primitive), the claim is false. Both half-adder outputs are 0 when both inputs are 0. By induction in topological order, every signal in a finite combinational network of such half adders is 0 when all external variables are 0. Therefore it cannot compute NOT x or the constant-1 function. Tying both inputs to the same variable gives XOR=0 and AND=x, not a source of 1. Feedback without a specified initial state is not a valid general combinational implementation. The intended universality proof consequently requires explicit permission to supply constant 1.

# GATE 1993: Q55 (printed II-C Q19)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005204139/GATE1993.pdf#page=12), physical page 12. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** DESCRIPTIVE, 5 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** AB,P → A⁺B⁺: 00,0→10; 00,1→01; 01,0→10; 01,1→10; 10,0→01; 10,1→01; 11,0→01; 11,1→00.

**Essential problem context (paraphrased):** Two common-clock D flip-flops hold A,B. The two NAND inputs to the final NAND for D_A are NAND(¬A,B), NAND(¬A,¬P); those for D_B are NAND(P,¬B), NAND(¬P,A). Derive the state transition table.

## Solution

Read the feedback labels and output inversion bubbles before simplifying: the upper two first-stage gates share ¬A, and the bottom gate uses A and ¬P. A NAND of two NAND outputs implements an OR of their underlying products. Therefore D_A=¬A·B+¬A·¬P=¬A(B+¬P), and D_B=P·¬B+¬P·A. On a clock edge the new states are these D values evaluated from the old A,B and sampled P, not from a newly updated flip-flop.

For P=0 the equations become A⁺=¬A and B⁺=A, so old states with A=0 go to 10 and those with A=1 go to 01. For P=1 they become A⁺=¬A·B and B⁺=¬B. Evaluating 00,01,10,11 gives 01,10,01,00 respectively.

Complete table (old AB: next for P=0 / next for P=1):
00: 10 / 01
01: 10 / 10
10: 01 / 01
11: 01 / 00.
This table includes all eight state/input combinations. Crossing wires without junction dots are not additional connections. Both source diagrams agree on the labelled feedback paths.
