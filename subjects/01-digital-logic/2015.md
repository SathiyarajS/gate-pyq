**Paraphrased problem context:** A 4-bit Johnson counter starts at 0000. Choose its decimal sequence. A 0,1,3,7,15,14,12,8,0; B 0,1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15,0; C 0,2,4,6,8,10,12,14,0; D 0,8,12,14,15,7,3,1,0.

## Solution

A Johnson counter feeds the complement of one end bit into the other end of a shift register. With the right-shift/MSB-insertion convention, bits fill with ones and then zeros, producing eight states before returning. Reversing the bit-weight convention reflects each four-bit word and converts D into A. Therefore the official D is consistent but not uniquely forced by the direction-free wording; B and C are not this eight-state twisted-ring cycle.

Baseline derivation and retained qualifications: Complemented end-bit feedback with right shifts gives 0000→1000→1100→1110→1111→0111→0011→0001→0000, the official D. With left shifts and the opposite end-bit feedback, the equally valid Johnson counter gives 0, 1, 3, 7, 15, 14, 12, 8, 0, option A. The question does not specify shift direction or bit-weight orientation; D is conventional, not uniquely implied.

## Solution

Recognize XOR, which is addition modulo 2. Swapping its two inputs preserves their sum modulo 2, proving commutativity. Grouping three inputs either way gives (p+q+r) mod 2, proving associativity. A truth table for only two inputs can show commutativity directly, but associativity needs the three-input relation or this algebraic argument.

Baseline derivation and retained qualifications: The truth table is XOR. Both (a XOR b) XOR c and a XOR (b XOR c) give parity of the three bits, and a XOR b=b XOR a. Thus the operation is associative and commutative, A.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Minimum JK flip-flops for synchronous count output sequence 0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 0, 0, …?

## Solution

The repeated output at each count needs an internal phase bit: the two appearances of 0 must have different next outputs eventually and cannot be the same deterministic state. The sequence has period 8, requiring eight states and at least 3 bits. A3-bit modulo 8 counter with the low bit suppressed in the output realizes it, so 3 JK flip-flops suffice.

Teaching detail:
Although only four output values appear, the repeated occurrences need different internal states. If the two consecutive zeros were the same state of an autonomous deterministic counter, they would have the same successor and could not later advance to 1. The complete repeating pattern has period 8, so at least 8 internal states and ceil(log 2 8)=3 flip-flops are required. A modulo 8 counter outputs its two upper bits, holding each value for two clocks, which attains the bound.

Conversely that assignment makes every original summand zero, verifying the result ABCD and exactly one minterm.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Construct a full adder from two half adders and an OR gate, and a four-bit ripple-carry adder from four such full adders. Each AND/OR gate delay is 1.2 microseconds; each XOR gate delay is 2.4 microseconds. Determine propagation delay, distinguishing a lumped full-adder timing model from gate-level overlapping propagation.

## Solution

Each XOR delay is 2.4 microseconds and each AND/OR delay 1.2. A full adder made from two half adders has P=A XOR B, sum=P XOR Cin and Cout=AB+P*Cin. Its worst input-to-output delay is 4.8. Multiplying this black-box delay by four gives the published 19.2. However in a gate-aware ripple circuit all four P signals are computed in parallel. The first carry settles by 4.8, and each later carry needs only AND+OR=2.4 more, giving carry delays 4.8, 7.2, 9.6, 12; the last sum also settles by 9.6+2.4=12. Thus 19.2 is a conservative lumped-stage estimate, not the tight standard gate-level propagation bound under simultaneous operand arrival. A fixed zero initial carry or different arrival assumptions can further change sensitized timing. The intended timing model is not explicit.

Teaching detail:
At gate level all bit propagate signals P=A XOR B become ready after 2.4 microseconds, and generates G=AB after 1.2. The first carry using P·Cin followed by OR is ready after 4.8. Later carries traverse only an AND and OR using already-ready P, adding 2.4 each: 7.2, 9.6 and 12.0. The last sum is also ready at 9.6+2.4=12.0. By contrast, treating each full adder as a black box with worst-case delay 4.8 and multiplying by four gives the published 19.2. These calculations use different overlap assumptions; the source/key discrepancy must remain visible rather than silently selecting a timing model.
