

# GATE 2015 CS1: Q21 (printed CS Q21)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 9](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D for one shift orientation; A for the reverse

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2015.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

Complemented end-bit feedback with right shifts gives 0000→1000→1100→1110→1111→0111→0011→0001→0000, the official D. With left shifts and the opposite end-bit feedback, the equally valid Johnson counter gives 0, 1, 3, 7, 15, 14, 12, 8, 0, option A. The question does not specify shift direction or bit-weight orientation; D is conventional, not uniquely implied.

## Solution

The truth table is XOR. Both (a XOR b) XOR c and a XOR (b XOR c) give parity of the three bits, and a XOR b=b XOR a. Thus the operation is associative and commutative, A.

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2015.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

Let the old outputs be(D, J), where J denotes the JK output. At a common positive edge, Dnext=J and Jnext=J XOR D. From initial(1, 0), the state sequence is(0, 1),(1, 1),(1, 0), repeating every three edges. The JK output including its initial value is 0110110..., A. Both devices sample old outputs at the edge; propagation delays do not make the updates sequential.

**Official answer:** 3

**Derived answer:** 3

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2015.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

The repeated output at each count needs an internal phase bit: the two appearances of 0 must have different next outputs eventually and cannot be the same deterministic state. The sequence has period8, requiring eight states and at least3 bits. A3-bit modulo8 counter with the low bit suppressed in the output realizes it, so 3 JK flip-flops suffice.

## Solution

For the outer complement to be1, every inner OR term must be0. D′=0 requires D=1. With D=1 and C=0, AC′D+A′C′D=A+A′=1, impossible; hence C=1. Then A′C=0 requires A=1, and AB′=0 requires B=1. Conversely A=B=C=D=1 makes all inner terms zero. The function is exactly ABCD, one minterm.

# GATE 2015 CS2: Q65 (printed CS Q65)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 51](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 19.2

**Derived answer:** Official19.2 uses lumped full-adder delays; gate-aware model gives12

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2015.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

Each XOR delay is 2.4microseconds and each AND/OR delay1.2. A full adder made from two half adders has P=A XOR B, sum=P XOR Cin and Cout=AB+P*Cin. Its worst input-to-output delay is 4.8. Multiplying this black-box delay by four gives the published19.2. However in a gate-aware ripple circuit all four P signals are computed in parallel. The first carry settles by 4.8, and each later carry needs only AND+OR=2.4 more, giving carry delays4.8, 7.2, 9.6, 12; the last sum also settles by 9.6+2.4=12. Thus19.2 is a conservative lumped-stage estimate, not the tight standard gate-level propagation bound under simultaneous operand arrival. A fixed zero initial carry or different arrival assumptions can further change sensitized timing. The intended timing model is not explicit.
