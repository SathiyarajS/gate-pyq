**Paraphrased problem context:** Which circuit is NOT equivalent to a two-input XNOR? Let the upper and lower inputs be x and y. The structural descriptions below replace the source gate drawings; NOT, AND, OR and XOR denote gates.

(A) XOR(x,y) followed by NOT
(B) NOT both inputs, feed them to XOR, then NOT its output
(C) NOT x, then XOR with y
(D) Invert y to y'; feed x and y' to an AND followed by NOT and also to an OR; AND those two outputs and then invert

## Solution

A complements XOR. B complements both XOR inputs and then its output, also producing XNOR. C complements exactly one XOR input, again XNOR. In D, let top input be x and lower input y. The inner branches are (xy′)′ and x+y′; the final NAND gives [(xy′)′(x+y′)]′, which is the complement of XOR(x, y′), hence XOR(x, y). Thus D is not XNOR. The wire bridge was checked in the source.

A truth-table check makes the distinction explicit. XNOR has outputs 1,0,0,1 for inputs 00,01,10,11. Inverting both XOR inputs leaves XOR unchanged, whereas inverting exactly one input complements XOR, explaining A through C. For D, write u=x and v=y'. The product (uv)'(u+v) equals u XOR v. The final inversion yields NOT(x XOR y')=x XOR y, whose outputs are 0,1,1,0, the opposite of the target.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Find the simplified sum-of-products form of (P+Q'+R')(P+Q'+R)(P+Q+R'), where + is OR, juxtaposition is AND, and ' denotes complement.

(A) P'Q+R'
(B) P+Q'R'
(C) P'Q+R
(D) PQ+R

## Solution

Using (P+A)(P+B)=P+AB, the product becomes P+(Q′+R′)(Q′+R)(Q+R′). The first two residual factors reduce to Q′. Then Q′(Q+R′)=Q′R′, yielding P+Q′R′, B.

First combine the first two factors by taking their common term X=P+Q': (X+R')(X+R)=X+R'R=X. The remaining product is (P+Q')(P+Q+R'). Apply the same common-term identity with common P to obtain P+Q'(Q+R')=P+Q'R'. This two-stage simplification avoids a long expansion of all eight products and makes the absorption of Q'Q=0 explicit.

**Paraphrased problem context:** What is the minimum number of D flip-flops needed for a modulo-258 counter?

(A) 9
(B) 8
(C) 512
(D) 258

## Solution

k flip-flops provide at most 2^k states. Since2^8=256<258≤512=2^9, at least 9 are needed; a 9-bit counter with suitable reset logic suffices. Hence A.

A modulo-m counter needs m distinct states before repeating. Each flip-flop stores one binary bit, so k of them encode at most 2^k different combinations. Taking a ceiling gives k=ceil(log2 258)=9. The 9-bit device has extra possible combinations, but logic can reset or redirect the sequence after 258 states. Options 258 and 512 confuse the number of states with the number of storage bits.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Three synchronous D flip-flops have outputs P,Q,R. The source circuit connects R directly to D_P, a NOR of P and R to D_Q, and an AND of Q and the complemented R output to D_R. All sample simultaneously at a clock edge. If PQR=010 immediately before the edge, what is PQR afterward?

(A) 000
(B) 001
(C) 010
(D) 011

## Solution

Tracing the wires, including the bridge crossings, gives next-state equations P_next=R, Q_next=NOT(P OR R), and R_next=Q AND NOT R. The upper two gate inputs are P and R; the lower AND gate uses Q and the complemented output of R. All D flip-flops update simultaneously from the pre-edge values. For PQR=010 this gives P_next=0, Q_next=1, R_next=1, so the next state is 011, option D.

For a D flip-flop, the output after the edge equals its D input just before the edge. Substitute the old values P=0,Q=1,R=0 into all three equations: D_P=0; D_Q=NOT(0 OR 0)=1; D_R=1 AND NOT 0=1. Assemble the new values in P,Q,R order as 011. Do not feed the newly computed R back into P in this same step; that would model sequential assignments rather than simultaneous hardware sampling.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Three synchronous D flip-flops have outputs P,Q,R and connections D_P=R, D_Q=NOR(P,R), D_R=AND(Q,NOT R). All sample simultaneously. Starting with all flip-flops reset to zero, how many distinct output states occur?

(A) 3
(B) 4
(C) 5
(D) 6

## Solution

From the figure the synchronous next-state equations are P_next=R, Q_next=NOT(P OR R), and R_next=Q AND NOT R. Starting from the power-on reset 000 gives 000→010→011→100→000. These four states are distinct, and the return to 000 repeats the same deterministic cycle. Thus the number of reachable states from reset is 4, option B; the question does not ask for all eight possible register patterns.

Evaluate every transition with the same pre-edge rule. At 000 the next bits are 0,1,0. At 010 they are 0,1,1. At 011 they are 1,0,0. At 100 they are 0,0,0. The reset state is included in the count, so there are four distinct states before repetition, not three transitions away from reset. A three-bit register can represent eight states, but its next-state logic and initialization restrict the reachable sequence.
