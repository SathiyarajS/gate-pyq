**Paraphrased problem context:** Let ⊕ be XOR and ⊙ XNOR. Which identity is false? A ¬(P⊕Q)=P⊙Q; B (¬P)⊕Q=P⊙Q; C (¬P)⊕(¬Q)=P⊕Q; D (P⊕¬P)⊕Q=(P⊙¬P)⊙¬Q.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

XOR is 1 exactly when its inputs differ; XNOR is its complement. Complementing one input changes “different” to “equal”, proving B, while complementing both preserves whether they differ, proving C. In D simplify the inner gates first, rather than assuming associativity transfers between XOR and XNOR. A concrete counterexample is Q=0: the left evaluates to 1 and the right to 0, regardless of P.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

In D, P XOR not-P=1, so the left side is not-Q. Meanwhile P XNOR not-P=0, and 0 XNOR not-Q=Q. The sides are opposite, so D is false. Complementing a single XOR input gives XNOR, while complementing both leaves XOR unchanged, validating the other identities.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Two positive-edge D flip-flops share a clock. External in feeds D1; Q1 feeds D2; output is Q2. How many states have a transition to themselves for at least one input?

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

Both flip-flops sample their inputs at the same edge, so Q2 receives the old Q1, not the newly sampled input. With states written q1q2, input 0/1 transitions are: 00→00/10; 01→00/10; 10→01/11; 11→01/11. Only 00 and 11 appear among their own successors. This explicit table also shows why the mixed states cannot remain unchanged for either input.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Let the state be(q1, q2). On a positive clock edge the next state is(in, q1), because both flip-flops sample old values simultaneously. A self-loop requires in=q1=q2, possible only for00 with input0 and 11 with input1. Exactly2 states have self-loops.

**Paraphrased problem context:** An unsigned 8-bit fixed-point format is b7b6b5b4b3.b2b1b0. Values are i 31.500, ii 0.875, iii 12.100, iv 3.001. Which cannot be exact? A all; B ii only; C iii,iv only; D i,ii only.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

The three fractional bit weights are 1/2,1/4,1/8. Thus every stored value is an integer multiple of 1/8 within [0,255/8]. Multiplying a proposed value by 8 must yield an integer between 0 and 255. As examples, 31.5=11111.100₂ and 0.875=00000.111₂. Values 12.1 and 3.001 satisfy the range constraint but fail the granularity constraint; being small enough does not make a decimal fraction exactly representable.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Three fractional bits give increments of 1/8 and maximum31.875. Both31.500=252/8 and 0.875=7/8 are representable. But12.100·8=96.8 and 3.001·8=24.008 are not integers, so iii and iv are not exact, C.

**Paraphrased problem context:** F(P,Q,R,S) has on-minterms {0,2,5,7,9,11} and don’t-cares {3,8,10,12,14}. All other minterms are off. How many essential prime implicants exist?

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

A prime implicant is a maximal Boolean cube containing no off-cell; don’t-cares may enlarge it but need not be covered. Essential means at least one required on-cell has no alternative prime covering it. In dash notation, the five primes cover required cells as follows: 01-1→{5,7}; 0-11→{7}; 10--→{9,11}; -01-→{2,11}; -0-0→{0,2}. Therefore 5,9,0 respectively force three primes. A prime that covers only redundantly covered on-cells is not essential, even if it appears in a possible cover.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Enumerating maximal cubes that contain an on-minterm and no off-minterm gives five prime implicants: 01-1, 0-11, 10--, -01-, -0-0 in P, Q, R, S order, where a dash means that variable is absent. Minterm5 is covered only by 01-1; minterm9 only by 10--; minterm0 only by-0-0. These three primes are essential. The remaining on-minterms2, 7, 11 each have more than one covering prime. Therefore exactly3 prime implicants are essential. The enumeration checks all81 four-variable cubes and treats only the listed d entries as don’t cares.
