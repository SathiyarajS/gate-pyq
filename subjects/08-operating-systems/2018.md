**Paraphrased problem context:** A memory access costs M when resident and total D when it faults. Observed mean is X. Find fault rate: A (D−M)/(X−M); B (X−M)/(D−M); C (D−X)/(D−M); D (X−M)/(D−X).

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

Partition accesses into the two mutually exclusive outcomes. With fault probability f, their probabilities are f and 1−f, not two independent rates. The weighted mean X=M+f(D−M) interpolates between M at f=0 and D at f=1. These endpoints immediately distinguish the fault probability from C, which is the hit probability. For ordinary D>M, valid observations satisfy M≤X≤D.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

If f is the fault rate, X=(1−f)M+fD=M+f(D−M). Therefore f=(X−M)/(D−M), B, assuming the stated fault access time D is the total fault-case time and D≠M.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Three processes share four identical resource instances. Each can need at most K, requesting and releasing one at a time. Find the largest K guaranteeing no deadlock.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

If every unfinished process has fewer than its maximum requirement, each may hold at most K−1 while waiting for another instance. With K=2, three such waiting processes can hold at most three resources, leaving the fourth available to let one finish. Releasing its resources then enables the others. For K=3, a feasible allocation (2,1,1) consumes all resources while each process can still request one; no request can be granted. This supplies both safety and a tight counterexample.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

For n=3 processes each requiring at most K identical resources, a sufficient and tight universal bound is R≥n(K−1)+1: for K=2, any allocation in which all three processes still need another instance leaves at least one of the four resources free; for K=3 the allocation (2,1,1) can instead exhaust all resources with all processes waiting. With R=4, K=2 satisfies4≥4, while K=3 permits holdings2, 1, 1 and all waiting. Thus the largest guaranteed-safe K is 2, assuming completed processes release their resources.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Available (E,F,G)=(3,3,0). Allocation rows P0…P3 are (1,0,1),(1,1,2),(1,0,3),(2,0,0); Max rows are (4,3,1),(2,1,4),(1,3,3),(5,4,1). Choose A safe already; B unsafe but safe with one extra E; C likewise extra F; D likewise extra G.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

Banker’s safety test asks whether there exists an order in which each process can obtain all its remaining need and release its allocation. Need=Max−Allocation, computed component by component. After hypothetically finishing a process, Work increases by its original Allocation, not by its Max: any temporary extra resources lent from Work are returned, cancelling out. Initially P0 and P2 are both eligible. Exhibiting one complete order suffices; the algorithm need not show that every arbitrary request order is safe.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Subtract Allocation from Max to obtain remaining needs P0=(3, 3, 0), P1=(1, 0, 2), P2=(0, 3, 0), P3=(3, 4, 1). Starting Work=(3, 3, 0), complete P0 to get(4, 3, 1), then P2 to get(5, 3, 4), then P1 to get(6, 4, 6), then P3. This safe sequence proves A.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A size-N buffer uses empty=0, full=N, mutex=1. Here empty counts readable items and full writable slots. Producer executes wait(P),wait(mutex),add,signal(mutex),signal(Q); consumer wait(R),wait(mutex),remove,signal(mutex),signal(S). Choose A full/full/empty/empty; B empty/empty/full/full; C full/empty/empty/full; D empty/full/full/empty.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

Follow the definitions rather than familiar variable names. Before adding an item, the producer must reserve one writable slot, so it waits on the semaphore initially N. After insertion it creates a readable item, so it signals the semaphore initially 0. The consumer performs the reverse transfer. Waiting for a resource before taking mutex also avoids holding the buffer lock while blocked for another process to make progress. Each successful pair of operations preserves the bounded-buffer accounting.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Use the definitions supplied, which reverse the usual semaphore names. full starts N and counts producer-writeable slots; empty starts0 and counts readable items. The producer waits(full) and signals(empty); the consumer waits(empty) and signals(full), each using mutex for buffer access. Thus P=full, Q=empty, R=empty, S=full, C.

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2018.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Disk has 4 platters, 200 cylinders and 256 sectors/track. Requests [sector,cylinder,platter]: [120,72,2],[180,134,1],[60,20,0],[212,86,3],[56,116,2],[118,16,1]. Head starts sector100,cylinder80 moving upward. SSTF movement costs are stated as 20 mW per 100 cylinders plus 15 mW per reversal; rotation/platter costs ignored. Find the intended total.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

SSTF chooses nearest pending cylinder, not the next request in the current direction. At 80 choose 86 (6 away), then 72 (14 away). From 72, 116 is 44 away versus 20 at 52; then 134 is nearest. The remaining requests are 20 and 16. Direction is up, down, up, up, down, down, so there are three reversals relative to the initially upward head. Sector and platter numbers do not affect the stipulated cost model.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

SSTF chooses cylinders80→86→72→116→134→20→16, with distances6, 14, 44, 18, 114, 4 totaling200. The head starts upward and reverses at 86, 72, 134, three times. Treating the supplied numbers as additive movement costs gives(200/100)*20+3*15=85, the official answer. Physical power in milliwatts cannot in general be added over sequential operations to obtain total consumption; energy or a time-based average would require durations and appropriate units. Thus85 is the intended numerical cost, not a dimensionally justified physical power calculation.
