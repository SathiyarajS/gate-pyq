**Independently derived answer:** A

## Solution

Larger blocks amortize per-I/O overhead across more bytes, often improving sequential disk throughput, but waste more unused space in partially occupied final blocks. Thus better throughput with poorer space utilization, A, under the conventional workload comparison. It is not a universal guarantee for every random-access workload.

## Solution

Dynamic linking can keep library code out of the executable, share physical library pages among processes, and permit compatible library updates without relinking each application. It also introduces runtime loading, relocation, or symbol-resolution work. Faster program startup is therefore not the characteristic advantage over static linking, option C. The other listed benefits are the conventional shared-library benefits; actual page-fault and startup measurements can depend on caching and workload.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** D

## Solution

Ignoring context-switch cost, FCFS, shortest-remaining-time, and distinct static priorities run the two initial CPU bursts consecutively for 10 ms each. Their I/O completions stay offset by 10 ms, giving 20 ms of CPU work in a 100 ms steady-state period. With round robin at a 5 ms quantum, the initial bursts finish at 15 and 20 ms, so the processes enter I/O only 5 ms apart. Under the usual ready-queue ordering at simultaneous quantum expiry and I/O completion, this pattern repeats every 105 ms, giving utilization 20/105≈19.05%, below 20%. Thus D is the conventional answer: fine time slicing makes their long I/O intervals overlap more.

# GATE 2003: Q79 (printed CS Q79)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005203452/GATE2003.pdf#page=12), physical page 12. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C

## Solution

Each page-table level has 2^10 entries of four bytes, so each allocated table uses 4096 bytes. The two code pages occupy top-level index 0; the two data pages starting at 0x00400000 occupy index 1; the stack page at 0xFFFFF000 occupies index 1023. Three second-level tables are therefore required, plus the first-level table. Total page-table storage is 4×4096=16384 bytes=16 KiB, option C. The five mapped code/data/stack pages are not themselves included in page-table storage.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** B

## Solution

Initially give P permission to run by setting S=1 and block Q with T=0. P waits on S at W, prints both zeroes, and signals T at X. Q waits on T at Y, prints both ones, and signals S at Z. Each process therefore hands permission to the other only after completing its pair, forcing 00,11,00,11,… starting with 00. This is B. Separate locks do not coordinate the processes, and initializing both cross-signalling semaphores to one would permit initial interleaving.

# GATE 2003: Q81 (printed CS Q81)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005203452/GATE2003.pdf#page=12), physical page 12. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C

## Solution

Use one common binary semaphore S, initially one, as a mutex around both print statements of either process: wait(S) at W and Y, signal(S) at X and Z. Each critical section then emits either 00 or 11 without interleaving, so any run between opposite digits is a concatenation of equal pairs and has even length. That is C. For example, when independent locks or two initially available cross-signalling permissions let P emit its first zero, Q one one, and P its second zero, the forbidden substring 010 occurs. Mutual exclusion of entire pairs is the needed property, not merely exclusion within each individual process.
