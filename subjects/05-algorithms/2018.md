**Paraphrased problem context:** For a simple undirected graph, I says DFS has no cross edge between unrelated vertices; II says every graph edge joins BFS depths differing by exactly 1. Which must hold? A I only; B II only; C both; D neither.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

If an undirected DFS edge led to a still-unvisited vertex, DFS would explore it before returning, making that vertex a descendant. If the vertex is already on the recursion stack it is an ancestor; an edge to a completed unrelated subtree would have discovered the current vertex earlier. For BFS, shortest-path distances differ by at most 1 across an edge. A triangle rooted at one vertex places the other two at depth 1 and still has an edge between them, disproving “exactly”.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

In undirected DFS, an edge to an undiscovered vertex is explored before the current DFS call completes, so every non-tree edge joins an ancestor and descendant; no cross edges, making I true. BFS edges can connect equal-depth vertices, for example the edge joining the two non-root vertices of a triangle. The necessary inequality is |i−j|≤1, not equality1. Thus only I, A.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Matrices F1…F5 have dimensions 2×25,25×3,3×16,16×1,1×1000. Multiplying p×q by q×r costs pqr scalar operations. Which adjacent original pairs are directly multiplied in an optimal parenthesization? A F1F2 and F3F4; B F2F3; C F3F4; D F1F2 and F4F5.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

The dynamic-programming state M[i,j] is the minimum scalar cost of multiplying the consecutive subchain; a split k adds the costs of its two subchains and their final product. This systematically tests all parenthesizations without a local greedy assumption. In the winning chain, F3F4 first produces 3×1 at cost 48; F2 times that gives 25×1 at cost 75; F1 times that gives 2×1 at cost 50; multiplying F5 costs 2000. Only the first operation multiplies two original matrices directly.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Use matrix-chain DP: M[i, j]=min_k(M[i, k]+M[k+1, j]+d[i−1]d[k]d[j]). Dimensions are2, 25, 3, 16, 1, 1000. Pair costs are150, 1200, 48, 16000; three-matrix optimal costs246, 123, 3048; four-matrix costs173, 25123. Splitting the full chain after F1, F2, F3, F4 costs75123, 9198, 48246, 2173 respectively. The unique optimum is((F1(F2(F3F4)))F5), costing48+75+50+2000=2173. Its only directly multiplied original pair is F3F4, C.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Name graph vertices top T, left L, bottom-left B, bottom-right C, right R. Edges: TL=4,LB=4,TB=1,BC=4,TC=3,TR=x,CR=5. Choose x to maximize the number of minimum-weight spanning trees; report that number.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

The cut property forces a uniquely lightest edge across a cut into every MST. Across {B}, TB=1 is strictly less than LB=4 and BC=4; across {C}, TC=3 is less than BC=4 and CR=5. Contract these two forced edges to a central component. Connecting L has two tied cost-4 choices. Connecting R has two choices only when x=5. These choices are independent and each yields four edges with no cycle, so their product counts actual spanning trees, not merely edge subsets.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Name the top vertex T, bottom-left B, bottom-right C, left L and right R. Edge TB of weight1 is uniquely cheapest across the cut {B}; TC of weight3 is uniquely cheapest across {C}. Both must be in every MST, independently of x. Contract them. L can connect to that component by either of its two weight4 edges. R can connect by TR of weight x or CR of weight5. At x=5 both choices tie, giving2*2=4 MSTs. Otherwise only one right edge is cheapest and there are2. The remaining BC edge would form a cycle with the two mandatory edges and cannot be chosen. Thus the maximum count is 4.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Capacity is 11 kg. Single available items (weight,value) are 1:(10,60),2:(7,28),3:(4,20),4:(2,24). Items cannot split. Greedy scans descending value/weight, taking items that fit. Find optimal value minus greedy value.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

The ratio order is item 4 then 1 then 3 then 2. Taking item 4 leaves 9 kg, so item 1 no longer fits. Item 3 then fits and leaves 5 kg, too little for item 2. To verify optimality, item 1 alone gives 60 and cannot pair with even the smallest other item. Without item 1, feasible pairs (2,3),(2,4),(3,4) have values 48,52,44; all three weigh 13 and are infeasible. Thus exhaustive feasible alternatives confirm the optimum.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Value/weight ratios order the items as4, 1, 3, 2, with ratios12, 6, 5, 4. Greedy takes item4 (weight2, value24), skips item1 because it no longer fits, then takes item3 (weight4, value20), and skips item2. Its value is 44. The best feasible subset is item1 alone, value60: pairs among2, 3, 4 have maximum52, all three weigh13, and item1 cannot accompany any other item under capacity11. Hence60−44=16.
