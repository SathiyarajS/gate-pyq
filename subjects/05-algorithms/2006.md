## Solution

Thus three is the standard generic cubic cost, not a universal lower bound for every coefficient choice. Original official wording and key remain unavailable.

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C under the standard swap-based comparison

## Solution

Selection sort places the minimum remaining item in its final location with at most one exchange per pass, hence at most n−1 swaps. Standard swap-based insertion sort, quicksort and heapsort can use more. Thus selection sort, C, in the conventional worst-case exchange-count comparison. A shift-based insertion implementation can avoid an explicit swap operation, so literal source-code swap counts are implementation-dependent.

# GATE 2006: Q17 (printed CS Q17)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005203119/GATE2006.pdf#page=2), physical page 2. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** B with constant extra space; A also possible with a stack

## Solution

Scanning right to left while retaining the largest value seen reports exactly the elements strictly larger than every element to their right. This is Θ(n) time and O(1) extra working space, yielding the usual B. However A is also achievable in linear time if extra storage is allowed: scan left to right, keep candidate leaders in a decreasing stack, and pop every prior candidate≤the new value before pushing it. Each entry is pushed/popped at most once; the final stack contains the leaders. Since the prompt does not require constant space, traversal direction alone does not make B uniquely correct.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** D

## Solution

In an undirected DFS, every non-tree edge joins a vertex to an ancestor. Since u is a DFS leaf, all its neighbors lie on its single root-to-u tree path. Let w be its highest ancestor-neighbor. The tree path w to u plus the edge uw forms a cycle containing every neighbor of u, since all those neighbors lie between w and u. Degree at least two ensures a genuine cycle. Thus D. Two distinct cycles sharing only a root refute the asserted common cycle; other DFS graphs refute the universal common neighbor and separator assertions.

## Solution

Set prefix differences D[0]=0 and D[k]=sum(a[1..k])−sum(b[1..k]). A span (i,j) has equal sums iff D[i−1]=D[j]. Every D[k] lies in [−n,n], so an array of 2n+1 entries can store the earliest index of each difference. Scan once and maximize the distance to its first occurrence. Time and space are Θ(n), option C; hashing is unnecessary. Reading the inputs already takes Ω(n) time.
