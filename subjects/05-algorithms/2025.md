## Solution

Every path in the spanning tree is also a path in the original graph, because T is a subgraph of G. Minimizing over all G paths cannot yield a larger distance than the sole T path, hence d1(u, v)≤d2(u, v), B. Equality can occur, e.g. G already a tree. Strict inequality can occur in a unit-weight triangle whose tree omits the direct edge between u and v. The premise that a spanning tree exists supplies connectedness.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 435 to 435

**Derived answer:** 435

## Solution

This is bubble sort. Every swap exchanges an adjacent inverted pair and decreases the inversion count by exactly one. A descending sequence of 30 distinct numbers has every pair inverted, so its inversion count is C(30, 2)=30×29/2=435. The final ascending sequence has zero inversions, hence exactly 435 swaps occur. Equivalently the passes perform 29+28+⋯+1 swaps.

## Solution

For N≥2, compare any two distinct list elements once and return the smaller. The larger of that pair certifies the returned element is not the maximum of the whole list. Zero comparisons cannot guarantee this for arbitrary distinct comparison keys. Thus the minimum is 1, option A. The question implicitly needs at least two elements; for N=1 no requested element exists.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q29

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 23](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS22025.pdf#page=23); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B;C;D

**Derived answer:** B;C;D

## Solution

A DFS need not give shortest paths: on a triangle rooted at s it may descend through u before v, yielding a two-edge tree path to v despite edge(s, v). For undirected DFS, each non-tree edge joins an ancestor and descendant, hence is a back edge when oriented toward the ancestor (or a forward/back connection in the option’s wording); there are no cross edges. For any BFS edge(u, v), shortest distances obey d(s, u)≤d(s, v)+1 and the reverse, giving absolute difference at most1. Repeated BFS or DFS from unvisited vertices enumerates connected components. Thus B, C, D.

## Solution

Every spanning tree on n vertices uses n−1 edges, so adding α to every edge adds the same(n−1)α to every tree’s total. Their ordering, hence every MST, is preserved. Paths need not use equal numbers of edges: take u–w and w–v each of weight1 and u–v of weight3. Initially the two-edge path costs2 and is shorter; adding α=2 gives costs6 versus5, so the direct edge becomes shorter. Thus C. Spanning trees are understood where they exist.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q41

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 31](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS22025.pdf#page=31); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

## Solution

Distinctness makes the two sorted portions strictly monotone. Even though the partition is written as1…i and i+1…n, their boundary makes a single peak at i or i+1. Locate that peak by comparing adjacent middle elements and discarding half the candidate interval each time. Then binary-search the increasing and decreasing portions. This takes O(log n) comparisons. Sorted arrays are special cases, and comparison-based membership among n distinct values needs Ω(log n) in the worst case. Therefore Θ(log n), D.

Joining such leaves uses3+3=6 edges. No branch pair is longer. Output6.
