## Solution

For any edge joining reachable vertices u, v, a shortest path to v followed by that edge proves d(u)≤d(v)+1, and symmetrically d(v)≤d(u)+1. Hence their distances differ by at most1, so 2 is impossible, D. The restriction to a non-tree edge does not change the bound; same-level and adjacent-level non-tree edges can occur. Distances are understood within the source component, since subtraction of two infinite unreachable distances is not defined.

## Solution

For a tree to remain the MST with distinct weights, every non-tree edge must be heavier than the maximum edge on its tree path. AB must exceed max(AC9, CB2)=9, so at least10. CD must exceed max(CB2, BE15, EF4, FD6)=15, so at least16. DE must exceed max(DF6, FE4)=6, so at least7. Values10, 16, 7 are mutually distinct and different from the given tree weights, and satisfy all cycle conditions. Minimum total is 36+10+16+7=69.

**Derived answer:** D

## Solution

The outer loop runs n−1 times. Repeated integer halving of j from n until≤1 gives p=floor(log₂n). Doubling k from 1 while k<p increments q ceil(log₂p) times when p≥1. Thus for sufficiently large n the return value is(n−1)*ceil(log₂ floor(log₂n))=Θ(n log log n), D. This is the mathematical count under no-overflow arithmetic; q can overflow a fixed-width int for sufficiently large inputs.

# GATE 2015 CS3: Q64 (printed CS Q64)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 78](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2015.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

The ratio g(n)/f(n)=n^(sin n). Since1/(2π) is irrational, integer n modulo2π is dense, and every tail is dense. There are therefore arbitrarily large n with sin n≥1/2 and arbitrarily large n with sin n≤−1/2. Along the former subsequence g/f≥sqrt(n) is unbounded, ruling out f=Ω(g); along the latter f/g≥sqrt(n) is unbounded, ruling out f=O(g). Thus neither statement holds, D, with sine in radians.
