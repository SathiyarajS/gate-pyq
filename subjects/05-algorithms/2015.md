This is the mathematical count under no-overflow arithmetic; q can overflow a fixed-width int for sufficiently large inputs.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Let f(n)=n and g(n)=n^(1+sin n), for positive integers n, with sine measured in radians. Which asymptotic statements hold? I: f(n)=O(g(n)); II: f(n)=Ω(g(n)). Options: (A) I only; (B) II only; (C) both; (D) neither.

## Solution

1. The ratio g(n)/f(n)=n^(sin n).

2. Since1/(2π) is irrational, integer n modulo2π is dense, and every tail is dense. There are therefore arbitrarily large n with sin n≥1/2 and arbitrarily large n with sin n≤−1/2. Along the former subsequence g/f≥sqrt(n) is unbounded, ruling out f=Ω(g); along the latter f/g≥sqrt(n) is unbounded, ruling out f=O(g). Thus neither statement holds, D, with sine in radians.
