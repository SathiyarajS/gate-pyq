**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** For positive functions f(n), identify the universally valid statement under its specified restriction: A f(n²)=Θ(f(n)²) if f is polynomial; B f(n²)=o(f(n)²); C f(n²)=O(f(n)²) if f is exponential; D f(n²)=Ω(f(n)²).

## Solution

For a positive polynomial of degree d with leading coefficient c>0, f(n²)~c n^(2d) and f(n)²~c² n^(2d), so they are Θ-equivalent, A. B fails with f(n)=n. C fails with f(n)=2^n, since 2^(n²)/2^(2n) diverges. D fails with f(n)=log n, since 2log n/(log n)² tends to zero.

Teaching detail:
The ratio f(n²)/f(n)² tends to 1/c for a degree-d polynomial, a positive finite constant, which is exactly enough for a Θ comparison. In B the ratio for n is 1 rather than tending to zero. In C the ratio is 2^(n²−2n), unbounded. In D use log(n+1) if positivity is required even at n=1; the ratio still tends to zero.

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 32](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2022/cs_2022.pdf#page=32); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A,B,C

**Derived answer:** A,B,C

**Paraphrased problem context:** A simple undirected weighted graph has distinct edge weights. Select true MST claims: A second-lightest edge is always included; B at least one of third/fourth lightest is included; C unique lightest edge across every nontrivial cut is included; D multiple MSTs can exist. Assume existence of an MST and the referenced edges.

## Solution

Assume an MST exists and the graph has the referenced four edges. Kruskal always accepts the second-lightest edge: with only one accepted edge a simple graph cannot form a cycle. If the third is rejected, the first three edges form a triangle. The fourth cannot be another simple edge inside that already complete triangle, so it is accepted. Thus at least one of the third and fourth edges belongs to the MST. The unique lightest edge crossing any nontrivial cut belongs to every MST by the cut property. Distinct weights imply the MST is unique, making D false. Hence A, B, C. Connectedness and existence of the named edges are implicit in the question’s wording.

Teaching detail:
The cut property follows by exchange: if an MST omits the unique cheapest crossing edge e, adding e creates a cycle containing another crossing edge f. Since w(f)>w(e), replacing f by e lowers the total, a contradiction. Distinct weights also prove uniqueness by considering the cheapest edge where two proposed MSTs differ.
