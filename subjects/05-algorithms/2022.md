

# GATE 2022 CS: Q11

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 11](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2022/cs_2022.pdf#page=11); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

## Solution

For a positive polynomial of degree d with leading coefficient c>0, f(n²)~c n^(2d) and f(n)²~c² n^(2d), so they are Θ-equivalent, A. B fails with f(n)=n. C fails with f(n)=2^n, since 2^(n²)/2^(2n) diverges. D fails with f(n)=log n, since 2log n/(log n)² tends to zero.

# GATE 2022 CS: Q49

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 32](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2022/cs_2022.pdf#page=32); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A,B,C

**Derived answer:** A,B,C

## Solution

Assume an MST exists and the graph has the referenced four edges. Kruskal always accepts the second-lightest edge: with only one accepted edge a simple graph cannot form a cycle. If the third is rejected, the first three edges form a triangle. The fourth cannot be another simple edge inside that already complete triangle, so it is accepted. Thus at least one of the third and fourth edges belongs to the MST. The unique lightest edge crossing any nontrivial cut belongs to every MST by the cut property. Distinct weights imply the MST is unique, making D false. Hence A, B, C. Connectedness and existence of the named edges are implicit in the question’s wording.
