**Paraphrased problem context:** Complete: “Didn't you buy ____ when you went shopping?” A any paper; B much paper; C no paper; D a few paper.

## Solution

Paper is used as a mass noun here, so it does not take “a few” without a countable unit such as sheets. “Any” is the usual neutral determiner in a negative question. “No” would create an unintended double negative. The official preference for A is idiomatic rather than proof that every other grammatical reading is impossible.

Baseline derivation and retained qualifications: Any paper is the idiomatic choice for asking about an unspecified quantity in a negative question. This yields A. Much paper is also grammatical if the intended question concerns a large quantity, so the isolated sentence does not uniquely exclude B without the intended context.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Passing probabilities for Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry are m,p,c. Probability of passing at least one is 0.75, at least two 0.50, exactly two 0.40. Claims: I p+m+c=27/20; II p+m+c=13/20; III pmc=1/10. A I only; B II only; C II and III; D I and III.

## Solution

Let K count passed subjects. Its probabilities for K=0,1,2,3 are respectively 0.25,0.25,0.40,0.10. Linearity of expectation requires no independence: E[K]=m+p+c=1.35. A product of marginal probabilities cannot generally replace an intersection. Indeed, nonnegative numbers summing to 1.35 have product at most (1.35/3)^3=0.091125, strictly below 0.10. The product claim is therefore impossible, not merely unproved. Preserve the published D for scoring while teaching A as the mathematical conclusion.

Baseline derivation and retained qualifications: Exactly three passes has probability0.5−0.4=0.1, and exactly one has probability0.75−0.5=0.25. The expected number of passes is m+p+c=0.25+2(0.4)+3(0.1)=1.35=27/20, so I is true and II false. III does not follow: mpc is not the intersection probability without independence. In fact AM–GM gives mpc≤(1.35/3)^3=0.091125<0.1, so III is false under these premises. The mathematically correct choice is A (I only), whereas the visible official answer is D (I and III).

Because the question asks what is guaranteed, I and II qualify; a special constant-sequence exception does not establish III generally.
