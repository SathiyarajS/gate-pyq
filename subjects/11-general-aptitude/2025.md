**Paraphrased problem context:** Fill the three blanks in this sentence: Ravi had ___ younger brother who taught at ___ university and was regarded as ___ honorable man. Options: A a; a; an. B the; an; a. C a; an; a. D an; an; a.

## Solution

Approach: Choose an indefinite article from the first sound of the following word, rather than its first written letter.
1. “Younger” starts with the consonant sound /j/, like the beginning of “yes”. Therefore the first phrase is “a younger brother”. Nothing in the sentence calls for a previously identified brother, so the indefinite article is appropriate.
2. “University” also begins with /j/ despite starting with the vowel letter u. Its article is therefore “a”, not “an”.
3. In “honorable”, h is silent and the first pronounced sound is a vowel. Thus the final phrase is “an honorable man”.
The required sequence is a; a; an, option A. Each of B, C and D uses “an university” and “a honorable”, which fail the sound-based rule.

## Solution

Approach: Use the contrast between immediate gain and future damage to infer the intended figurative meaning.

A, “visionary”, suggests unusually strong foresight and would reverse the criticism. C, “progressive”, describes a preference for change or reform, not inadequate planning. **Question:** [Official archived paper page 3](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS12025.pdf#page=3); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A class average was recorded as 30.8. One mark had been entered as 24 instead of 42. Correcting that single mark changes the mean to 31.4. Find the number of students. A 25; B 28; C 30; D 32.

## Solution

Approach: Compare the change in total marks with the change in the average; the number of observations stays fixed.
Let n be the class size and S the originally recorded total. Then S/n=30.8. Replacing 24 by 42 increases the total by 42−24=18, so (S+18)/n=31.4.
Subtract the first equation from the second: 18/n=31.4−30.8=0.6. Consequently n=18/0.6=30, option C.
As a check, the original total would be 30×30.8=924 and the corrected total 942. Dividing 942 by 30 indeed gives 31.4. The correction is positive, so an increased rather than decreased mean is consistent. The other proposed sizes would not make an 18-mark correction produce a 0.6-mark mean increase.

**Paraphrased problem context:** In triangle PQR, S lies on PQ and T on PR, with ST parallel to QR. The perpendicular separation of ST and QR is half the perpendicular distance from P to QR. Find area(PST)/area(SQRT), where SQRT is the remaining trapezium. A 1/3; B 1/4; C 2/5; D 1/2.

## Solution

Approach: Use similarity to obtain the small triangle’s fraction of the whole, then subtract to obtain the trapezium’s fraction.
Let P’s altitude above QR be h. Since ST is h/2 above QR, P’s altitude above ST is h−h/2=h/2. Parallelism gives equal corresponding angles, so triangles PST and PQR are similar with linear scale factor (h/2)/h=1/2.
Areas scale as the square of a linear factor. If the large triangle has area A, then area(PST)=A/4. The trapezium occupies the remainder, A−A/4=3A/4. Their ratio is (A/4)/(3A/4)=1/3, option A.
Option B, 1/4, is the small triangle’s ratio to the entire triangle, not the requested trapezium. Option D incorrectly treats area as scaling linearly with height. No right angle or symmetry of the sloping sides is required.

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** A fair six-sided die is rolled independently three times. Find the probability of exactly one result equal to 6. Options: A 75/216; B 1/6; C 1/18; D 25/216.

## Solution

Approach: Count the mutually exclusive possible positions of the sole six.
If the first roll is the only six, the pattern is six, non-six, non-six. Its probability is (1/6)(5/6)(5/6)=25/216. The same probability applies when the unique six is second or third.
These three events cannot occur together, so their probabilities add: 3×25/216=75/216, option A. Equivalently, among the 6³=216 equally likely ordered outcomes, choose the position of the six in 3 ways and the other two faces in 5 ways each, giving 3×5²=75 favorable outcomes.
D counts just one fixed placement. B is the probability of a six on one specified roll, not exactly one across three rolls. Independence is what permits multiplication within each three-roll pattern.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Real constants P and Q satisfy P e^x = Q e^(−x) for every real x. Which assertion must hold?
Options: A. P=Q=0; B. P=Q=1; C. P=1 and Q=−1; D. P/Q=0.

## Solution

The important condition is “for every real x,” not just one value of x. Substitute x=0. Since e^0=1, the identity becomes P=Q. Next substitute x=1 and use Q=P:
P e = P e^(−1), so P(e−e^(−1))=0.
Because e and e^(−1) are unequal, their difference is nonzero. Hence P=0, and the first equation gives Q=0. Conversely, P=Q=0 makes both sides zero for every x, so it satisfies the original condition.

B works at x=0 but fails at x=1. C already fails at x=0. D is not an alternative way to state the answer: at P=Q=0 the quotient P/Q is undefined. Dividing the original equation by P or Q would therefore wrongly discard the actual solution. Answer: A.

Every prime is an integer at least 2. The product p1p2 has p1 as a divisor, with 1<p1<p1p2 because p2≥2. It therefore has a nontrivial divisor and is composite. This argument also works when p1=p2; for example, 2·2=4. Thus B is universally true.

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** A letter code maps IMAGE to FHBNJ and FIELD to EMFJG. Using the simple rule consistent with both examples, choose the code for BEACH.
Options: A. CEADP; B. IDBFC; C. JGIBC; D. IBCEC.

## Solution

Reverse the order of the input letters, then replace each letter with its next alphabet letter. Verify both parts of the rule on both examples:
IMAGE → EGAMI → FHBNJ, because E→F, G→H, A→B, M→N, I→J.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q8

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 8](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS22025.pdf#page=8); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** For iterations i=0,1,2,3, the input I is [20,−4,10,15], output X is [20,16,26,41], and output Y is [20,−80,−800,−12000]. Choose recurrences for i>0.
A. X(i)=X(i−1)+I(i); Y(i)=Y(i−1)I(i).
B. X(i)=X(i−1)I(i); Y(i)=Y(i−1)+I(i).
C. X(i)=X(i−1)I(i); Y(i)=Y(i−1)I(i).
D. X(i)=X(i−1)+I(i); Y(i)=Y(i−1)I(i−1).

## Solution

For X, take consecutive differences: 16−20=−4, 26−16=10, and 41−26=15. Each difference equals the current input, so X(i)=X(i−1)+I(i). Equivalently, X is the running sum including I(0).

For Y, check the products: 20·(−4)=−80, (−80)·10=−800, and (−800)·15=−12000. Thus Y(i)=Y(i−1)I(i), a running product. These two recurrences together are A.

B and C predict X(1)=20·(−4)=−80 rather than 16, so they fail immediately. D has the correct X recurrence but uses the previous input for Y: it would predict Y(1)=20·20=400. The initialization at i=0 is supplied separately; the recurrences begin at i=1. Answer: A.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q9

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Paraphrased problem context:** PQRS is a square of side 2 cm, with P at upper left, Q lower left, R lower right, and S upper right. PLMN is a rectangle whose vertices are in that order; L lies on QR, and side MN passes through S. Find its area in cm².
Options: A. 2√2; B. 2; C. 8; D. 4. The drawing is illustrative, not to scale.

## Solution

Use triangle PLS to avoid calculating the rectangle's tilt. Relative to base PS, this triangle has base 2 cm and altitude 2 cm, because L lies on QR, a line parallel to PS and 2 cm away. Consequently area(PLS)=(1/2)·2·2=2 cm² regardless of L's position.

Now regard PL as the triangle's base. Since S is on the rectangle's opposite side MN, its perpendicular distance from PL equals the rectangle's other side length. Hence triangle PLS has exactly half the area of rectangle PLMN. The rectangle therefore has area 2·2=4 cm².

A coordinate check gives the same invariant: set P=(0,2), L=(t,0), S=(2,2). Twice the triangle area is |det((t,−2),(2,0))|=4, which equals the rectangle area. The side lengths can change as L moves, but their product does not. Answer: D. The stated incidence and rectangle relations, rather than measured angles in the figure, determine the result.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q10

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 10](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS22025.pdf#page=10); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** A river divides land into five zones Z1,…,Z5. A bridge over each labelled segment connects the following pair of zones: P: Z1–Z2; Q: Z1–Z3; R: Z2–Z3; S: Z1–Z5; T: Z3–Z5; U: Z5–Z4; V: Z3–Z4. Choose a listed set of bridges that connects every zone with the fewest bridges.
Options: A. P,Q,T; B. P,Q,S,T; C. Q,R,T,V; D. P,Q,S,U,V.

## Solution

Replace each land zone by a vertex and each possible bridge by an undirected edge. Connecting all five vertices requires at least 5−1=4 edges: starting from five isolated components, one edge can reduce the component count by at most one. A connected set with exactly four edges is a spanning tree and attains this lower bound.

C uses Q,R,T,V, all incident to Z3. These connect Z3 respectively to Z1,Z2,Z5,Z4, so every zone is reachable and four bridges suffice. A has only three bridges and cannot connect five vertices; in particular, it never reaches Z4. B also omits both U and V, the only links to Z4, so its four bridges do not provide connectivity. D does connect the zones, but uses five bridges and is not minimal. Thus C is the required optimal choice. The full edge-to-zone correspondence above was read from the source river map.
