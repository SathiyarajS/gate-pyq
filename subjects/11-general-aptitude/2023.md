**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** Complete “We reached the station late, and ___ missed the train” using A near, B nearly, C utterly, or D mostly.

## Solution

Nearly is the adverb meaning almost and correctly modifies missed. Near is not the appropriate adverb in this construction; utterly and mostly do not express narrowly avoiding the event. Thus B.

Teaching detail. The blank modifies the verb “missed”, so the choice must fit both grammar and meaning. “Nearly missed” means they came close to missing the train but did not actually miss it. Being late at the station does not force the conclusion that departure had already occurred. “Near” ordinarily describes location or proximity, “utterly” means completely, and “mostly” means for the greater part; none expresses this near-event relation here.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Complete the meaning analogy Kind : ___ :: Often : Frequently. Choices: A Mean; B Type; C Cruel; D Kindly. Distinguish a semantic answer from the final official marking outcome.

## Solution

Often and frequently are synonyms. Kind in the sense of category is synonymous with type, making B a defensible semantic answer. However the official key explicitly awards MTA, meaning Marks to All. Preserve that administrative outcome; do not replace it with B or invent a reason for the award.

This analysis does not establish why the organizers awarded marks to all. An official marking decision and a plausible language solution are different facts, and both must remain visible.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** Natural numbers satisfy F(n+1)=F(n)+F(n−1) for n≥2. Given F6=37 and F7=60, find F1. Choices A 4, B 5, C 8, D 9.

## Solution

Work backward using F_(n−1)=F_(n+1)−F_n: F5=60−37=23, F4=37−23=14, F3=23−14=9, F2=14−9=5, F1=9−5=4. Answer A.

Teaching detail. The recurrence is reversible because knowing two consecutive terms determines the preceding term by subtraction. Label each step carefully rather than treating F6 as the sixth Fibonacci number of the usual sequence beginning 1,1. A forward check from 4,5 gives 9,14,23,37,60, reproducing both supplied values. Thus no approximation or closed-form formula is necessary, and the positivity/natural-number condition is respected throughout.

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** A survey says 90% of pregnant women received care at least once before delivery; among those, 60% received care from doctors and 40% from other providers. Which follows certainly: A more than half received doctor care at least once; B less than half did so; C more than half received doctor care at most once; D less than half did so?

## Solution

Of all pregnant women, the fraction receiving care from doctors is 0.90×0.60=0.54. Thus more than half received doctor care at least once, proving A. The information does not limit the number of visits, so at-most-once claims do not follow.

Teaching detail. Use 100 hypothetical women to make the conditional percentage concrete: 90 received some care, and 60% of those 90, namely 54, received doctor care. The remaining cared-for group has 36 women, and 10 received no care. The count 54 exceeds 50. “At least once” is not “exactly once”: the same 54 could each have visited one doctor once or many times, so the at-most-once alternatives are not fixed by the survey.

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** Viewed externally, what is possible for a smooth three-dimensional object's surface? A necessarily concave everywhere; B necessarily convex everywhere; C concave in some regions and convex in others; D sharp edges but no corners.

## Solution

A smooth surface can include outward bulges and inward indentations, so it may be convex in some regions and concave in others. Smoothness rules out sharp edges and corners rather than requiring everywhere convex or everywhere concave shape. Answer C.

Teaching detail. Smoothness is a local regularity condition, not a global convexity condition. A rounded object with a smoothly formed dent supplies the required mental model: a bulging exterior region curves outward and the dent curves inward, without a sharp join. A sphere shows that wholly convex surfaces are possible, but it does not show they are compulsory. The question asks what may happen, so a valid smooth counterexample defeats any asserted universal shape constraint.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Paraphrased problem context:** In Zombieland, over 75% of workers have serious health issues, and experts identify lack of exercise as a leading cause. The government responds with incentives to cycle to work. Which inference follows: A all workers will cycle; B cycling removes all health issues; C experts recommended mandatory cycling; D the government regards cycling as exercise?

## Solution

The intervention is presented as a response to lack of exercise, so the intended inference is that the government treats cycling as physical exercise, option D. Nothing states that everyone will adopt it, that it cures every cause of illness, or that experts recommended compulsion. This is the ordinary policy-purpose reading of the passage rather than a formal proof about hidden beliefs independent of that context.

Teaching detail. Read the strength of each proposed conclusion. A financial incentive encourages behavior but does not logically guarantee universal adoption. The passage identifies exercise deficiency as one leading cause, leaving other causes untreated; it therefore cannot promise complete health. An incentive scheme is also not a mandatory rule, nor does the passage report that experts recommended one. D is the modest inference connecting the stated problem to the stated intervention, which is why the official answer selects it.

**Paraphrased problem context:** For t>0, f(t)=0.01t² and g(t)=4t. Decide whether (i) g(t)>f(t) for some t>0, and (ii) there is T with f(t)>g(t) for every t>T. A only i; B only ii; C both; D neither.

## Solution

For t>0, 4t>0.01t² exactly when t<400; for instance t=1 proves statement(i). Conversely, 0.01t²>4t whenever t>400, so T=400 proves(ii). Both hold, option C.

Teaching detail. Factor the difference f(t)−g(t)=0.01t(t−400). Since t is positive, its sign is the sign of t−400: negative on (0,400), zero at 400, positive after 400. Statement (i) is existential and needs only one witness, while statement (ii) is eventually universal and needs a threshold after which every value works. These statements are compatible: a quadratic can start below a linear function and ultimately dominate it.

**Official answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** For every independent pair of real values x,y, f(x)=g(y). Must A both functions be zero, B both be the same constant, C both be nonconstant, or D f(x)+g(y)=f(x)−g(y)?

## Solution

Fix any y0. The premise gives f(x)=g(y0) for every x, so f is constant. Similarly fixing x0 makes g constant, and their constants are equal. They need not be zero. Hence B.

Teaching detail. The key phrase is “for all x and y”, not merely f(x)=g(x) for a common argument. Taking y=0 forces every value of f to equal the single number g(0). Taking x=0 similarly forces every value of g to equal f(0), and those two numbers agree. The common value can be 7, for example, refuting the necessity of zero and of the equality of sum and difference. No continuity or differentiability assumption is needed.

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** An asymmetric star P has its longest point toward the upper right; Q is P with that point toward the lower right and all features correspondingly rotated; R has Q's top and bottom features interchanged while retaining left/right positions. Identify the two transformations. A clockwise 90° then horizontal-axis reflection; B counterclockwise 90° then horizontal reflection; C clockwise 90° then vertical reflection; D counterclockwise 180° then vertical reflection.

## Solution

Track the unusually long point: in P it points upper-right, after operation1 in Q it points lower-right, a clockwise 90-degree rotation. Reflecting Q across a horizontal line moves that long point to upper-right in R while preserving its left/right orientation. The other notches and points also agree. Answer A; all three figures were visually inspected.

Teaching detail. Use an asymmetric landmark rather than the overall star shape, since symmetric-looking outlines can make rotation and reflection easy to confuse. In Cartesian coordinates centered on the shape, clockwise 90° maps (x,y) to (y,−x); reflection in a horizontal axis maps (x,y) to (x,−y). The first operation changes orientation without reversing handedness, whereas the second reverses handedness. Checking the shorter neighboring points as well as the longest point rules out an accidental single-landmark match.
