**Paraphrased problem context:** The arrow means increasing intensity. Complete the analogy dry → arid → parched, diet → fast → ___. Options: A starve; B reject; C feast; D deny.

## Solution

Approach: Match both the subject of the words and the direction of the progression.
1. “Dry”, “arid” and “parched” describe increasingly severe lack of moisture. The last word should therefore intensify the idea introduced by the first two words in the second sequence.
2. In the intended food-related sense, to diet is to restrict eating, to fast is to abstain from eating, and to starve is to suffer extreme deprivation of food. “Starve” completes that progression.
3. “Reject” and “deny” concern refusal but do not specifically describe a stronger degree of food deprivation. “Feast” means eating abundantly and reverses the direction.
Answer: A, starve. The analogy uses the intended semantic scale, rather than claiming that every voluntary diet necessarily develops into fasting or starvation.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Distinct nonzero real variables x and y satisfy that x+y is proportional to x−y. What can be said about x/y? A It depends on xy. B It depends only on x. C It depends only on y. D It is constant.

## Solution

Approach: Replace “proportional” with an equation involving one fixed proportionality constant.
1. Write x+y=k(x−y), where k does not change with the variables. The distinction x≠y ensures that the quantity to which x+y is proportional is nonzero.
2. Expand and collect terms: x+y=kx−ky, so (k−1)x=(k+1)y.
3. Because y≠0, division by y is allowed. Also k cannot equal 1: substituting k=1 into the original equation gives 2y=0, contrary to the premise.
4. Consequently x/y=(k+1)/(k−1). Its value depends only on the fixed k, not on the particular allowed pair x,y.
Answer: D. A useful distinction is that x and y may vary while their ratio stays fixed.

Approach: A median uses positions in the sorted data, not the arithmetic average of every observation.

Answer: A. The unusually large observation 69 affects the mean much more than the median. Option D, 18.7, is the mean because the sample sum is 187; it is not the requested statistic.

Answer: A, 6. This gives both a lower bound and a construction attaining it; simply counting visually nearby squares can miss the gap beneath the existing upper square.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Positive real x,y satisfy ln((x+y)/2)=(ln x+ln y)/2. Find x/y+y/x. A 1; B 1/2; C 2; D 4.

## Solution

Approach: Combine the logarithms and use the injectivity of the exponential function.

1. Derivation. Exponentiating yields(x+y)/2=√(xy), equality in the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. Equivalently(x−y)²=0, so x=y>0. Hence x/y+y/x=1+1=2, option C.

Positivity licenses the logarithms, square root and later divisions. The equality condition matters: without the given equation, x/y+y/x is merely at least 2, not necessarily equal to 2. Another useful check is x/y+y/x−2=(x−y)²/(xy). The numerator vanishes for the forced equality x=y, so the excess above two is exactly zero.

## Solution

Approach: Prove both a construction and a lower bound.

1. Derivation. Three mutually perpendicular central plane cuts divide the cube into eight congruent smaller cubes. Two plane cuts produce at most four pieces, so fewer than three cannot suffice. The minimum is 3, option A.

2. Explanation and checks. For a cube of side a, cut at the midpoint along each of the three coordinate directions. The result has two intervals in every direction, hence 2×2×2 cubes, each of side a/2. For the lower bound, one plane creates at most two pieces; a second plane can split each existing piece at most once, producing at most four. Thus two cuts cannot reach eight, and the three-cut construction is optimal.
