**Paraphrased problem context:** Which option is closest in meaning to 'Inexplicable'?

(A) Incomprehensible
(B) Indelible
(C) Inextricable
(D) Infallible

## Solution

Inexplicable describes something that cannot be explained or understood. Incomprehensible is the closest supplied synonym, A; the other words concern permanence, entanglement or inability to err.

Break down the alternatives by meaning. 'Indelible' means impossible to remove or erase, 'inextricable' means impossible to disentangle or escape, and 'infallible' means incapable of error. 'Incomprehensible' means impossible to understand, so it is nearest to something that cannot be explained. The question asks for the closest supplied synonym, not an assertion that the words are interchangeable in every sentence; that is why a shared negative prefix alone is not enough.

**Paraphrased problem context:** If log(P)=(1/2)log(Q)=(1/3)log(R), which relation is guaranteed? Use positive arguments and the same valid logarithm base.

(A) P²=Q³R²
(B) Q²=PR
(C) Q²=R³P
(D) R=P²Q²

## Solution

Put log P=t. Then log Q=2t and log R=3t. Thus log(Q²)=4t=log(PR), so Q²=PR, B, for positive arguments and a common valid logarithm base. This is the relation guaranteed for every allowed t; special values such as P=Q=R=1 may also satisfy other displayed equations.

Equivalently, the equalities imply Q=P² and R=P³. Substituting into each candidate gives A: P²=P^12, B: P^4=P^4, C: P^4=P^10, and D: P³=P^6. Only B is an identity for arbitrary positive P. Taking logarithms of products requires the common valid base and positive quantities. Accidental equalities at P=1 do not make the other options consequences for all values satisfying the premise.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Complete: 'I contemplated _____ Singapore for my vacation but decided against it.'

(A) to visit
(B) having to visit
(C) visiting
(D) for a visit

## Solution

Contemplate normally takes a noun or gerund complement. I contemplated visiting Singapore is the natural construction, C; having to visit changes the intended meaning to an obligation.

The missing phrase is the direct complement of 'contemplated', meaning considered doing something. The gerund 'visiting' serves as that complement and retains the idea of a possible vacation. 'To visit' is not the standard complement here; 'for a visit' lacks the normal construction connecting the destination. 'Having to visit' can be grammatical but introduces necessity or obligation, which is not the intended meaning of choosing a vacation and deciding against it.

## Solution

Restrained is closest in sense to understated and tentative, continuing the sentence’s contrast with making a strong impression. Hence B is the most appropriate choice in context.

The list gives subdued styles that work against a strong impression. 'Restrained' means controlled or held back and therefore fits with 'understated' and 'tentative'. 'Indifferent' refers to lack of interest, not specifically restrained presentation. The parallel meaning of the neighboring adjectives makes B the closest contextual completion.

**Derived answer:** D under inverse-dose toxicity model

**Source access:** Archived official IIT Kharagpur paper and IIT Madras key, booklet A. Physical PDF page numbers are used; code A is the matching key column. Final-revision designation is unverified.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A plot compares dangerous microbes P,Q,R,S. Circle area represents 24-hour growth after surviving immunity. The x-axis is potency (probability of overcoming immunity); the y-axis is labeled toxicity and measures milligrams of microbe required to destroy half the body mass in kilograms. Data (potency,plotted dose,circle diameter in mm) are P=(0.4,800,50), Q=(0.5,600,40), R=(0.4,300,30), S=(0.8,200,20). Danger is said to vary proportionately with toxicity, potency and growth. Which microbe should be targeted first as most dangerous?

(A) P
(B) Q
(C) R
(D) S

## Solution

Growth is proportional to circle area and hence diameter squared. The vertical axis is the dose needed to cause the stated harm, so greater toxicity corresponds to a smaller dose. Using danger proportional to potency×diameter²/dose gives P=.4×2500/800=1.25, Q=.5×1600/600=4/3, R=.4×900/300=1.2 and S=.8×400/200=1.6. S is largest, D. This requires treating the plotted dose as an inverse measure of toxicity, not multiplying by its numeric axis value. Direct multiplication would instead rank P first; the plot’s toxicity label and prose are imprecise about that distinction.

Use circle area πd²/4, not diameter d, for growth. The common π/4 factor cancels when comparing microbes, so it need not be calculated. Lower required harmful dose indicates greater toxicity, hence the inverse-dose interpretation produces scores 1.25,1.333...,1.2,1.6 and ranks S>Q>P>R. This is not simply choosing the largest bubble or the highest point. Because the source calls the vertical dose itself 'toxicity', the intended inverse interpretation must be disclosed rather than silently treated as a literal multiplication of all plotted coordinates.

**Paraphrased problem context:** For production quantity q, variable cost V=4q and the stated fixed cost F=100/q. How many units minimize V+F?

(A) 5
(B) 4
(C) 7
(D) 6

## Solution

For q>0, total cost=4q+100/q≥2√400=40 by AM–GM. Equality holds when4q=100/q, or q²=25. Thus q=5 minimizes the cost, A, and is also an allowed integer quantity.

A calculus check gives C'(q)=4-100/q², which is zero at the positive q=5. Also C''(q)=200/q³>0 for q>0, so the stationary point is a strict minimum. The objective tends to infinity as q approaches zero from above or grows without bound, confirming a global minimum. Directly, C(4)=41, C(5)=40, C(6)=40.666..., C(7)≈42.286, consistent with the chosen integer option. Use the supplied inverse-q formula despite the unusual 'fixed cost' terminology.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A transporter receives the same amount of new orders daily and starts with a backlog. Seven trucks clear everything by the end of day 4; three trucks clear everything by the end of day 10. What is the minimum whole number of trucks needed to clear everything by the end of day 5?

(A) 4
(B) 5
(C) 6
(D) 7

## Solution

Measure work in truck-days. Let backlog b and new daily demand d. Then b+4d=28 and b+10d=30, giving d=1/3 and b=80/3. Five days require b+5d=85/3 truck-days, or17/3 trucks per day. The smallest integer fleet is 6, C.

One truck-day is the amount one truck can ship in a day; assume identical constant-capacity trucks. The two scenarios use 28 and 30 truck-days, and their difference of 2 represents six additional days of incoming orders. For a five-day deadline the required daily fleet size is 17/3≈5.667. Five trucks provide only 25 truck-days, below 85/3; six provide 30, enough. Rounding down would leave a backlog, so the ceiling is mandatory.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A container initially holds 10 litres of pure spirit. Remove 1 litre and replace it with water. Then remove 1 litre of the well-mixed contents and replace it with water, and repeat that operation once more. How many litres of spirit remain?

(A) 7.58
(B) 7.84
(C) 7
(D) 7.29

## Solution

Each well-mixed replacement removes one tenth of the spirit currently present. There are three replacements in total, so the remaining spirit is 10(9/10)^3=7.29 litres, D.

Track the spirit concentration after each replacement. First 9 litres remain out of 10. The next litre removed contains 0.9 litre spirit, leaving 8.1; after refilling, the third litre removed contains 0.81 litre spirit, leaving 7.29. Water restores total volume to 10 each time but does not restore spirit. Subtracting three litres of spirit would incorrectly assume all three withdrawals were pure spirit; the later withdrawals are diluted mixtures.
