# GATE 2011 CS: Q7 (printed GA Q62)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 17](https://web.archive.org/web/20220204093545id_/https://gate.iitkgp.ac.in/documents/gatepapers/2011/cs_2011.pdf#page=17); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D under inverse-dose toxicity model

**Source access:** Archived official IIT Kharagpur paper and IIT Madras key, booklet A. Physical PDF page numbers are used; code A is the matching key column. Final-revision designation is unverified.

## Solution

Growth is proportional to circle area and hence diameter squared. The vertical axis is the dose needed to cause the stated harm, so greater toxicity corresponds to a smaller dose. Using danger proportional to potency×diameter²/dose gives P=.4×2500/800=1.25, Q=.5×1600/600=4/3, R=.4×900/300=1.2 and S=.8×400/200=1.6. S is largest, D. This requires treating the plotted dose as an inverse measure of toxicity, not multiplying by its numeric axis value. Direct multiplication would instead rank P first; the plot’s toxicity label and prose are imprecise about that distinction.
