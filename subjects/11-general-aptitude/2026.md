# GATE 2026 CS1: Q5

**Question:** [Official master paper page 4](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2026/QPs/CS1.pdf#page=4); consult original wording and options.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

## Solution

Let T mean the teacher is present, S that every student is standing, and Q that every student is silent. The premise is T implies S and Q. It entails each individual consequence and also its contrapositive: a student failing to stand silently disproves T. It does not entail the converse S implies T; students might stand while the teacher is absent. Therefore C is the statement not necessarily true.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

## Solution

The passage describes a use of combinatorial counting in some physical models and then explains assigning probabilities to compute averages. This supports statement Q. It does not justify promoting that limited use into the universal assertion P that all physical modeling invokes combinatorics. The intended comprehension classification is therefore P false and Q true, option B. In strict logic, lack of support for an always claim is not by itself a proof of its negation; the answer uses the passage-based reading of P rather than an independent theorem about all physical models.

# GATE 2026 CS1: Q10

**Question:** [Official master paper page 9](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2026/QPs/CS1.pdf#page=9); consult original wording and options.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

## Solution

There are 36 equally likely ordered pairs. Conditioning on the first result 1 through 6, the numbers of possible multiples on the second roll are respectively 6,3,2,1,1,1. The count of favorable pairs is 14. Hence the probability is 14/36=7/18, option C. Equal results count because a number is one times itself; reversing the order of the two rolls does not preserve the condition.

# GATE 2026 CS1: Q2

**Question:** [Official paper page 2](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2026/QPs/CS1.pdf#page=2); consult original wording and options.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

## Solution

Every permitted piece covers exactly four unit squares, so any nonoverlapping assembly must have area divisible by four. The rectangle in C has 3*5=15 cells and is impossible. The other options can actually be tiled: the 2-by-4 rectangle by two horizontal bars, the 3-by-4 rectangle by three horizontal bars, and the 4-by-5 rectangle by five vertical bars. Thus the area obstruction singles out C, rather than merely giving a necessary condition. The cell counts and piece shapes were visually checked in the official diagram.

# GATE 2026 CS1: Q8

**Question:** [Official paper page 7](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2026/QPs/CS1.pdf#page=7); consult original wording and options.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

## Solution

Reading the plot gives three intervals: zero up to S=10, then S-10 between 10 and 20, then the constant 10 thereafter. Subtract the hinge turning on at 20 from the hinge turning on at 10. For S<=10 both are zero. For 10<S<=20 only the first is active, giving S-10. Above 20 the difference is (S-10)-(S-20)=10. Thus H_10(S)-H_20(S), option A, matches the whole graph including both junctions. The graph’s slope and plateau were inspected directly.
