Let T mean the teacher is present, S that every student is standing, and Q that every student is silent. The premise is T implies S and Q. It entails each individual consequence and also its contrapositive: a student failing to stand silently disproves T. ## Solution

There are 36 equally likely ordered pairs. Conditioning on the first result 1 through 6, the numbers of possible multiples on the second roll are respectively 6,3,2,1,1,1. The count of favorable pairs is 14. Hence the probability is 14/36=7/18, option C. Equal results count because a number is one times itself; reversing the order of the two rolls does not preserve the condition.

# GATE 2026 CS2: Q8

**Question:** [Official master paper page 7](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2026/QPs/CS2.pdf#page=7); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

## Solution

A desired tour is a Hamiltonian cycle. For the 4-by-4 grid, label vertices by row and column. One cycle is (1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,4),(2,3),(2,2),(3,2),(3,3),(3,4),(4,4),(4,3),(4,2),(4,1),(3,1),(2,1), then back to (1,1). Every step is a drawn edge and every vertex is visited once. The second graph is K_(2,3): three degree-two vertices each connect the same two hubs. A cycle containing all three would force each hub to have three incident cycle edges, impossible because cycle degree is two. Equivalently, a bipartite graph has no odd cycle and this graph has five vertices. Hence only (i) permits the tour, option A.
