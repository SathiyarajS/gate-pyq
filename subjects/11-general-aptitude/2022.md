**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Paraphrased problem context:** Choose the words that complete “The ___ is too high for it to be considered ___.” A fair/fare; B faer/fair; C fare/fare; D fare/fair.

## Solution

Fare is the price charged; fair means reasonable or equitable. A fare may be too high to be considered fair, so D.

Teaching detail:
First identify the two grammatical roles: the first blank names a charge, while the second describes whether that charge is reasonable. “Faer” is not the required English word. Interchanging the homophones changes the meanings rather than improving the sentence.

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 2](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2022/cs_2022.pdf#page=2); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** Find the area under y(x) on [0,1], where y=2 on [0,1/3), y=3 on [1/3,3/4), and y=1 on [3/4,1]. A 5/6; B 6/5; C 13/6; D 6/13.

## Solution

Integrate each constant piece: area=2(1/3)+3(3/4−1/3)+1(1−3/4)=2/3+5/4+1/4=13/6. The values at the breakpoints do not affect area. Thus C.

Teaching detail:
The three rectangle widths are 1/3, 3/4−1/3=5/12, and 1/4. Multiply widths by their respective heights before adding; averaging 2,3,1 without weighting would be wrong because the intervals differ in length.

**Derived answer:** D

**Paraphrased problem context:** If r is either root of x²+2x+6=0, evaluate (r+2)(r+3)(r+4)(r+5). A 51; B −51; C 126; D −126.

## Solution

The root satisfies r²=−2r−6. Pair the factors as (r+2)(r+4)=4r+2 and (r+3)(r+5)=6r+9. Their product is 24r²+48r+18=−126. The calculation holds for either complex root, so D.

Teaching detail:
Reduction modulo the root equation avoids finding complex roots. Expanding the chosen pairs gives r²+6r+8=4r+2 and r²+8r+15=6r+9. On multiplying, the remaining r terms cancel after substituting r²=−2r−6.

**Paraphrased problem context:** There is at least one student. Statements are 1: all students are inquisitive; 2: some are inquisitive; 3: none is inquisitive; 4: some are not inquisitive. Which offered pair cannot both be true? A (1,3); B (1,2); C (2,4); D (3,4).

## Solution

Statements 1 and 3 assert that all students are inquisitive and no student is inquisitive. With at least one student, they cannot both hold. All and some can coexist, as can no and some-not. Therefore A.

Teaching detail:
To refute simultaneous truth of 1 and 3, pick an existing student: statement 1 assigns inquisitiveness and statement 3 denies it. The existence condition matters because universal statements about an empty class would both hold. Pair (2,4) is possible with one inquisitive and one noninquisitive student.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** Starting with plates A and D, select three more plates that permit a five-letter palindrome using every plate exactly once. In-plane rotation is allowed. Normalizing the displayed rotations, the choices contain A: D,D,J; B: R,A,R; C: Z,E,D; D: I,L,Y. In the source, the second D of A, A and last R of B, E of C, and L of D are rotated 180 degrees.

## Solution

Rotate the upside-down A and R in B by 180 degrees. The five plates then contain A, A, R, R, D, permitting RADAR, which reads identically in both directions. Rotations suffice; no reflection or replacement of a plate is needed. Thus B. The letter shapes and orientations were visually verified.

Teaching detail:
In any odd-length palindrome, all letter counts except the center letter must be even. B supplies two A plates and two R plates overall, leaving D as center. The other choices have more than one odd-count letter after accounting for rotations, so cannot fill both symmetric pairs.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Boys comprise 65% of entrance-test candidates and girls the other 35%. Among qualified candidates, 60% are girls. Choose the valid inference: A equal boys and girls qualified; B equal numbers appeared; C fewer boys appeared; D fewer boys qualified.

## Solution

Girls are 60% of the qualified candidates, so boys are 40% of that group. Therefore fewer boys than girls qualified, D. The 65% male share among those appearing does not change this direct comparison or determine absolute counts.

Teaching detail:
Let the total qualifiers be q. Then girls=0.60q and boys=0.40q, so girls exceed boys by 0.20q. The appearance percentages apply to a different denominator and must not be substituted for qualifier percentages.

# GATE 2022 CS: Q8

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 8](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2022/cs_2022.pdf#page=8); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Paraphrased problem context:** A box initially holds 3 green and 2 orange balls. A drawn green is removed; a drawn orange is replaced by another orange. After one draw, what is the unconditional probability that the second draw is orange? A 1/2; B 8/25; C 19/50; D 23/50.

## Solution

Condition on the first draw. If green is drawn (3/5), it is removed and the next orange probability is 2/4. If orange is drawn (2/5), it is replaced and the next orange probability remains 2/5. Thus P(second orange)=3/5·2/4+2/5·2/5=23/50, D.

Teaching detail:
The first-draw cases are disjoint and exhaustive, so the law of total probability applies. The second-draw probability after green is higher because only the denominator falls, whereas after orange the complete original composition is restored. Thus the answer must lie between 0.4 and 0.5; 0.46 passes this check.

# GATE 2022 CS: Q9

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 9](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2022/cs_2022.pdf#page=9); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** Assign P,Q,R,S,T,U bijectively to a triangle's three corners and three side midpoints. PR is parallel to QS; T is a corner and P is on its opposite side; S and U share no side. Which must hold? A P is not a corner; B S is not a corner; C U is not a midpoint; D R is not a corner.

## Solution

S cannot be a corner. If S is corner A, U must be the midpoint of opposite side BC: every other marked point shares a side with A. By reflection take T=B, so P is C or the midpoint of AC. Testing the remaining placements of Q and R shows that PR cannot be parallel to AQ. Hence S must be a midpoint. For an explicit valid assignment use corner coordinates A=(0, 0), B=(2, 0), C=(0, 2), with midpoints M_AB, M_BC, M_AC: (P, Q, R, S, T, U)=(A, M_AB, C, M_BC, B, M_AC). Here PR and QS are vertical, P and R are corners, and U is a midpoint, refuting the other three offered restrictions. Thus B. An exhaustive check of all 720 assignments independently gave 30 valid assignments, all with S a midpoint.

Teaching detail:
To test a proposed arrangement, compare direction vectors rather than slopes, since vertical lines are valid. Two vectors are parallel exactly when their determinant is zero. The exhibited assignment satisfies all three constraints while showing that each of A,C,D can fail; the corner-S case eliminates the only alternative to B.

# GATE 2022 CS: Q10

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 10](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2022/cs_2022.pdf#page=10); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D under the pole-to-pole full-partition model

**Paraphrased problem context:** Partition a plot into four similar shapes, not necessarily equal areas, retaining two fixed ropes. Reconstruct the diagram with unit integer-grid poles, x right/y down: boundary (0,0),(5,0),(6,1),(6,6),(1,6),(0,5), and all integer lattice points inside/on it. Fixed ropes are (0,4)–(2,4) and (4,0)–(4,2). A straight rope may pass through intermediate collinear poles. Minimum additional straight ropes? A 2; B 4; C 5; D 3.

## Solution

Use unit pole spacing, x right and y down. The boundary is (0, 0),(5, 0),(6, 1),(6, 6),(1, 6),(0, 5). Existing ropes are (0, 4)–(2, 4) and (4, 0)–(4, 2). Add (2, 4)–(2, 6), (4, 2)–(6, 2), and (2, 4)–(4, 2), the last through (3, 3). The four pieces are similar clipped squares: two have side 2 with corner-cut legs 1, and two have side 4 with corner-cut legs 2. This proves three ropes suffice and matches official D. For minimality under the pole-to-pole full-boundary-partition interpretation, an exhaustive exact-rational check enumerates all 47 poles and 1019 non-boundary straight segments. Of 9395 two-rope pairs that connect both existing interior rope ends, 528 yield four faces and none yield four mutually similar polygons; one rope never yields four faces. Similarity is checked using cyclic complex edge-ratio signatures, allowing reflection. The checker uses exact rational intersections, orientation ordering, and edge-ratio signatures. This proves the finite pole-to-pole model, not a general arbitrary-endpoint variant. The three-rope construction passes the same test.

Teaching detail:
The four polygons can be reconstructed from the boundary and the listed new segments. The two small pieces have area 2²−1²/2=3.5 each, and the two large pieces area 4²−2²/2=14 each. Their total 35 matches the original 6×6 square minus two half-unit corner triangles. Similarity follows from scaling lengths by two, with rotation/reflection as needed; areas therefore scale by four. The exact enumeration is a finite-model lower-bound certificate, not a replacement for stating the allowed endpoints.
