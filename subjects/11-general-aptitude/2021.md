**Paraphrased problem context:** The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 7:3. Which total number of students is possible? Options: A 21; B 37; C 50; D 73.

## Solution

The coprime ratio 7: 3 requires integer counts 7k and 3k, so the total must be a multiple of 10. Only 50 qualifies among the choices; it gives 35 boys and 15 girls. Thus C.

The integer restriction matters: writing boys=0.7N alone is not enough unless 0.7N and 0.3N are integers. Because gcd(7,3)=1, the multiplier k must be integral. Testing the alternatives modulo 10 immediately rejects 21,37,73; 50 supplies a concrete feasible class.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A polygon is convex when the segment between every pair of its points remains inside or on it. Which depicted polygon is NOT convex? Diagram descriptions: A a right-pointing chevron-shaped hexagon with an inward notch at the middle of its left boundary; B a triangle; C a square; D a trapezoid with a shorter parallel top side and a longer bottom side.

## Solution

The left-side indentation in A creates a reflex interior angle. A segment joining points above and below that indentation can leave the shaded polygon, violating convexity. The triangle, square, and trapezoid have no such indentation and are convex. Thus A. All four shapes were visually inspected.

Convexity is about every joining segment, not whether most angles look ordinary. In A, choose two points just to the right of the upper-left and lower-left corners: their vertical joining segment passes through the notch outside the polygon. A single such pair disproves convexity. For B–D every interior angle is at most 180°, and each simple polygon is convex.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A circular paper sheet is folded with its right half onto its left half, then with its lower half upward, leaving the upper-left quarter. The final folded quarter is punched: a quarter-circle at the original center, a horizontal rectangular notch into the vertical fold above the center, and a small vertical rectangular notch into the horizontal fold left of the center. Which pattern appears after unfolding? All choices show a central round hole and four rectangular holes. A long horizontal rectangles above/below and short vertical rectangles left/right; B long horizontal rectangles above/below and short horizontal rectangles left/right; C short horizontal rectangles above/below and long vertical rectangles left/right; D short vertical rectangles above/below and long vertical rectangles left/right.

## Solution

The first fold moves the right half onto the left; the second moves the lower half upward. Unfolding reflects the quarter-circle center cut into a complete central circular hole. The rectangular cut reaching the vertical fold becomes a long horizontal slot, mirrored above and below the center. The cut reaching the horizontal fold becomes a vertical slot, mirrored to the left and right. These orientations match A. The fold directions and cuts were visually checked.

Track each cut independently rather than rotating the whole pattern mentally. Reflection across a fold duplicates a notch and joins its two copies at the fold; it never rotates the notch by 90°. The horizontal notch touches the vertical fold, so that reflection doubles its horizontal length. The other fold makes its above/below pair. The small vertical notch similarly becomes the left/right pair. The central quarter-circle is reflected four times into one circle, not four separated holes.

## Solution

Joining the short edges makes the cylinder circumference 6 cm and height 1 cm. Its radius is 3/π, so its volume is 9/π cubic centimeters. The circumference is the long side because the short edges are identified. Thus 2πr=6, not 1; h=1. The cube requires six congruent faces. Six unit squares exactly exhaust the sheet's area, so the volume calculation uses all six faces, not an open box with five faces. Closing the cylinder ends is assumed rather than charged against sheet M's material, as the question explicitly states.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Five bags each contain the same set of ten distinct chocolate types. Independently pick one chocolate uniformly from each bag. What is the probability that at least two selected chocolates have the same type? A 0.3024; B 0.4235; C 0.6976; D 0.8125.

## Solution

Use the complement event that all five selected chocolates have distinct types. Under independent uniform selections its probability is (10·9·8·7·6)/10⁵=0.3024. Therefore the probability of at least one repeated type is 1−0.3024=0.6976, C.

There are 10^5 equally likely ordered selections, one coordinate per bag. Distinct-type selections have successively 10,9,8,7,6 choices. Counting the complement avoids double-counting outcomes containing two pairs or a triple, which would complicate direct counting of 'at least two'. It is not a without-replacement draw from one bag: the bags make equal types across draws possible.

**Official answer:** C OR D

**Derived answer:** C as an exhaustive alternative; D as individual entailment

**Paraphrased problem context:** Statements: All bacteria are microorganisms. All pathogens are microorganisms. Conclusions: I Some pathogens are bacteria. II All pathogens are not bacteria. Which option is logically correct? A only I; B only II; C either I or II; D neither I nor II. Preserve the wording of II because its scope is ambiguous.

## Solution

Both premises only place bacteria and pathogens inside the microorganism set. They do not force their overlap or disjointness. If conclusion II means no pathogen is a bacterium, it is the logical negation of conclusion I, so exactly one must hold in any interpretation, supporting C’s either-or reading. Neither conclusion individually follows from the premises, supporting D if the question asks which conclusions are entailed individually. The phrase all pathogens are not bacteria also permits a not-all scope reading, adding ambiguity. The official key explicitly accepts C OR D; preserve both rather than selecting one without qualification.

Two models show the lack of entailment: take microorganisms {a,b}, bacteria {a}, pathogens {a} for overlap, and take bacteria {a}, pathogens {b} for disjointness. Both satisfy the premises. Under the 'no pathogens are bacteria' reading, I and II are complementary propositions, so their disjunction is always true although neither individual proposition is forced. This distinction between truth of an either-or and individual entailment is why collapsing the accepted pair to one letter would lose information.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** Music improves exercise performance and reduces discomfort. Learning research is inconclusive: students needing external stimulation fared worse with music; those not needing it benefited. Choose the supported inference: A no learning effect and positive exercise effect; B positive effects on exercise and learning generally; C positive exercise effect and learning benefits for only some students; D positive learning effect for all and exercise benefits for only some.

## Solution

The passage attributes positive effects to music during exercise, while qualifying learning benefits as applying only to some learners. C preserves that contrast without turning a qualified observation into a universal one.

Separate two claims rather than averaging them into one conclusion. Exercise benefits are stated positively. The learning statement divides students into two groups with different outcomes, so “all learners” and “no effect on learning” both overstate the evidence. No causal claim about why the groups differ is required.

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** Choose which missing piece A–D combines with the supplied jigsaw piece to form a rectangle. Translation, rotation and reflection are allowed. The embedded figure preserves all projections and recesses of the supplied piece and candidates.

## Solution

Rotate candidate A clockwise by 90 degrees so its straight side becomes the left outer boundary. Its stepped right boundary complements the supplied piece: the reentrant hook after the tallest step fits the corresponding projection, and the deep central recess fits the remaining ledge. Together the outside edges form a rectangle. The competing shapes differ at the hook, recess depth, or stepped profile and cannot give the same fit. The source pieces and options were visually inspected.

A useful verification is to trace the shared boundary after rotating A: every protrusion of one piece must fill a recess of the other, with no overlap or gap, and the four external boundaries must be straight. Matching the overall silhouette or merely counting teeth is not sufficient; their order and depths matter.

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** Three class sizes have ratio 3:13:6. Adding 18 students to each changes it to 15:35:21. Find their original total. A 22; B 66; C 88; D 110.

## Solution

Write the initial quantities as 3k, 13k, 6k. Adding18 to each gives ratio15: 35: 21. The first two give35(3k+18)=15(13k+18), hence90k=360 and k=4. The third also gives 42/30=21/15. The initial total is 22k=88, C.

The common increase does not preserve the original ratios, so one cannot scale 3:13:6 directly to 15:35:21. With k=4, the initial sizes are 12,52,24 and the new sizes 30,70,42. Dividing the new sizes by 2 verifies every component of the target ratio.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Graph data (units sold, net profit): Year 1 (100,240), Year 2 (200,296), Year 3 (300,210). Year 3 cost/unit is 1, half Year 2 and one-third Year 1. Tax on selling price is respectively 10%,13%,15%. Net profit = selling price − cost − tax. Find Year 2:Year 3 total selling price. A 4:3; B 1:1; C 3:4; D 1:2.

## Solution

The graph gives units200 and 300, and total net profits296 and 210, for Years2 and 3. Their unit costs are2 and 1, giving total costs400 and 300. If total selling prices are S2, S3, then0.87S2−400=296 and 0.85S3−300=210. Thus S2=800 andS3=600, a 4: 3 ratio, A. This uses total selling price as in the graph accounting; unit selling prices would instead be4 and 2.

For each year write profit=(1−tax rate)×total revenue−units×unit cost. Taxes are not percentages of profit or cost. Check Year 2: revenue 800 less tax 104 less cost 400 gives 296. Year 3: revenue 600 less tax 90 less cost 300 gives 210. Dividing by units answers a different question.

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** Six distinct heights P,Q,R,S,T,U satisfy S>R; Q shortest; U taller than only one; T>S but T not tallest. Number taller than R equals number shorter than whom? A T; B R; C S; D P.

## Solution

The unique increasing height order is Q<U<R<S<T<P: Q is shortest, U next, R<S<T, and T is not tallest. Three students exceed R, while three are shorter than S. Thus S, option C.

Assign ranks 1 through 6 in increasing height. Q occupies 1 and U occupies 2. The ordered chain R<S<T uses three of the remaining four ranks, and T cannot occupy 6, so it must be 5, leaving P at 6 and R,S at 3,4. This also shows why merely knowing S>R is not enough without the other conditions.
