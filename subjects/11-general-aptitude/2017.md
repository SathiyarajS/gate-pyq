**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** 7^k/(9·10^(k−1)); C only for digit strings

**Source access:** Archived July2017 original official paper and key. Page numbers refer to the paper PDF; key is a separate two-page PDF.

## Solution

A uniformly chosen positive k-digit integer has 9·10^(k−1) possibilities. Each allowed position can contain 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, giving 7^k favorable integers. Thus the literal probability is (10/9)(0.7)^k, absent from the options. Official C, (0.7)^k, is valid for k independent uniformly random decimal digits with leading zeros allowed, not the usual k-digit-integer sample space.

# GATE 2017 CS1: Q7 (printed GA Q62)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 26](https://web.archive.org/web/20170712112248id_/http://www.gate.iitg.ac.in:80/2017questions/CS1.pdf#page=26); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Source access:** Archived July2017 original official paper and key. Page numbers refer to the paper PDF; key is a separate two-page PDF.

## Solution

At least three people are right-handed, hence at most three are left-handed. Every woman is left-handed, so there can be at most three women. If there were three women, they would be precisely the three left-handed people. Each woman’s immediate clockwise successor would then be another woman. A nonempty proper subset of a single six-seat cycle cannot be closed under successor, a contradiction. With at least two women, exactly two remain. Feasibility: consecutive seats W-left, W-left, M-left, M-right, M-right, M-right satisfy the rule. Thus A.
