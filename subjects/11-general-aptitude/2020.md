

# GATE 2020 CS: Q1 (printed GA Q1)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 1](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=1); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Paraphrased problem context:** Complete: Raman is confident of speaking English ___ six months as he has been practising regularly ___ the last three weeks. Options: A during, for; B for, since; C for, in; D within, for.

## Solution

Worked explanation

Read each blank according to its time role. The first says when the desired ability will have been acquired: “within six months” means no later than the end of that period. The second follows present-perfect continuous “has been practising” and specifies a duration, so it takes “for.” “Since” would require a starting point such as “since Monday,” not “the last three weeks.” “During” locates an event inside a period but does not express this deadline. Substitution produces the coherent sentence with D.

Derivation and answer

Within six months sets a future deadline, while for the last three weeks describes the duration of ongoing practice. Thus within, for, D.

# GATE 2020 CS: Q2 (printed GA Q2)

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** Complete: His knowledge of the subject was excellent but his classroom performance was ___. Options: A extremely poor; B good; C desirable; D praiseworthy.

## Solution

Worked explanation

The conjunction “but” signals an unexpected contrast between strong subject knowledge and classroom results. A negative description supplies that contrast. All three distractors, good, desirable and praiseworthy, positively evaluate the performance and therefore continue rather than contrast the first clause. This is a semantic fit question, not a question of grammatical adjective placement: all four phrases can follow “was,” but only A matches the intended contrast.

Derivation and answer

But signals a contrast with excellent knowledge. Extremely poor supplies that contrast for classroom performance; the positive alternatives do not. Thus A.

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** Complete the analogy Cook : Cook :: Fly : ___. Options: A Flyer; B Flying; C Flew; D Flighter.

## Solution

Worked explanation

Identify the relationship before trying to match word endings. In the left pair, the same spelling has two grammatical roles: to cook is the action, and a cook is the person doing it. Apply action→agent to fly. A flyer is someone or something that flies. Flying names the activity or participle, flew is a past-tense verb, and flighter is not the standard agent noun. Thus the matching role, rather than identical spelling, decides A.

Derivation and answer

The first cook is an action and the second cook is the person performing it. The corresponding person associated with flying is a flyer, A.

# GATE 2020 CS: Q4 (printed GA Q4)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 1](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=1); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Paraphrased problem context:** Passage essentials: Melting glaciers supply too much or too little freshwater to billions dependent on them. Without deep emissions cuts, a UN report estimates at least 30% of northern-hemisphere surface permafrost could melt by century end. Rising seas could displace billions, so nations should rethink their carbon footprint for political concerns, if not environmental ones. What follows? A Nations have no environmental concerns; B Nations are responsible for providing freshwater to billions; C Billions are responsible for man-made emissions; D Billions are affected by melting glaciers.

## Solution

Worked explanation

An inference must be supported by the passage without importing extra claims. Water-supply changes already establish an effect on people, while displacement adds another effect. The phrase “if not for environmental ones” urges political motivation even where environmental motivation is insufficient; it does not assert that every nation lacks environmental concerns. Dependence on freshwater does not establish which institution must supply it, and mention of human emissions does not identify all affected people as emitters. Hence only D is warranted.

Derivation and answer

The passage explicitly connects melting glaciers to billions of people who depend on them and to displacement from rising seas. Thus billions are affected, D. It does not establish the stronger claims about lack of environmental concern or responsibility in the other choices.

# GATE 2020 CS: Q5 (printed GA Q5)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 2](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=2); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** Find the cheapest directed route from 1 to 2, counting edge costs and tolls. Complete figure equivalent, edge:rupees: 1→a:200, a→c:100, c→2:100, a→2:200, 1→b:300, b→2:200, 1→f:100, f→b:0, b→d:0, d→e:100, f→e:100, e→2:200. Tolls: a,e cost 200 each; b,c,d,f cost 100 each. Options: A 1-a-c-2; B 1-f-b-2; C 1-b-2; D 1-f-e-2.

## Solution

Worked explanation

A useful accounting rule is route cost = sum of directed edge weights + sum of tolls at intermediate vertices. Do not confuse a zero-cost edge with a free toll booth. For B, the edges cost 100+0+200=300 and booths f,b add 100+100=200, totaling 500. For A, edges total 400 and booths add 300, totaling 700. C totals 500+100=600 and D totals 400+300=700. For completeness, route 1-a-2 costs 400+200=600. A detour b-d-e-2 costs 0+100+200 plus tolls d,e instead of direct b-2 at 200, so it cannot improve a route already reaching b. This checks the global minimum as well as the listed options.

Derivation and answer

Include both edge costs and every visited toll. The listed routes cost: 1-a-c-2=200+100+100+200+100=700; 1-f-b-2=100+0+200+100+100=500; 1-b-2=300+200+100=600; 1-f-e-2=100+100+200+100+200=700. Thus B. The additional routes through d or directly from a do not beat500; all edge directions and tolls were visually checked.

# GATE 2020 CS: Q7 (printed GA Q7)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 2](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=2); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** If P=3, R=27 and T=243, find Q+S using the intended alphabetic number pattern. Options: A 40; B 80; C 90; D 110.

## Solution

Worked explanation

Fill in consecutive letters rather than interpolating by arithmetic differences: P,Q,R,S,T correspond to 3¹,3²,3³,3⁴,3⁵. Consecutive multiplication by 3 produces Q=9 and S=81, so their sum is 90. As a consistency check, moving two letters multiplies the value by 9: 3→27→243. The inference is the intended simple puzzle rule, not uniqueness among all functions on letters; infinitely many contrived rules fit the three supplied values.

Derivation and answer

The intended alphabetic sequence P, Q, R, S, T takes successive powers of 3: 3, 9, 27, 81, 243. Hence Q+S=9+81=90, C. As in sequence puzzles, this uses the simple geometric pattern; three specified values do not mathematically determine a unique arbitrary assignment.

# GATE 2020 CS: Q8 (printed GA Q8)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 2](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=2); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** An annulus has inner radius a and outer radius b. Its painted region comprises n nonoverlapping equal disks, each tangent to both concentric boundaries (figure equivalent). Find unpainted area. Options: A π[(b²−a²)−(n/4)(b−a)²]; B π[(b²−a²)−n(b−a)²]; C π[(b²−a²)+(n/4)(b−a)²]; D π[(b²−a²)+n(b−a)²].

## Solution

Worked explanation

Let ρ be a painted disk’s radius and d the distance from the common annulus center to its center. Internal tangency to the outer circle gives d+ρ=b; external tangency to the inner circle gives d−ρ=a. Subtracting yields 2ρ=b−a. Therefore one disk has area π(b−a)²/4. The nonoverlap shown in the figure lets us multiply by n. The annular area is the larger circle’s area minus the smaller circle’s area. Subtract the painted area, not add it: this rejects C,D. B mistakenly treats the annulus thickness as a radius rather than a diameter.

Derivation and answer

Each painted disk touches both concentric boundaries, so its diameter is b−a and radius(b−a)/2. For n nonoverlapping disks as pictured, total painted area is nπ(b−a)²/4. Subtract from annular areaπ(b²−a²), givingπ[(b²−a²)−n(b−a)²/4], A. The options continue on physical page3.

# GATE 2020 CS: Q9 (printed GA Q9)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 3](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=3); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** Two perpendicular lines in the xy-plane make acute angles α and β with the x-axis. Find α+β. Options: A 60°; B 90°; C 120°; D 180°. 

## Solution

Worked explanation

A line has no preferred direction, so use its smaller angle with the x-axis. If one line has inclination θ with 0<θ<90°, its perpendicular has inclination θ+90°, whose acute angle to the x-axis is 180°−(θ+90°)=90°−θ. The sum is therefore 90°. Equivalently, for nonvertical perpendicular lines m1 m2=−1, so their acute-angle tangents are reciprocals. The word “acute” excludes the horizontal/vertical endpoint case.

Derivation and answer

For perpendicular non-axis-aligned lines, their acute angles with the x-axis are complementary: if one isθ, the other is 90°−θ. Hence α+β=90°, B.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Bar-chart equivalent: company revenues for 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018 are respectively 500,700,800,600,400 million rupees. Expenditure is 500 million each year. Find aggregate profit/loss as a percentage of total expenditure. Options: A 16.67% profit; B 16.67% loss; C 20% profit; D 20% loss.

## Solution

Worked explanation

Compute aggregate figures before taking a percentage. The annual profits in millions are 0,200,300,100,−100, whose sum is 500. Total expenditure is 2500, so 100×500/2500=20%. The alternative 16.67% arises from dividing by revenue 3000; that is a profit margin, not the requested return on expenditure. The positive aggregate identifies profit rather than loss. Because expenditures happen to be equal, averaging annual expenditure-based percentages also works here, but adding the money amounts is the safer general method.

Derivation and answer

Visually reading the bars gives revenues500, 700, 800, 600, 400 million, totaling3000. Total expenditure is 5·500=2500. Profit is 500, and profit/expenditure is 500/2500=20%, C.
