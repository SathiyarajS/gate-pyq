**Paraphrased problem context:** Choose the phrase that completes a sentence explaining how the project's expenditure is divided into equipment (Rs. 20 lakhs), salaries (Rs. 12 lakhs), and contingency (Rs. 3 lakhs): “The expenditure on the project ___ as follows …”. Choices: A break down; B break; C breaks down; D breaks.

## Solution

Method: check both subject–verb agreement and the meaning of the verb phrase.

1. The grammatical subject is “expenditure”, a singular mass noun. The words “on the project” only modify it. The later list of three spending categories does not make the subject plural.
2. A present-tense verb with that singular subject takes the -s form. This eliminates “break down” and “break”.
3. “Breaks down” means can be analyzed or divided into constituent amounts, exactly what the following cost list does. “Breaks” alone suggests interruption or damage and does not express that meaning.

Result: C, “breaks down”. The individual amounts are context for the breakdown; adding them is unnecessary for this grammar question.

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

**Paraphrased problem context:** Complete the statement that a search engine's business model ___ around the fulcrum of trust. Choices: A revolves; B plays; C sinks; D bursts.

## Solution

Method: interpret the physical metaphor and then choose the matching expression.

1. A fulcrum is a pivot or support about which a lever turns. In the sentence, trust is metaphorically the central support of the business model.
2. “Revolves around” is the established expression for being organized around or depending centrally on something. It fits both “around” and “fulcrum”.
3. “Plays around” implies casual activity, “sinks” implies downward movement, and “bursts” implies rupture. None conveys rotation or central dependence in this construction.

Result: A, “revolves”. The decision is based on meaning and collocation, not on a disagreement in verb number: all four offered verbs are singular forms.

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** Two cars depart simultaneously from the same place in the same direction, at constant speeds 50 km/h and 60 km/h. How long until they are 20 km apart? Choices in hours: A 1; B 2; C 3; D 6.

## Solution

Method: measure the motion of the faster car relative to the slower one.

1. After t hours, the slower car has travelled 50t km and the faster car 60t km from the shared starting point.
2. Because they travel in the same direction, their separation is the difference, 60t − 50t = 10t km. Adding the speeds would apply to motion in opposite directions, not this case.
3. Set the required separation equal to 20: 10t = 20, so t = 2 hours.
4. Check: after two hours their distances are 100 km and 120 km; the difference is indeed 20 km.

Result: B, 2 hours.

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** Ten friends initially plan equal shares of a teacher's gift. Two withdraw, so each of the remaining eight pays Rs. 150 more than originally planned. Find the total gift cost. Choices: A Rs. 666; B Rs. 3000; C Rs. 6000; D Rs. 12000.

## Solution

Method: express both individual shares in terms of the unchanged total cost.

1. Let the gift cost be G rupees. The original contribution per person is G/10.
2. With only eight contributors, the new contribution is G/8. The given increase is therefore G/8 − G/10 = 150.
3. Use common denominator 40: (5G − 4G)/40 = 150, giving G = 150 × 40 = 6000.
4. Verify both shares: 6000/10 = 600 and 6000/8 = 750. Their difference is 150, as required.

Result: C, Rs. 6000. The total extra payment by the eight remaining friends is 8 × 150 = 1200, which also equals the two missing original contributions, 2 × 600.

Result: D, a school. Merely finding any association with “teacher” would not preserve the original analogy.

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** Exactly one of P, Q, R, S committed a crime, and exactly one of their statements is true. P accuses Q; Q accuses S; R denies committing it; S says Q's accusation of S is false. Identify the offender. Choices: A P; B R; C S; D Q.

## Solution

Method: exploit the pair of logically opposite statements before testing suspects individually.

1. Q's statement is “S committed the crime”. S's statement denies precisely that proposition. One of these two statements must be true and the other false, regardless of the offender.
2. That pair already accounts for the one permitted true statement. Thus P's statement and R's statement must both be false.
3. P's false accusation tells us Q is not the offender. R's false denial tells us R is the offender.
4. Check the entire assignment with R guilty: P's accusation of Q is false, Q's accusation of S is false, R's denial is false, and S's denial of Q's accusation is true. There is exactly one offender and exactly one true statement.

Result: B, R. It is unnecessary to assume that the guilty person always lies; the conclusion follows from the given count of true statements.

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** A diagram uses a triangle for teachers, circle for researchers, and rectangle for administrators. Its five disjoint numbered regions contain: teachers only 70; teachers and administrators 10; administrators only 20; researchers and administrators 20; researchers only 40. Find the range containing the percentage who are administrators. Choices: A 0–15; B 16–30; C 31–45; D 46–60 percent.

## Solution

Method: add disjoint regions once for the population, then add every region inside the administrator rectangle for the numerator.

1. The total represented population is 70 + 10 + 20 + 20 + 40 = 160. Region counts describe distinct people, so overlap regions must not be counted twice in this total.
2. Being an administrator does not exclude being a teacher or researcher. The rectangle contains its teacher overlap (10), administrator-only region (20), and researcher overlap (20), for 50 administrators.
3. The required percentage is (50/160) × 100 = 31.25%.
4. Compare the numeric percentage with the intervals: 31.25 lies between 31 and 45.

Result: C. Counting only the administrator-only region would give 20/160, but would omit administrators who also belong to another category.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A passage describes a High Court judgment treating begging as a symptom rather than a disease, and identifies the underlying problem as state failure to protect people who fall through the social-security net. Which inference is closest? A beggars are unwilling to work; B lack of social-welfare schemes produces begging; C begging is an offence requiring firm punishment; D begging should be banned for harming state welfare.

## Solution

Method: distinguish the passage's stated causal explanation from unsupported judgments about individuals or punishment.

1. The passage rejects treating begging itself as the underlying disease. It instead points to failure of public protection for vulnerable citizens.
2. Option B connects begging with inadequate social welfare. This is the closest available restatement of the passage's cause-and-symptom argument.
3. Option A introduces laziness or unwillingness to work, which the passage never asserts. Options C and D recommend criminalization or prohibition, whereas the passage explicitly questions the stigmatizing and criminalizing view.

Result: B is the intended answer. Strictly, failure or insufficiency of provision does not prove the complete absence of every welfare scheme; read lack in that practical sense. The answer is a best-supported inference among the options, not evidence for a stronger universal claim that no welfare schemes exist.

**Paraphrased problem context:** The exclusive club memberships are Drama only 60, Dance only 80, Maths only 30. Inclusive pairwise memberships are Drama–Dance 40, Dance–Maths 12, Drama–Maths 7; all three clubs contain 2 students. Seventy-five percent of the college belongs to none. Find total enrolment. Choices: A 1000; B 975; C 900; D 225.

## Solution

Method: first convert the overlapping counts into disjoint regions, then use the percentage outside the union.

1. Each inclusive pair count contains the two students belonging to all three clubs. Thus exactly Drama and Dance contains 40 − 2 = 38; exactly Dance and Maths contains 12 − 2 = 10; exactly Drama and Maths contains 7 − 2 = 5.
2. Add all seven disjoint regions once: 60 + 80 + 30 + 38 + 10 + 5 + 2 = 225 students in at least one club.
3. If 75% belong to no club, the union is the remaining 25% of the college. With total N, 0.25N = 225.
4. Hence N = 225/0.25 = 900. As a check, 675 outside plus 225 inside gives 900, and 675/900 = 75%.

Result: C, 900. The value 225 is only the club union, not the whole college.
