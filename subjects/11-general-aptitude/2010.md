**Paraphrased problem context:** Choose the most appropriate completion: “His rather casual remarks on politics ______ his lack of seriousness about the subject.”
Options: (A) masked; (B) belied; (C) betrayed; (D) suppressed.

## Solution

The remarks reveal, rather than hide or contradict, the lack of seriousness. In this context betrayed means inadvertently revealed. Hence C; masked, belied and suppressed have the wrong relationship to the evidence.

Teaching detail: The sentence treats the casual remarks as evidence of a lack of seriousness. “Betray” can mean reveal something unintentionally, as a nervous voice betrays anxiety. “Masked” and “suppressed” would hide the lack, while “belied” would contradict it. The contextual sense is revelation, not the separate sense of disloyalty.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Choose the word closest in meaning to “circuitous.”
Options: (A) cyclic; (B) indirect; (C) confusing; (D) crooked.

## Solution

Circuitous means taking a roundabout rather than direct route, literally or figuratively. Indirect is therefore the closest synonym, B. Cyclic means repeating in a cycle, which is not required.

Teaching detail: A circuitous explanation reaches its point by a roundabout route; a circuitous road reaches its destination indirectly. “Cyclic” instead means recurring in cycles, “confusing” describes difficulty understanding, and “crooked” can mean physically bent or dishonest. These may sometimes accompany a circuitous route, but indirect is the closest direct synonym.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Of 25 people in a room, 15 play hockey, 17 play football and 10 play both. How many play neither sport?
Options: (A) 2; (B) 17; (C) 13; (D) 3.

## Solution

Inclusion–exclusion gives 15+17−10=22 people playing at least one of the two sports. The complement in the 25-person room has size 25−22=3, option D. Subtracting the intersection once avoids double counting.

Teaching detail: A disjoint-region check gives hockey only 15−10=5, football only 17−10=7 and both=10. Together these account for 22 people, leaving three outside both circles. The tempting 15+17=32 double-counts the ten people in the overlap and cannot be subtracted directly from 25.

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** If 137+276=435, how much is 731+672? Use the intended interpretation that all numerals are written in a common positional base.
Options: (A) 534; (B) 1403; (C) 1623; (D) 1513.

## Solution

Assume the numerals use one positional integer base b. Expanding 137_b+276_b=435_b gives 3b²+10b+13=4b²+3b+5, so (b−8)(b+1)=0. The valid base is 8. Then 731_8+672_8 has units 3, tens 3+7=10 decimal giving digit 2 and carry 1, and hundreds 7+6+1=14 decimal giving digit 6 and carry 1. The result is 1623_8, option C. This uses the intended common-base interpretation; an arbitrary invented operation would not be uniquely determined.

Teaching detail: A base must exceed every digit used, so b>7 rules out the negative root −1 of the quadratic. Carrying is then in base eight: the tens-column sum 10 in decimal is 12 in octal, and the hundreds-column sum 14 in decimal is 16 in octal. As a check, the addends are 473 and 442 in decimal, totaling 915, whose octal representation is 1623.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Hari (H), Gita (G), Irfan (I), and Saira (S) are siblings, all born on January 1, with no twins. Successive siblings differ in age by less than three years. H’s age+G’s age>I’s age+S’s age. G and S differ by one year; G is not oldest and S is not youngest. Find their order, oldest first.
Options: (A) HSIG; (B) SGHI; (C) IGSH; (D) IHSG.

## Solution

Since all birthdays are January 1 and there are no twins, adjacent age gaps are positive integers 1 or 2. Gita and Saira differ by one year, so they are adjacent in birth order. A places Irfan between them. In C, the order Irfan–Gita–Saira–Hari gives I>G and S>H, hence I+S>G+H, contradicting condition (i). D gives Irfan oldest and Hari second, also forcing H+G<I+S. Only B, Saira–Gita–Hari–Irfan, can satisfy the inequalities: ages 5,4,3,1 give H+G=7>I+S=6 and all adjacent gaps are below 3. Thus B.

Teaching detail: For option D, I>H>S>G and the difference I−H is positive while S−G=1, so (I+S)−(H+G)>0, contradicting the required inequality. Option A already violates the one-year G–S gap because another sibling lies between them. The ages used for B are only a feasibility witness; adding the same constant to all four ages preserves every condition.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Five skilled workers build a wall in 20 days; eight semi-skilled workers build it in 25 days; ten unskilled workers build it in 30 days. How long will two skilled, six semi-skilled and five unskilled workers together take?
Options: (A) 20 days; (B) 18 days; (C) 16 days; (D) 15 days.

## Solution

One skilled worker builds 1/(5·20)=1/100 wall per day; one semi-skilled worker builds 1/(8·25)=1/200; one unskilled worker builds 1/(10·30)=1/300. The team rate is 2/100+6/200+5/300=1/15 wall per day. Therefore completion takes 15 days, D, assuming additive constant productivity as in the question.

Teaching detail: Convert each class to per-worker productivity before combining headcounts. Two skilled workers contribute 2/100=6/300 walls/day; six semi-skilled contribute 6/200=9/300; five unskilled contribute 5/300. Their sum is 20/300=1/15 wall/day, so the reciprocal is 15 days. Adding or averaging the three completion times would ignore team size and differing productivity.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Using digits from the multiset {2,2,3,3,3,4,4,4,4}, how many distinct four-digit numbers greater than 3000 can be formed? A digit cannot be used more often than it occurs.
Options: (A) 50; (B) 51; (C) 52; (D) 54.

## Solution

The available digits are two 2s, three 3s and four 4s. A number above 3000 starts with 3 or 4. After a leading 3, the remaining three positions have 3³ possibilities except 222 and 333, giving 25. After a leading 4, only 222 is forbidden, giving 26; 444 is allowed because four 4s are available overall. Total 25+26=51, B. Equal copies are not distinguished.

Teaching detail: Count arrangements of digit values, not permutations of distinguishable physical copies. There are initially 27 suffixes for either allowed leading digit. With leading 3, suffix 333 would require four 3s and 222 would require three 2s; with leading 4, only 222 exceeds supply. No leading 2 can produce a number above 3000, so the two cases are exhaustive.
