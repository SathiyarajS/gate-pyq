**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D under the general recursion storage model

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2014.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

Unbounded nested activations need distinct live activation records, allocated and reclaimed as calls occur; a single static record per procedure is insufficient. A stack suffices, without heap allocation or automatic garbage collection, so the intended correct statements are1 and 3, D. Bounded recursion or tail-call elimination can use fixed storage; the claim concerns general unrestricted recursive calls, not every special recursive program.

# GATE 2014 CS3: Q44 (printed CS Q34)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 60](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A with algebraic simplification

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2014.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

Let initial leaves be b0, c0, d0. Form u=b0+c0, v=u+d0, w=b0+v. Then final c=v, d=w, e=w−b0=v, and a=e+b0=w. The optimized DAG has 3 leaves and 3 binary-add nodes, total 6 nodes and 6edges, A. A purely syntactic DAG without cancellation would have8 nodes and 10edges. The keyed minimum assumes algebraic identities valid for the arithmetic model, not arbitrary floating-point reassociation/cancellation.
