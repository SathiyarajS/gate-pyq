## Solution

Removing left recursion and left factoring remove common obstacles but do not make every CFG LL(1). ## Solution

Thus their automata have the same number of states, n1=n2, B, under the conventional constructions, even though their reduction lookaheads and conflict behavior differ.

**Independently derived answer:** B under a restricted one-pass evaluation model

## Solution

Inherited attributes cannot in general be computed by the simple reduce-only mechanism used for synthesized attributes. In the usual left-to-right one-pass implementation, usable inherited information must be available from the parent context and already processed left siblings, the L-attributed dependency condition behind B. Even then suitable scheduling/marker productions or stack access may be needed; being L-attributed is not a blanket guarantee for every unmodified LR evaluation scheme. If bottom-up evaluation instead allows building a full parse/dependency graph and later scheduling arbitrary acyclic dependencies, L-attributedness is not necessary. The choice therefore depends on the conventional parsing-time model, which the prompt does not spell out.

## Solution

FIRST(eS)={e}, so the rule S′→eS enters M[S′,e]. Because S′ can be empty and follows S in S→iEtSS′, e belongs to FOLLOW(S), while $ belongs to FOLLOW(S) because S is the start symbol. The trailing S′ inherits FOLLOW(S), giving FOLLOW(S′)={e,$}. Thus S′→ε also enters both cells. The e cell contains both alternatives and the $ cell contains only ε, option D; the conflict means this grammar is not LL(1).

**Independently derived answer:** D

## Solution

Under lexical scope the delayed actual expression i+j refers to main's enclosing global bindings, initially 100 and 5. Its first demand evaluates and memoizes 105, so print(x+10) emits 115. Assigning 200 to P's local i and 20 to global j cannot change the already cached actual value. The later print(x) emits 105. Thus D, 115 followed by 105. Call by need evaluates once on first demand; it is not repeated call-by-name evaluation.
