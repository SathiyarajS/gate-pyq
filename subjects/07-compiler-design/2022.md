

# GATE 2022 CS: Q13

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 12](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2022/cs_2022.pdf#page=12); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

## Solution

Every deterministic context-free language has an LR(1) grammar, so LR(1) parsing is sufficient for these languages, D. LALR merging can introduce reduce-reduce conflicts even when canonical LR has none. Symbol-table information is used beyond lexical analysis, and data-flow analysis is not a necessary runtime step in program execution. The claim is about existence of a suitable LR(1) grammar, not that every grammar of the language is LR(1).

**Type / marks:** NAT, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 5 to 5

**Derived answer:** 5

## Solution

Taking goto on + advances S→S·+R to S→S+·R. Closure then adds R→·R*P, R→·P, P→·(S), and P→·id. No dot precedes S in these items, so no further closure is needed. Total 5 items.

# GATE 2022 CS: Q65

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 41](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2022/cs_2022.pdf#page=41); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 80 to 80

**Derived answer:** 80

## Solution

The grammar gives % higher precedence than #, with both operators left-associative. Their semantic operations are division and multiplication, respectively, rather than C remainder. The three T values are 20, 10/5=2, and (8/2)/2=2. The left-associative S combination therefore gives (20*2)*2=80. The printed nonterminal list includes an unused P, but no production or calculation depends on it.
