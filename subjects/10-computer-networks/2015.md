**Paraphrased problem context:** Two hosts transfer a large file over TCP. Which claims are false? I a segment numbered m is always followed by m+1; II estimated RTT t implies retransmission timeout≥t; III advertised window size never changes; IV unacknowledged bytes are always≤advertised window. A III only; B I and III; C I and IV; D II and IV.

## Solution

A segment carrying L data bytes advances the data sequence number by L, not by one, so a 1000-byte segment beginning at m normally leads to m+1000. The receive window tracks available receiver buffer space and can change as data arrives and is consumed. The textbook RTO adds a delay-variation allowance to the smoothed RTT. The intended flow-control invariant yields B, but the absolute “always” in IV must retain the inherited window-shrink qualification: a new smaller advertisement does not retract bytes already in flight.

Baseline derivation and retained qualifications: Sequence numbers count bytes, so I is false. RTO includes estimated RTT plus a nonnegative variability margin, making II true in the usual model. The receiver-advertised window varies, so III is false. With a nonshrinking receive-window right edge, outstanding data respects the advertised window, giving IV true and false statements I and III, official B. The word always in IV needs qualification: a receiver can shrink its window after data was sent, leaving more outstanding bytes than its new advertisement. TCP discourages shrinking but implementations must tolerate it. Therefore B assumes the simplified flow-control model.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Two systems are separated by 8000 km. The link rate is 500×10⁶ bits/s, propagation speed is 4×10⁶ m/s, and average packet size is 10⁷ bits. Design a Go-Back-N sliding-window protocol that fully utilizes the link, neglecting processing delays and using the standard negligible-ACK-transmission model. What minimum number of sequence-number bits is required?

## Solution

1. Full utilization requires enough outstanding packets to bridge a packet’s serialization plus the round-trip propagation before its ACK permits reuse. A 201-packet window meets this bound; the Go-Back-N sequence space must contain at least 202 distinct sequence numbers to avoid old/new ambiguity.

2. One-way propagation=8·10^6/(4·10^6)=2s. Packet serialization=10^7/(500·10^6)=0.02s. To stay busy with negligible ACK time, window≥1+2·2/0.02=201 packets. Go-Back-N requires W≤2^k−1; 2^7−1=127 is insufficient and 2^8−1=255 suffices. Thus8 bits.
