## Solution

There are10⁶/10³=1000 chunks, each of 8000 bits. Each link takes 8000/10⁶=0.008 seconds per chunk. # GATE 2024 CS2: Q28

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 19](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2024/CS224S6.pdf#page=19); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B;C

**Derived answer:** B;C when DF=0; C without that qualification

## Solution

In ordinary IPv4 with fragmentation permitted, an intermediate router can fragment a datagram larger than its outgoing MTU, and reassembly occurs only at the final IP destination. This gives the official B, C. However B is overbroad without the DF-bit condition: when Don’t Fragment is set, the router drops an oversized datagram and normally sends an ICMP fragmentation-needed message instead of fragmenting it. Thus B is not an unconditional statement about every IPv4 datagram. A and D are false; C remains true. Preserve official B, C, and distinguish the intended DF=0 model from the literal unrestricted claim.

**Official answer:** 4 to 4

**Derived answer:** 4

## Solution

Under the standard timeout slow-start model, a timeout sets ssthresh to half the old congestion window, 6MSS, and resets cwnd to 1MSS. The first subsequent RTT uses1 and its successful ACKs grow cwnd to 2; the second uses2 and grows it to 4. Thus during the third RTT cwnd is 4MSS, still below the threshold. This assumes the textbook one-MSS post-timeout reset and per-round doubling.

# GATE 2024 CS2: Q55

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 500 to 500

**Derived answer:** 500

## Solution

The one-way propagation delay is 500/(2×10⁸)=2.5μs. To detect a collision originating at the far end, transmission must continue for at least the round-trip delay5μs. At10⁸bits/s this requires10⁸×5×10⁻⁶=500 bits. This is the theoretical collision-detection minimum from the supplied parameters, not the separate standardized512-bit Ethernet minimum.
