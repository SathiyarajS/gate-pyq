**Essential problem context (paraphrased):** In serial transmission, a byte is preceded by 0 and followed by one or two 1 bits. What is the purpose?
(A) Synchronize the receiver for byte reception.
(B) Recover lost 0s and 1s from those extra bits.
(C) Use those extra bits for parity calculation.
(D) None of these.

## Solution

Step 1: Explain why each byte needs a framing event.

Step 2: The receiver and transmitter need not share one continuously aligned clock. The falling transition into the start bit establishes the reference for sampling the following data bits; the stop interval checks framing and separates successive bytes. A fixed start/stop pattern cannot reconstruct arbitrary lost bits and does not calculate whether the data contains an odd or even number of ones.

Conclusion and qualifications: Asynchronous reception detects the low start bit to establish the bit-sampling phase for that byte; the high stop interval returns the line to its idle condition and permits framing. These bits synchronize byte reception, A. They are not parity or recovery codes.
