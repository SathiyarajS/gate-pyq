# GATE 2017 CS2: Q28 (printed CS Q18)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 7](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B under single bidirectional peer interpretation

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2-2017.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

A UDP connect sets a default destination and filters ordinary receives to that peer; calling connect again can change that association, so II is true and intended B treats I as simultaneous bidirectional associations. Linux nevertheless permits sendto/sendmsg with another destination even on a connected UDP socket. Consequently a blanket claim that it cannot send to multiple peers is false. The word communicate needs the bidirectional/default-peer interpretation for B. See https://www.man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/udp.7.html for the explicit alternate-destination rule.

# GATE 2017 CS1: Q25 (printed CS Q15)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 9](https://web.archive.org/web/20170712112248id_/http://www.gate.iitg.ac.in:80/2017questions/CS1.pdf#page=9); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Source access:** Archived July2017 original official paper and key. Page numbers refer to the paper PDF; key is a separate two-page PDF.

## Solution

A birthday collision attack lets the signer prepare many benign and fraudulent messages, find a pair with equal hash, sign the benign member and substitute the fraudulent one. An outsider or receiver given an already fixed signed message must find a second preimage for its specific hash, not a birthday collision between two freely chosen sets. Thus only I, B, under the standard hash-then-sign model.

## Solution

Read the 1980 bytes in the information frame as its total length, including20 overhead bytes. Useful payload is 1960 bytes, taking15.68ms at 1Mbps. Data transmission takes 15.84ms, ACK transmission0.16ms, round-trip propagation1.50ms, and processing both received frames0.50ms. The stop-and-wait cycle is 18.00ms; useful-data efficiency is 100*15.68/18=87.11%. If1980 instead denotes payload with 20 additional overhead bytes, efficiency is 100*15.84/18.16=87.22%. Both lie in the archived official interval86.5–89.5; the precise interpretation of frame length/processing must be stated. The archive key is not certified here as the final revision.
