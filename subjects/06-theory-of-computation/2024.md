**Derived answer:** B;C

## Solution

Let u count applications of S→aS and v count applications of S→aSbS. Then n_a=u+v and n_b=v. A derivation starts with one S; unary rules leave the number of outstanding S unchanged, binary rules increase it by one, and S→c decreases it by one. Thus n_c=v+1, proving C. Also n_a≥v=n_c−1>n_c−2, proving B. A fails for w=c, where both a and b counts are0; D also fails for c, whose c count is 1 and b count 0.

It therefore accepts exactly the strings with an odd positive number of a’s. For any positive length ℓ exactly half of the 2^ℓ binary strings have odd a-parity, by flipping one fixed position.
