**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2018.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

To recognize the intersection of two recursively enumerable languages, dovetail their recognizers on the same input and accept when both accept. Hence intersection closure holds. Complement closure would make every recognizable language decidable, which is false; encodings of recognizers are countable. Thus B.

# GATE 2018 CS: Q45 (printed CS Q35)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 14](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2018.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

I is context-free: a PDA tracks the signed running difference(#a+#c)−(#b+#d) with one stack symbol for positive excess and another for negative excess, canceling opposite contributions; finite control enforces a*b*c*d* order. II is the concatenation{a^n b^n}{c^p d^p}, a CFL. For III, intersect with a+b+c+ (no d); the result is{a^n b^n c^n: n≥1}, not context-free. For IV, suppose pumping length p and take a^p b^p c^(p²). Pumping up either disrupts block order or adds α a symbols, β b symbols and γ c symbols, with α+β+γ≤p and at least one added symbol. If α+β=0, the product stays p² but γ>0. Otherwise the required increase in c is p(α+β)+αβ≥p, while actual γ≤p−α−β<p. In every case the equality mn=p+q fails (here the fourth-block length remains0). Thus only I and II are CFL, B.

# GATE 2018 CS: Q46 (printed CS Q36)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 14](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2018.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

I is the undecidable membership problem for unrestricted grammars/Turing-recognizable languages. II is a nontrivial semantic property of a TM language, hence undecidable by Rice’s theorem. III, equivalence of general grammars, is undecidable (even equivalence of CFGs is undecidable). IV always has answer yes: convert the NFA to a DFA and view that DFA as a DPDA that does not use its stack. Hence only I, II, III are undecidable, D. If III were restricted to regular grammars it would be decidable, but no such restriction is stated.

# GATE 2018 CS: Q62 (printed CS Q52)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 21](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 2 to 2

**Derived answer:** 2

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2018.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

The start state is accepting. After one0 the automaton enters an accepting state alternating with a nonaccepting state, so L1 contains epsilon and precisely the positive odd-length unary words. L1 squared contains every unary word: odd lengths use one odd word and epsilon, while any positive even length is the sum of two positive odd lengths. Hence L1²=0* and L1³=0*. But L1^0 differs from L1, and L1 differs from L1² because length 2 is absent from L1. The smallest equality index is therefore2.
