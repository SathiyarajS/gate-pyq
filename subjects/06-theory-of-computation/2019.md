

# GATE 2019 CS: Q17 (printed CS Q7)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 5](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=5); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B under not-necessarily-regular interpretation

## Solution

Concatenation with the reversal of a regular language is regular, as are its prefix and suffix closures. In contrast {ww^R: w∈L} need not be regular: L={a, b}* gives the nonregular even palindromes. Thus B identifies the operation that does not preserve regularity. Literally B can be regular for finite or empty L, so the unconditional wording must mean not necessarily regular.

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

## Solution

The language is the union of a-lengths2 modulo3 and b-lengths10 modulo12. With pumping length 24, pump an initial a³ or b^12; every admitted word of length at least24 stays in its residue class for all repetition counts, including zero. Each offered smaller value3, 5, 9 fails on b^10: a nonempty pumped substring has length at most that value, so pumping up changes the residue modulo12. Thus24, D.

# GATE 2019 CS: Q41 (printed CS Q31)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 10](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=10); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

## Solution

A is the even-palindrome CFL. B has grammar S→aSa|bSb|T and T→aTb|epsilon. D is a finite union of fixed-ratio CFLs. To prove C non-context-free, intersect it with the regular language a+ b a+ b a+ b+. The result is {a^i b a^j b a^i b^k: i, k≥1, j≥k, j−k even}: the two isolated b symbols force w=a^i b a^t, giving j=2t+k. If this intersection had pumping length p, consider a^p b a^p b a^p b^p. A pumping window of length at most p cannot touch both first and third a-runs, nor both the middle a-run and final b-run. Pumping any first/third a changes their required equality; pumping a separator destroys the required singleton separator; otherwise pumping down a nonempty middle-a part makes j<k, or pumping up a final-b part makes k>j. Every decomposition therefore fails for some pumping count, a contradiction. Thus C is not context-free.

# GATE 2019 CS: Q44 (printed CS Q34)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 11](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=11); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

## Solution

Turing machines and finite C programs admit finite-string encodings, hence both sets are countable. All languages over a nonempty finite alphabet form an uncountable power set. Regular languages are countable, so removing them still leaves uncountably many nonregular languages. Thus the uncountable sets requested are S3 and S4, B.

# GATE 2019 CS: Q58 (printed CS Q48)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 14](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=14); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 120 to 120

**Derived answer:** 120

## Solution

Use one state for each permutation of five elements, storing the accumulated composition. There are5!=120 states, start at the identity, and accept only identity. Reading a permutation composes it onto the stored one, so this DFA recognizes L. Every state is reachable by its corresponding one-letter input. For distinct permutations g, h, suffix g^(-1) makes g compose to identity but not h, proving those states distinguishable. Thus all120 states are necessary and sufficient.
