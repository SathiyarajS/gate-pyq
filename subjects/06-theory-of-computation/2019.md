**Question:** [Official archived paper page 5](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=5); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B under not-necessarily-regular interpretation

**Paraphrased problem context:** Given a regular language L over {a,b}, compare A L·L^R, where L^R is the set of reversed words; B {ww^R : w∈L}; C Prefix(L), all prefixes of words in L; D Suffix(L), all suffixes. The source asks which is “NOT regular”; evaluate this as which operation is not guaranteed to preserve regularity.

## Solution

Method: use closure constructions for A, C, D, and a counterexample for B.

1. Regular languages are closed under reversal and concatenation. Reversing an automaton's edges and exchanging start/final roles gives an NFA for L^R; concatenating automata then recognizes L·L^R. Thus A is always regular. Its two factors are chosen independently.
2. For Prefix(L), take a DFA for L and mark as accepting every state from which some original accepting state is reachable. A word reaches such a state exactly when it can be extended to a word of L. Thus C is regular.
3. For Suffix(L), use an NFA whose possible initial states are all states reachable from the original start, while keeping the original accepting states. It recognizes strings that can follow some prefix to complete a word of L. Thus D is regular.
4. B requires the second half to be the reversal of the very same first half. Choose regular L = {a,b}*. B then contains precisely the even-length palindromes. If it had pumping length p, the palindrome a^p bb a^p would have a nonempty pumped part wholly among its first p a's. Pumping that part down shortens only the left a-run, producing a non-palindrome. This contradicts the pumping lemma.

Result: B identifies the operation that does not preserve regularity. Literally B can be regular for finite or empty L, so the unconditional wording must mean not necessarily regular. The recorded regularity-quantifier discrepancy is retained.

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 6](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=6); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Paraphrased problem context:** Over {a,b}, L = {a^(2+3k) : k≥0} ∪ {b^(10+12k) : k≥0}. Which offered value can be a pumping length? Choices: A 3; B 5; C 9; D 24. The question asks for a valid length, not necessarily the smallest one.

## Solution

Method: use the actual pumping-lemma conditions. For every word s with |s|≥p, find s=xyz with |xy|≤p, |y|≥1, and xy^iz∈L for every integer i≥0.

1. Reject p=3, 5, or 9 using s=b^10, which has length at least each candidate. Any nonempty y in such a decomposition consists only of b's and has length r with 1≤r≤p≤9.
2. Pumping up once more, i=2, produces length 10+r. For this all-b word to remain in L, r would have to be a multiple of 12. No r from 1 through 9 qualifies. Thus each of those three candidates fails.
3. Now take p=24. If s=a^m∈L and m≥24, set x=ε and y=a³. Pumping changes m by a multiple of 3, preserving m≡2 mod 3. Even at i=0, m−3≥21, and the permitted residue makes the remaining exponent an admitted nonnegative case.
4. If s=b^m∈L and m≥24, set x=ε and y=b^12. The first eligible such m is 34. Pumping changes m by multiples of 12; removing one block leaves at least 22, still an admitted length 10+12k.
5. Both constructions have nonempty y and |xy| equal to 3 or 12, at most 24. They therefore satisfy all requirements for every sufficiently long word in either branch.

Result: D, 24. A pumping length is an upper threshold at which suitable repetition is guaranteed; it does not have to equal a shortest accepted word or the least possible threshold.

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 10](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=10); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** Over {a,b}, choose the language that is not context-free: A {ww^R}; B {wa^n b^n w^R}; C {wa^n w^R b^n}; D {a^n b^i : i is n, 3n, or 5n}. Here w ranges over all finite binary-alphabet strings, n≥0, and w^R is reversal.

## Solution

1. To distinguish these languages, separate nested matching, which a stack supports, from overlapping matching constraints. A is generated by S→aSa|bSb|ε, so it is context-free.
2. B is also nested: use S→aSa|bSb|T and T→aTb|ε. The S rules build w and its reversal around the central equal-length a/b runs. D is a union of three context-free languages; for each r in {1,3,5}, S_r→aS_r b^r|ε generates its fixed-ratio component. CFLs are closed under finite union.
3. For C, intersect with the regular language a+ b a+ b a+ b+. CFLs are closed under intersection with regular languages. In this pattern the two isolated b symbols force w=a^i b a^t, and n=k, giving precisely K={a^i b a^j b a^i b^k : i,k≥1, j≥k, j−k even}, with j=2t+k.
4. Suppose K were context-free with pumping length p. Choose s=a^p b a^p b a^p b^p. In any CFL pumping decomposition s=uvxyz, |vxy|≤p and |vy|>0. The window cannot touch both the first and third a-runs, nor both the middle a-run and final b-run.
5. If v or y contains a separator b, pumping down removes a required separator and destroys the regular pattern. Otherwise, pumping any first/third-run a changes one side of their required equality without changing the other. This also leaves K.
6. The remaining possibility pumps only middle-run a's or only final-run b's. Pumping down middle a's makes j<k; pumping up final b's makes k>j. Thus every decomposition fails for some pump count, contradicting the pumping lemma. K, and hence C, is not context-free. Answer C.

# GATE 2019 CS: Q44 (printed CS Q34)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 11](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=11); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** Which sets are uncountable? S1: recursively enumerable languages over {0,1}; S2: syntactically valid C programs; S3: all languages over {0,1}; S4: all nonregular languages over {0,1}. Options A S1/S2, B S3/S4, C S2/S3, D S1/S4.

## Solution

1. A set is countable if its members can be listed using natural-number indices. Finite strings over any finite alphabet can be listed by length and then lexicographic order, so their collection is countable.
2. Each Turing machine has a finite description. There are therefore only countably many such machines and only countably many languages they recognize. Every recursively enumerable language is recognized by a machine, so S1 is countable.
3. Each C program is also a finite string. Restricting the countable set of all strings to those obeying C syntax cannot make it uncountable, so S2 is countable.
4. The set {0,1}* of words is countably infinite, but a language is an arbitrary subset of that set. Its power set is uncountable by Cantor's theorem. Hence S3 is uncountable; confusing words with sets of words is the main trap.
5. Regular languages have finite automaton descriptions, so there are only countably many of them. If S4 were countable too, all languages would be a union of two countable sets, contradicting step 4. Thus S4 is uncountable and the answer is B.

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 14](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=14); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 120 to 120

**Derived answer:** 120

**Paraphrased problem context:** The alphabet Σ consists of all bijections of {1,...,5} to itself. A word x=x1...xn denotes the composition π(x)=x1∘...∘xn; the empty composition is identity. L contains words whose composition is identity. Find the number of states in a smallest DFA recognizing L.

## Solution

1. Each alphabet symbol is a permutation of five objects, so there are 5!=120 possible permutations. A prefix matters only through its accumulated composition, suggesting a state for each permutation.
2. Construct that DFA: start in the identity state; if the current state is g and the next symbol is σ, transition to g∘σ. Make identity the sole accepting state. Associativity ensures the stored state after x is exactly π(x), so this 120-state DFA recognizes L.
3. All 120 states are reachable: reading the single alphabet symbol g takes the identity start to g. Hence there are no unreachable states to discard.
4. To prove no states can be merged, take distinct permutations g and h. Append the one-letter suffix g^−1, which is available because inverses are also permutations in Σ. From g this produces g∘g^−1=id and accepts.
5. From h the same suffix produces h∘g^−1. It cannot equal id, since that would imply h=g after composing with g. Thus it rejects, so the two states are distinguishable.
6. Every pair is distinguishable, proving a lower bound of 120 states. Combined with the construction, the minimum is exactly 120. Merely counting symbols would not prove minimality without the reachability and distinguishing-suffix arguments.
