**Question:** [Official archived paper page 5](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=5); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** MTA

**Derived answer:** No correct option; official MTA

## Solution

Each offered expression fails. A accepts1111: choose111 for one repeated block and the final 1, producing an even number of ones. B always ends in 1 and misses the valid string10. C begins in 1 and misses01. D also misses01: zero repetitions begin with 1, while any positive repetition contributes at least two ones before the final 1. A correct expression is 0*(10*10*)*10*. The official key awards MTA, which is preserved rather than mapped to a letter.

# GATE 2020 CS: Q20 (printed CS Q10)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 6](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=6); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

## Solution

A DPDA pushes while reading a symbols, then pops one per b after the first b. It accepts an all-a input by final state at end-of-input, and accepts an a^n b^n input when the count matches, so the language is deterministic context-free. The standard strict-separation example a* union {a^n b^n} is not an LL(k) language for any finite k: arbitrarily long common a-prefixes postpone the distinction between an unmatched all-a ending and the matching b-suffix beyond any fixed lookahead. A predictive leftmost derivation cannot use that late distinction to discharge an unbounded number of already predicted matching b obligations. This is the language-level bounded-lookahead obstruction, not merely an observation that one displayed grammar is not left-factored. Thus I and III, C.

# GATE 2020 CS: Q42 (printed CS Q32)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 11](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=11); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

## Solution

For L1 choose x=ε, y=ε and let w be any binary string; hence L1=Σ*, regular. For L2 a CFG can guess a differing position in the two equal-length halves. Explicitly let U generate all odd-length strings with central a via U→a|aUa|aUb|bUa|bUb, and V generate those with central b by the analogous rules with base b. S→UV|VU then generates exactly even-length strings whose two halves differ somewhere. If component lengths are2i+1 and 2j+1, their differing central symbols are i+j+1 positions apart, exactly half the total length. Conversely any mismatch supplies such a split. Thus L2 is context-free and A holds.

# GATE 2020 CS: Q61 (printed CS Q51)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 15](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2020/cs_2020.pdf#page=15); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 6 to 6

**Derived answer:** 6

## Solution

Track the number of a symbols modulo6. On a increment the remainder, on b leave it unchanged, and accept remainders2 and 4; these are exactly even counts not divisible by 3. All six states are reachable. From remainder r, accepting suffix lengths modulo6 form {2-r, 4-r}. These six sets are distinct, so any pair of remainders is distinguished by some suffix a^k. Myhill-Nerode therefore requires all6 states, matching the construction.
