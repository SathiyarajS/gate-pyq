**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2015.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

Every CFL is decidable, so complement(L1) is recursive. The complement of nonrecursive L2 cannot be recursive, because recursive languages are closed under complement. CFLs are not closed under complement. Since complement(L1) is recursive and L2 is RE, their union is RE by dovetailing recognizers. Hence I and IV, D.

## Solution

The NPDA pushes a prefix, optionally consumes a middle symbol while changing phase, then pops only on the complementary symbol. The full listed strings have length 11, so the first five symbols10110 are pushed and the sixth0 is the middle. The remaining five must be the complement of the reversed prefix: reverse10110=01101, complement10010. Thus B.

A decider halts on every input, so exchanging accept and reject decides the complement. An NP computation has a finite polynomially bounded certificate/search tree, which a deterministic machine can exhaust, so every NP language is decidable. I and III hold, II fails: D.

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2015.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

A polynomial reduction Q1≤p3-SAT implies Q1∈NP, by composing the reduction with a 3-SAT verifier. The reduction3-SAT≤pQ2 makes Q2 NP-hard. Thus A is necessarily implied. The literal word consistent is weaker: taking Q1=Q2=3-SAT satisfies all four options simultaneously. The key A requires reading the question as asking what is guaranteed, not merely possible.

## Solution

Substitute X2=0X1+{epsilon} into X1=0X1+1X2 to obtain X1=(0+10)X1+1. Since the repeated-prefix language excludes epsilon, Arden’s lemma gives X1=(0+10)*1. Then X0=1X1=1(0+10)*1, C. The source’s X2 right side is 0X1, not0X2.
