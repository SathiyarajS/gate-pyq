## Solution

A generates exactly aⁿ for n≥1, and B exactly bⁿ for n≥1. The start production aaB therefore generates a²bⁿ, while Abb generates aⁿb². Taking the union yields {a²bⁿ: n≥1}∪{aⁿb²: n≥1}, option A. The grammar does not tie the two run lengths by a ratio or equality.

# GATE 2025 CS1: Q28

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 28](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS12025.pdf#page=28); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

## Solution

Subset construction produces a DFA whose states are subsets of the n NFA states; there are at most 2ⁿ such subsets, including the empty subset if needed. Therefore C is true and D, which asserts every accepting DFA exceeds that bound, is false. The given NFA is not stated to be minimal, so fewer-state NFAs or DFAs are possible (for example, add redundant states to a one-state accept-all automaton). Thus A and B are possibility statements, not promises of reduction for every NFA or for the n=1 boundary. D is the requested false statement.

# GATE 2025 CS1: Q44

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 44](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS12025.pdf#page=44); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

## Solution

L2 equals a(a|b)+a: every word in L2 starts and ends in a with a nonempty middle, and choosing α=a proves the reverse inclusion. Thus L2 is regular. Intersect L1 with the regular language {ab^r aa b^s: r, s≥1}. Each such word has exactly three a’s, so a repeated border α can contain only one a. Because it starts at the initial a and ends at the final b, it must be ab^s, with the suffix starting at the third a. This is a prefix exactly when r≥s. The remaining middle is b^(r−s)a, which is nonempty. Consequently the intersection is {ab^r aa b^s: r≥s≥1}. This is nonregular: for pumping length p take ab^p aa b^p. A pumpable substring within the first p symbols either contains the initial a, whose deletion destroys the pattern, or consists of at least one b, whose deletion makes r<s. Pumping down contradicts regularity in every permitted decomposition. Closure under intersection therefore proves L1 nonregular. Answer C.

# GATE 2025 CS1: Q45

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 45](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS12025.pdf#page=45); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

## Solution

L2 is generated by S→aSc | aTc and T→bTc | bc: each a or b contributes one trailing c, with at least one of each. For L1 assume CFL pumping length p and take a^p b^p c^(p+1). A pumping window of length at most p cannot meet both a and c blocks. If the pumped pieces change a and b unequally, their required equality fails. If they change both equally by a positive amount, pumping up leaves the c count p+1 but makes m at least p+1, violating c>m. If only c changes, pumping down leaves at most p c’s, again violating c>m. Any b/c change involving b breaks a=b; changes disrupting block order already fail. These exhaust cases, contradicting CFL pumping. Thus only L2 is context-free, C.

**Official answer:** C

**Derived answer:** C

## Solution

Read the four states left to right as matching the longest suffix that is a prefix of bab: ε, b, ba, bab. From ε, a stays and b advances; from b, b stays and a advances; from ba, a resets and b reaches the final state; from bab, a returns to ba and b to b. Thus the only accepting state means the current word ends in bab, C. Containing bab is not sufficient (baba is rejected), and neither even-b parity nor avoiding aba characterizes acceptance. # GATE 2025 CS2: Q24

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

## Solution

A DPDA can simulate any DFA using its finite control and leaving its stack unused, so every regular language is accepted by some DPDA. Deterministic context-free languages are a proper subset of all context-free languages; nondeterministic PDAs recognize the latter larger class. Decidable languages are larger still. Thus A alone is guaranteed.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q25

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 21](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS22025.pdf#page=21); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

## Solution

CFG emptiness is decidable: mark nonterminals that can derive terminal-only strings, iteratively add a left-hand side when every nonterminal on its right-hand side is marked, and test whether the start symbol becomes marked. This finite fixed point terminates. General CFG equivalence and emptiness of the intersection of two CFLs are undecidable. Equality to a supplied regular language is also undecidable in general, since taking that language as Σ* asks the undecidable CFG universality question. Thus D.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q30

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 24](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS22025.pdf#page=24); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B;C

**Derived answer:** B;C

## Solution

CFLs are closed under union but not under complement. Recursive/decidable languages are closed under union and intersection by running their halting deciders and combining answers, and under complement by reversing acceptance. Regular languages are closed under union, intersection, and complement through finite-automaton constructions. Both B and C use valid matches. A wrongly denies complement closure to recursive languages; D wrongly denies it to regular languages.

**Derived answer:** A;C

## Solution

A is a*b*, hence regular. In B the intersection forbids c, forcing m−n=0 and leaving{a^n b^n}, nonregular. C is recognized by a 63-state product counter tracking a-count modulo7 and b-count modulo9, accepting residue(2, 3). For D intersect with a*b* to obtain{a^k b^k: k≡2 mod7}. This remains nonregular: choose a permitted k above a pumping length and pump within the initial a-block, destroying equality. Thus A, C.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q60

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 45](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS22025.pdf#page=45); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 6 to 6

**Derived answer:** 6

## Solution

No input symbol is divisible by 7, so products stay in the six nonzero residues. A six-state DFA stores the current product, starts at 1, multiplies by each input modulo7, and accepts2. All six states are reachable because powers of 3 cycle through3, 2, 6, 4, 5, 1. Distinct residues r, s are distinguishable: append a string with product2r^(−1) mod7, realizable by a power of 3. It takes r to 2 but cannot take s to 2. Thus all six states are necessary and sufficient.
