## Solution

CFLs are closed under union and intersection with regular languages. Hence L1−R=L1∩complement(R) is context-free because regular languages are complement-closed. CFLs are not generally closed under complement or intersection with another CFL. Thus I and III, B.

## Solution

The language requires its third symbol to be a and at least three symbols afterward. Use three prefix-count states before that required symbol, four states counting0, 1, 2, at least3 symbols after it, plus a dead state for third-symbol b. This gives 8. The seven nondead states have different shortest accepting suffix lengths6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0, so they are pairwise distinguishable; the dead state has no accepting continuation. Thus8 is minimal.

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 15](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2-2017.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

I is false: unary prime lengths are not context-free. A CFL pumping decomposition of a^q for prime q above the pumping length adds some positive d when pumped; pumping q extra copies makes length q(1+d), composite. II is false because L2=a*{b^m c^(2m)} is context-free. III is true: L3 is decidable by counting, but taking inverse homomorphism h(a)=a, h(b)=b, h(c)=cc gives the non-CFL{a^n b^n c^n}, proving L3 non-context-free. IV is true by a DPDA that pushes on a and pops on b. Hence III and IV, D.

**Derived answer:** 4

**Source access:** Archived July2017 original official paper and key. Page numbers refer to the paper PDF; key is a separate two-page PDF.

## Solution

The regex accepts exactly words whose penultimate symbol is b. Store the last two symbols, padding a short prefix on the left with a. The four pair states aa, ab, ba, bb suffice, accepting ba and bb. They are distinguishable: a difference in the first stored symbol is detected by the empty suffix, and a difference in the second by appending a. Thus four states are necessary and sufficient.

## Solution

Every production preserves #a≥#b: SaS adds an a, aSb and bSa add one of each, SS adds the counts, and epsilon starts at zero. Thus babba, with two a and three b, is impossible. The other words are derivable: aabb via S→aSb→aaSbb→aabb; aaab as a(aa)b; abbaa as ab·ba·a, using SS, the pair productions and SaS. Hence D.

**Derived answer:** A

**Source access:** Archived July2017 original official paper and key. Page numbers refer to the paper PDF; key is a separate two-page PDF.

## Solution

Each language is context-free: one matches the a and b counts while allowing an arbitrary trailing c block, the other matches b and c with arbitrary initial a. CFL closure under union proves I. Their intersection forces all three counts equal, giving {a^n b^n c^n}, not context-free by the pumping lemma: a short pump window touches at most two blocks, leaving the third count unchanged. Thus I only, A.

# GATE 2017 CS1: Q49 (printed CS Q39)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 17](https://web.archive.org/web/20170712112248id_/http://www.gate.iitg.ac.in:80/2017questions/CS1.pdf#page=17); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A and B both valid for total f

**Source access:** Archived July2017 original official paper and key. Page numbers refer to the paper PDF; key is a separate two-page PDF.

## Solution

If f is computable, decide its graph by parsing x#y, computing f(x), and comparing with y; hence the graph is recursive and therefore RE. Conversely, given an RE recognizer for the graph and an input x, enumerate all y in B* and dovetail the recognizer on x#y. Totality guarantees exactly one y will be accepted, and outputting it computes f(x). A recursive graph is a special case. Therefore both biconditionals A and B are true, although the official single-choice key gives only A.
