**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** D

## Solution

Using a preceding result can cause a data hazard; a conditional branch causes a control hazard; simultaneous demand for the single ALU causes a structural hazard. Therefore all three can cause hazards, D. The question asks possibility, not that every instance necessarily stalls.

# GATE 2003: Q43 (printed CS Q43)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005203452/GATE2003.pdf#page=6), physical page 6. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C

## Solution

There are six exponent bits and nine fractional mantissa bits. Exponent 63 is reserved for zero, so the largest usable exponent is e=62. For fixed e, adjacent significands differ by 2⁻⁹, giving spacing 2^(e−31−9). This is largest at e=62, namely 2²², option C. Crossing a binade boundary has the smaller binade’s spacing, and the gaps adjacent to zero are only 2⁻³¹, so neither produces a larger gap.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** A

## Solution

The gates feeding the B inputs are XOR gates. For an n-bit word the circuit computes A+(B XOR K…K)+C₀ modulo 2ⁿ. Setting (K,C₀)=(0,0) gives A+B; setting (1,1) gives A+¬B+1=A−B. Neither setting can remove arbitrary B to give A+1 solely by changing those two control lines. Therefore A+B and A−B, but not general A+1, is option A.

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** B

## Solution

The counter C starts at eight and the loop performs exactly eight one-bit right rotations through carry. The carry tested immediately after the kth rotation is the kth original low-to-high bit of A. Newly inserted carry bits cannot reach the outgoing low position during these eight iterations. B starts at zero and is incremented exactly when the outgoing bit is one, so B is the population count of the original A, option B.

**Independently derived answer:** A under the stated instruction effects

## Solution

Treat carry and A together as a nine-bit circular register and use the instruction effects stated for this hypothetical processor. Eight right rotations leave one right rotation short of a full nine-bit cycle. Inserting RRC A,#1 at X therefore restores A, irrespective of the initial carry value, giving A. This reasoning uses carry preservation by instructions whose listed effects do not change carry; importing different flag side effects from a real ISA would require a new analysis.

# GATE 2003: Q78 (printed CS Q78)

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005203452/GATE2003.pdf#page=11), physical page 11. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** None

## Solution

If every physical memory reference, including page-table reads, uses the stated cache with 90% hit probability, its mean service time is H=1+0.10×10=2 ns, charging the cache probe before a miss fetch. A lookup always costs 1 ns for the TLB and one data reference; a TLB miss, probability 0.04, adds two page-table references. Thus E=1+(1+2×0.04)H=3.16 ns, which rounds to 3.0 ns, C. If the two page-table references instead bypass the cache and each costs 10 ns, E=1+0.04×20+H=3.8 ns, rounding to 4.0 ns, D. The statement that page tables reside in main memory does not itself specify whether their reads are cached or share the data-cache hit rate. C is the usual all-physical-accesses-cached interpretation, but an unconditional answer is withheld.
