**Essential problem context (paraphrased):** Compare latency T1 for one instruction on a pipelined CPU with T2 on an otherwise identical nonpipelined CPU. Options: T1≤T2,T1≥T2,T1<T2,or T1=T2 plus one fetch cycle.

## Solution

Step 1: Pipelining overlaps different instructions; one instruction still passes through all of its stages. If stage delays are d1,…,dk, the unpipelined combinational work is their sum.

Step 2: A clocked pipeline needs roughly k·max(di), plus register overhead, which is at least sum(di). Perfectly balanced ideal stages can match the unpipelined latency; real pipeline registers usually increase it.

Thus T1≥T2, B, in this standard comparison. Throughput improvement across many instructions must not be confused with reducing one instruction’s latency; no universal extra-fetch-cycle formula follows.

Task: For (B,C)=(44,25) and (33,46), find D,E at S4.

## Solution

## Solution

**Essential problem context (paraphrased):** An ideal five-stage pipeline has stage time 2 ns; every instruction uses all five stages. For a nonoverlapped branch, the following instruction is not fetched until the branch completes. Branches are 20% of all executed instructions. Compute steady-state average time, ignoring finite-stream fill/drain overhead.

Task: Find average instruction execution time when every branch prevents overlap.

## Solution

1. Without branches, the pipeline accepts a new instruction every cycle, hence steady-state throughput time is 2 ns per instruction, not the individual instruction latency of 10 ns.

2. A branch fetched in cycle t completes at the end of t+4. The successor can be fetched in t+5, whereas normal overlap would fetch it in t+1. Thus each such branch adds four idle fetch cycles, or 8 ns.

3. The expected cycles per instruction are 1+0.20×4=1.8. Multiplying by 2 ns gives 3.6 ns per instruction.

4. Equivalently, 80% of inter-fetch intervals are 2 ns and 20% are 10 ns, so 0.8×2+0.2×10=3.6 ns. Finite programs have fill/drain effects; the question’s average uses the usual long-run idealization.

4. The 50% figure applies only to conditional branches, not to every branch. Ignoring the unconditional 20% of branches would incorrectly reduce the answer to 2.64 ns. No branch prediction or extra recovery penalty is introduced beyond the assumptions given.
