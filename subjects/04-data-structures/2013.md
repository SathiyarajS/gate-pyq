## Solution

In an ordinary unbalanced binary search tree, increasing insertions can create a chain of n nodes. Finding the next insertion position then takes a linear search down that chain. Worst-case insertion is O(n), C, not the logarithmic bound of balanced trees.

# GATE 2013 CS: Q53 (printed CS Q43)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 10](https://web.archive.org/web/20130418030400id_/http://www.gate.iitb.ac.in:80/gate2013/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/CS-A.pdf#page=10); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

**Source access:** Archived official IIT Bombay booklet A. Physical PDF page numbers are used; ORS code A is the matching key column. Booklets B/C/D permute the same questions, not separate sessions.

## Solution

The root is 30. Its preorder left-subtree segment is 20, 10, 15, 25, 23 and its right-subtree segment is 39, 35, 42. Under 20, node 10 has right child 15, and node 25 has left child 23; their postorders are 15, 10 and 23, 25, giving the full left-subtree postorder 15, 10, 23, 25, 20. Under 39 are children 35 and 42, so its postorder is 35, 42, 39. Appending the root gives 15, 10, 23, 25, 20, 35, 42, 39, 30, option D.
