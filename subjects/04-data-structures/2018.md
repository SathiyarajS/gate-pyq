**Derived answer:** B

**Paraphrased problem context:** A noncircular singly linked queue stores head and tail pointers. Enqueue inserts at head; dequeue removes tail. For n nodes, choose the optimal worst-case time pair: A Θ(1),Θ(1); B Θ(1),Θ(n); C Θ(n),Θ(1); D Θ(n),Θ(n).

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

Enqueue sets new.next=head and head=new, also initializing tail if previously empty. Tail removal must change the predecessor’s next pointer to null and make it the new tail. A tail pointer identifies the node to remove but not its predecessor, because links point only forward. Scanning from head is unavoidable with only these stored pointers. Reversing the operation orientation would allow constant-time deletion at head, but that is not this question’s data structure.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

Inserting at the head updates a constant number of pointers, Θ(1). Removing the tail requires locating its predecessor, which a singly linked list with only head/tail pointers does not directly supply; traversing n nodes costsΘ(n) in the worst case. Thus B.

**Derived answer:** 4

**Paraphrased problem context:** Postorder is 8,9,6,7,4,5,2,3,1 and inorder is 8,6,9,4,7,2,5,1,3. Height counts edges on a longest root-to-leaf path. Find the height.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

Postorder ends with the root, and inorder splits each subtree into left part, root, right part. Root 1 splits off right leaf 3. Its left subtree ends in 2, whose inorder split leaves right leaf 5 and left subtree rooted at 4. Continuing gives 4 with children 6 and 7, and 6 with children 8 and 9. There are five vertices but four edges on the longest path, so do not report the number of levels.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

The final postorder element gives root1, with right child3 and left subtree rooted2. In that left subtree, 5 is the right child, while4 is the left child of 2; 4 has left child6 and right child7, and 6 has children8, 9. A longest path is 1→2→4→6→8, four edges. Thus height4.

# GATE 2018 CS: Q56 (printed CS Q46)

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 19](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 80 to 80

**Derived answer:** 80

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2018.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

**Paraphrased problem context:** How many distinct min-heaps use each key 1,2,3,4,5,6,7 once? Use the standard complete binary heap shape.

## Solution

Method and intermediate reasoning:

The shape is fixed: a root with two three-node subtrees. Minimum key 1 must occupy the root. Select a three-element subset of the remaining six keys for the left subtree; the right gets the complement, giving C(6,3)=20 distinct assignments. Within each subtree its least key must be its root, but its two leaves can be swapped independently. Left and right positions are distinguished in a heap, so do not divide the count by 2 for mirror images.

Derivation, answer and qualifications:

The minimum1 must be the root. The complete seven-node shape has three positions in each subtree. Choose which three of the other six keys go left in C(6, 3)=20 ways. In either three-node subtree its smallest key is the root and its two leaves can be ordered in 2 ways. Thus there are20*2*2=80 heaps.
