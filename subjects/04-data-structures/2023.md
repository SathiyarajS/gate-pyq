## Solution

# GATE 2023 CS: Q13

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 10](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2023/cs_2023.pdf#page=10); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

## Solution

Deleting an arbitrary node of a singly linked list requires locating its predecessor to reconnect the list. In the worst case the target is the tail and this takes a traversal, Θ(n); copying the next node’s value is not a universal solution for the tail. A doubly linked node directly exposes its predecessor and successor, so a constant number of links and possibly the head pointer suffice, Θ(1). Answer D.

# GATE 2023 CS: Q46

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 49](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2023/cs_2023.pdf#page=49); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

## Solution

A binary heap has heightΘ(log n). Extract-Max removes the root, moves the final element there, and can sift it down the full height. Insertion appends a leaf and can sift it up the full height. Both therefore take O(log n) worst-case time, option B, under the ordinary heap-storage model without charging an occasional external dynamic-array resize.

# GATE 2023 CS: Q59

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 62](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2023/cs_2023.pdf#page=62); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 8 to 8

**Derived answer:** 8

## Solution

Write the stack from bottom to top. After the initial pushes it is [1, 5, 7, 8, 9, 2], and the queue front-to-back is [1, 5, 7, 8, 9, 2]. The two pops remove 2 and 9; the two dequeues remove 1 and 5. The stack is then [1, 5, 7, 8] and the queue [7, 8, 9, 2]. Operation VII and its three repetitions append 7, 8, 9, 2 to the stack. The final two pops remove 2 and 9, leaving top element 8.
