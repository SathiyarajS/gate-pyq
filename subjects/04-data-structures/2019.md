**Question:** [Official archived paper page 13](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=13); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A under distinct-key comparison model

**Paraphrased problem context:** Test max-heap statements: I the smallest element is always at a leaf; II the second largest is always a root child; III a max-heap can be built from a BST in Θ(n); IV a BST can be built from a max-heap in Θ(n). Options A I/II/III, B I/II/IV, C I/III/IV, D II/III/IV.

## Solution

1. Under the intended distinct-key model, a max-heap parent is larger than its children. If the smallest key were internal, one of its children would be smaller still, a contradiction. Hence I holds.
2. The largest key is at the root. If the second largest were below depth one, its nonroot parent would have to be larger, producing a third key above it in rank. Thus it must be one of the root's children and II holds.
3. To establish III, traverse the BST once and copy its n keys into an array, costing Θ(n). Then run bottom-up heap construction, also Θ(n), by sifting down internal nodes. Sorting first is not required.
4. IV is false in the general comparison model. If it were possible, arbitrary distinct keys could be heapified in Θ(n), converted to a BST in Θ(n), and output in sorted order by an inorder traversal in Θ(n). That would contradict the Ω(n log n) comparison-sorting lower bound.
5. Therefore the intended true statements are I, II, III: option A. The qualification about distinct keys is material. With repeated minimum values, a minimum can occur at an internal node as well as a leaf, so the literal word 'always' in I is not valid for unrestricted duplicate-key heaps. Specialized bounded-integer models also cannot be silently substituted for comparison complexity.

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 14](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2019/cs_2019.pdf#page=14); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 4.25 to 4.25

**Derived answer:** 4.25

**Paraphrased problem context:** A binary tree T has 8 leaves and every level is full (a perfect tree). Two leaves a,b are selected uniformly and independently. Find the expected number of edges on their unique connecting path, to two decimal places.

## Solution

1. A perfect binary tree with eight leaves has height 3 because 8=2³. All leaves are at depth 3, with the root at depth zero.
2. By symmetry, fix the first chosen leaf a. Independence means b remains uniformly distributed among all eight leaves, including a itself.
3. If b=a, there is one choice and distance 0. Its sibling is one choice at distance 2, via their parent. The two leaves in the other child subtree of the same depth-one ancestor have distance 4.
4. The remaining four leaves lie across the root, so the path goes up three edges and down three: distance 6. The counts 1+1+2+4=8 exhaust all equally likely outcomes.
5. Therefore E[d]=(1×0+1×2+2×4+4×6)/8=34/8=4.25.
6. The zero-distance case is required by independent sampling. Sampling two distinct leaves would instead condition on b≠a and produce 34/7, a different answer. Also, the question's parenthetical definition fixes a perfect tree; the broader textbook meaning 'every internal node has two children' alone would not determine these distances.
