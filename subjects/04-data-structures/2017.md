**Question:** [Official archived paper page 5](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2-2017.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

For constant-time queue operations the links run forward from FRONT through successive elements to REAR, with REAR.next=FRONT. Insert after REAR and advance REAR; delete FRONT and update REAR.next. The reverse orientation would require predecessor traversal for front deletion. Thus II only, B, as a general structural condition rather than a special two-node coincidence.

## Solution

Under the convention that order m means the maximum number of children of an internal node, it holds m pointers and m−1 separator keys. The size condition is 2m+8(m−1)≤512, giving10m≤520 and m≤52. At52 the node fits exactly. This ignores unspecified node metadata and follows the standard internal-node order convention.

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 4](https://web.archive.org/web/20170712112248id_/http://www.gate.iitg.ac.in:80/2017questions/CS1.pdf#page=4); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

**Source access:** Archived July2017 original official paper and key. Page numbers refer to the paper PDF; key is a separate two-page PDF.

## Solution

A binary tree of edge-height h has at most 2^(h+1)−1 nodes. Fifteen nodes therefore require h≥3, attained by a perfect BST. A chain of 15 nodes has height14, the largest possible. Thus B.
