**Paraphrased problem context:** Find the printed integer. C code: void f1(int a,int b){int c;c=a;a=b;b=c;} void f2(int *a,int *b){int c;c=*a;*a=*b;*b=c;} int main(){int a=4,b=5,c=6;f1(a,b);f2(&b,&c);printf("%d",c-a-b);}. Assume required declarations.

## Solution

At f1 entry the parameters copy the caller's 4 and 5; swapping these copies leaves main's a,b,c at 4,5,6. In f2, the pointer parameters are also copied, but dereferencing them reaches main's b and c. Saving *a before overwriting prevents losing the original b. After the swap main has 4,6,5; subtraction is left-associative, so (5−4)−6=−5.

Baseline derivation and retained qualifications: The value-parameter swap f1 changes only its local copies. The pointer-based f2 swaps caller b and c, giving a=4, b=6, c=5. Therefore c−a−b=−5, with routine declarations assumed.

**Paraphrased problem context:** For positive integers x,y: q:=0; r:=x; while r≥y do {r:=r−y; q:=q+1}. Which postcondition holds? A r=qx+y and r<y; B x=qy+r and r<y; C y=qx+r and 0<r<y; D q+1<r−y and y>0.

## Solution

Prove the invariant x=qy+r by initialization and preservation: (q+1)y+(r−y)=qy+r. The guard ensures subtracting y never makes r negative. Since positive y decreases r at every step, the loop terminates and the false guard gives r<y. Therefore q is the integer quotient and r the remainder with 0≤r<y. A remainder may be zero, so a strict lower bound such as that in C would be wrong.

Baseline derivation and retained qualifications: Initially q=0, r=x, so x=qy+r. Each iteration replaces r by r−y and q by q+1, preserving that invariant. Positive y decreases nonnegative r on every iteration; termination gives 0≤r<y. Hence the listed postcondition x=qy+r and r<y is B; the full invariant also supplies the nonnegative lower bound.
