**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C

**Paraphrased problem context:** Which is true for every planar graph on n vertices?

(A) It is connected
(B) It is Eulerian
(C) It has a vertex cover of size at most 3n/4
(D) It has an independent set of size at least n/3

## Solution

1. By the four-color theorem, a simple loopless planar graph has an independent color class of size at least n/4. Its complement covers every edge and has size at most 3n/4, proving C.
2. A planar graph need not be connected or Eulerian. K4 is planar but has maximum independent-set size 1<4/3, refuting D.
3. The standard loopless graph convention is needed for the coloring argument.

**Paraphrased problem context:** For positive integer k, P is the sum of odd i with 1≤i≤2k, and Q is the sum of even i in the same range. Which relation holds?

(A) P=Q−k
(B) P=Q+k
(C) P=Q
(D) P=Q+2k

## Solution

1. Write the odd terms as 2j−1 and their paired even terms as 2j, for j=1,…,k.
2. Each even term exceeds its odd partner by exactly one, so Q−P=Σ1=k.
3. Rearrange to P=Q−k, A. Equivalently P=k² and Q=k(k+1).

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C under the Markov interpretation

**Paraphrased problem context:** Aishwarya studies either CS or mathematics each day. After a CS day, the probability of mathematics next day is 0.6; after a mathematics day, the probability of CS next day is 0.4. Given CS on Monday, what is the probability of CS on Wednesday?

(A) 0.24
(B) 0.36
(C) 0.4
(D) 0.6

## Solution

1. Interpret the given next-day probabilities as the transition probabilities of a two-state Markov chain. After CS, the chance of CS is 1−0.6=0.4.
2. Wednesday CS can arise through CS→CS→CS with probability 0.4×0.4=0.16 or CS→Math→CS with probability 0.6×0.4=0.24.
3. These cases are disjoint; add them to get 0.40, C. Without the usual Markov interpretation, unconditional pairwise information alone would not justify the two-step conditional calculation.

(A) I and II only

3. In IV, the first two terms again combine to P, but P∨(¬P∧Q)=P∨Q. At P=false,Q=true, this differs from I. Thus exactly I,II,III agree, B.

**Paraphrased problem context:** A graph G has n vertices and 2n−2 edges partitionable into two edge-disjoint spanning trees. Which statement is NOT true?

(A) Every induced subgraph on k vertices has at most 2k−2 edges
(B) The minimum cut has at least two edges
(C) Every pair of vertices has two edge-disjoint paths
(D) Every pair of vertices has two internally vertex-disjoint paths

## Solution

1. The claim about two internally vertex-disjoint paths is not guaranteed. Take two copies of K4 sharing exactly one vertex: n=7 and m=12=2n−2. Each K4 decomposes into two spanning trees (for example a path on its four vertices and its complementary path); joining corresponding trees at the shared vertex gives two edge-disjoint spanning trees of the whole graph.
2. The common vertex is nevertheless an articulation vertex, so paths between the two sides must share it. A is true because each tree induces a forest with at most k−1 edges on a nonempty k-vertex set. B is true since each spanning tree contributes a distinct edge across every nontrivial cut.
3. C follows by taking the unique path in each of the two edge-disjoint trees.

**Paraphrased problem context:** Let xₙ count binary strings of length n containing no consecutive zeros. Which recurrence does it satisfy?

(A) xₙ=2xₙ₋₁
(B) xₙ=x⌊ₙ/₂⌋+1
(C) xₙ=x⌊ₙ/₂⌋+n

## Solution

1. Divide valid strings of length n≥2 by their first bit. A leading 1 can be followed by any valid length-(n−1) string.

3. Add their counts: xₙ=xₙ₋₁+xₙ₋₂, D. Initial values are x₀=1 and x₁=2, which are essential for numerical evaluation.

3. Do not use Fibonacci seeds 1,1, which count the wrong objects.
