**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** C

## Solution

By the four-color theorem, a simple loopless planar graph has an independent color class of size at least n/4. Its complement covers every edge and has size at most3n/4, proving C. A planar graph need not be connected or Eulerian. K4 is planar but has maximum independent-set size1<4/3, refuting D. The standard loopless graph convention is needed for the coloring argument.

# GATE 2008: Q42 (printed CS Q42)

**Question:** [Official-source archive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1popAHO-cX69oQEx_tD0wZXL1tTrBljeO/view), member CS/CS2008.pdf, physical page 7. Consult original wording and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** D

## Solution

The claim about two internally vertex-disjoint paths is not guaranteed. Take two copies of K4 sharing exactly one vertex: n=7 and m=12=2n−2. Each K4 decomposes into two spanning trees (for example a path on its four vertices and its complementary path); joining corresponding trees at the shared vertex gives two edge-disjoint spanning trees of the whole graph. The common vertex is nevertheless an articulation vertex, so paths between the two sides must share it. A is true because each tree induces a forest with at most k−1 edges on a nonempty k-vertex set. B is true since each spanning tree contributes a distinct edge across every nontrivial cut. C follows by taking the unique path in each of the two edge-disjoint trees.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** D

## Solution

Partition valid length-n strings by their beginning. Those beginning with 1 can be followed by any valid length-(n−1) string; those beginning with 0 must begin 01 and then have any valid length-(n−2) suffix. The classes are disjoint and exhaustive, giving xn=x(n−1)+x(n−2), option D, with x0=1 and x1=2.
