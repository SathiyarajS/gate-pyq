**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** D only if the empty sample is assigned value0

## Solution

The event that2^i is the minimum requires excluding the i−1 smaller values and including2^i, with probability2^-i. Summing2^i×2^-i over i=1,...,n gives n. But the empty sample occurs with probability2^-n and has no smallest element. Thus the expectation as literally stated is undefined. Setting the empty-sample value to0 gives n, D; conditioning on a nonempty sample instead gives n/(1−2^-n). No official answer is inferred.

## Solution

For a vertex S with |S|=k, choose the two intersection elements from S and any subset outside S, giving C(k,2)·2^(n−k) candidate neighbors. When k=2 subtract one to exclude S itself; for k=0,1 degree is zero. For k≥3, the ratio of successive degrees is (k+1)/(2(k−1)), equal to 1 at k=3 and less than 1 for k>3. The maximum is therefore attained at k=3 and k=4, with degree 3·2^(n−3). This exceeds the k=2 degree 2^(n−2)−1. Thus C.
