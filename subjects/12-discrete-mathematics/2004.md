**Independently derived answer:** A

## Solution

Treat each nonzero entry as an edge from its row vertex to its column vertex. The stated a rows together with b columns cover all such edges. Any selection with no two entries in the same row or column is a matching. Each chosen entry must use at least one of the a+b covering vertices, and no covering vertex can serve two chosen entries. Consequently the selection has at most a+b entries, A. The bound can be attained with edges using distinct other endpoints; it need not equal the actual maximum.

## Solution

Name outer vertices T,R,B,L and inner square vertices u (upper-left), v (upper-right), w (lower-right), z (lower-left). The diagram has outer cycle T-R-B-L-T, inner cycle u-v-w-z-u, and edges T-u,T-v,R-v,R-w,B-w,B-z,L-z,L-u. Suppose three colours suffice. Triangle Tuv can be coloured T=3,u=1,v=2. Triangle Tuz is not present, but L is adjacent to T and u, forcing L=2. Since z is adjacent to L and u, z=3. Since w is adjacent to z and v, w=1. B, adjacent to z and w, must then be 2, contradicting edge B-L. Four colours suffice: T=B=1, L=R=2, u=w=3, v=z=4. Therefore the chromatic number is 4, C.
