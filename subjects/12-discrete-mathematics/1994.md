**Essential problem context (paraphrased):** For arbitrary events A,B, choose the universal statement: product formula for intersection, equality of union to the sum, a multiplied conditional formula, or P(A∪B)≤P(A)+P(B).

## Solution

Inclusion–exclusion gives P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B). The intersection probability is nonnegative, so the union is at most the sum. Equality needs disjointness, which is not stated. The product formula for the intersection needs independence. Conditional probability, when P(B)>0, is P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B), not multiplication by P(B). Thus D is the only unconditional assertion among the listed alternatives.

The question asks for an example, not a unique familiar arithmetic operation.

**Question:** [Third-party reproduction](https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/cdn-uploads/20211005204113/GATE1994.pdf#page=6), physical page 6. Consult the reproduced wording and choices. Original official provenance and completeness have not been established.

**Type / marks:** descriptive, 5 mark(s).

**Official answer:** Not available; no official key located.

**Independently derived answer:** Yes, multiplication is commutative.

**Essential problem context (paraphrased):** Every element a of a ring (R,+,∘) satisfies a∘a=a. Decide whether the ring is commutative.

## Solution

1. Write multiplication by juxtaposition. Apply idempotence to a+a: (a+a)²=a+a. Distributivity and a²=a give 4a=2a, hence 2a=0 by additive cancellation. Thus −a=a for every a.

2. For arbitrary a,b, idempotence of a+b gives (a+b)²=a+b. Expanding yields a²+ab+ba+b²=a+b. Cancel a²=a and b²=b to get ab+ba=0.

3. Since every element is its own additive inverse, −ba=ba. Therefore ab=−ba=ba. No multiplicative identity is needed.
