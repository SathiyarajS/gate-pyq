

# GATE 2025 CS1: Q17

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 17](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS12025.pdf#page=17); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** D

**Derived answer:** D

## Solution

A counterexample shows g need be neither injective nor surjective. Let A={1, 2}, B={u, v}, C={w}; send both elements of A to u under g, and send both u and v to w under f. Then f and f∘g are onto C, but g misses v and maps two inputs to u. Thus no injectivity or surjectivity requirement follows for g, and D is correct.

# GATE 2025 CS1: Q30

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 30](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS12025.pdf#page=30); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** NAT, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** 195 to 195

**Derived answer:** 195

## Solution

Count the complement. There are 3⁵=243 length-five ternary strings. To avoid equal adjacent symbols, choose the first symbol in 3 ways and each later symbol in 2 ways, yielding 3×2⁴=48. Thus at least one aa, bb, or cc occurs in 243−48=195 strings. This complement method automatically avoids overcounting strings with several repeated adjacent pairs.

# GATE 2025 CS1: Q48

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 48](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS12025.pdf#page=48); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B;D

**Derived answer:** B;D

## Solution

Both B and D require a mother y for every x, then exclude any different z from also being a mother of x. B says every unequal z is not a mother; D equivalently says no unequal mother exists. A merely requires one mother and one nonmother, allowing multiple mothers. C is satisfied by taking z=y whenever its antecedent holds, and is also vacuous if no mother exists. Therefore B and D alone encode exactly one.

# GATE 2025 CS1: Q49

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 49](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS12025.pdf#page=49); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B

**Derived answer:** B

## Solution

Pointwise addition is closed on functions from non-negative integers to themselves, associative and commutative. The function identically zero is an identity. The constant-one function has no additive inverse in this set, since an inverse would have to be constantly −1. Hence this is an Abelian monoid, not a group: B.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q15

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 13](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS22025.pdf#page=13); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MCQ, 1 mark(s).

**Official answer:** A

**Derived answer:** A

## Solution

A is induction over the nonnegative integers: establish P(0), then propagate P(x) to P(x+1), proving every P(n). A downward step from 0 cannot establish positive cases. Starting at 1000 and moving down cannot establish values above1000, while moving up cannot establish values below1000. The options implicitly include0 in the natural numbers; expressions x−1 at 0 require a domain convention, but do not provide the missing upward/downward coverage. Answer A.

# GATE 2025 CS2: Q42

**Question:** [Official archived paper page 31](https://gate2026.iitg.ac.in/doc/download/2025/CS22025.pdf#page=31); consult original wording, diagrams and choices.

**Type / marks:** MSQ, 2 mark(s).

**Official answer:** B;C

**Derived answer:** B;C

## Solution

Reflexivity, antisymmetry and transitivity hold coordinatewise, so this is a partial order. For any f, g the pointwise minimum is their greatest lower bound and the pointwise maximum their least upper bound. Thus it is a lattice. For n≥1, the zero function is below the one function but not conversely, so the relation is not symmetric or an equivalence relation. Select B, C. The question conventionally assumes positive n; the empty-domain n=0 case would collapse to a singleton.
