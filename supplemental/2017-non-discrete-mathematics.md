## Solution

f′(x)=(Rπ/2)cos(πx/2). At x=1/2 this is Rπ√2/4=√2, so R=4/π. The integral from 0 to 1 is 2R/π+S; equating it to 2R/π gives S=0. Thus C.

## Solution

For real symmetric A, the sum of squared entries equals the sum of squared eigenvalues. Rank2 leaves exactly two nonzero eigenvalues λ1,λ2 with λ1²+λ2²=50. At least one has absolute value≤5, proving I even when n=2. II fails for diag(5, 5), whose largest eigenvalue magnitude is exactly5. Hence I only, B.
