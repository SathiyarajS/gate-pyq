**Source access:** Open archive member CS/CS2014.pdf inside the linked official CS.zip. Page numbers refer to the extracted PDF, not the Drive preview.

## Solution

For A, define h(y)=f(y)−f(y+1). It is continuous on[0, 1], with h(0)=−2 and h(1)=2, so an interior zero exists. But D also follows: g(y)=f(y)+f(2−y) is continuous, g(0)=−2 and g(1)=2, so an interior zero gives f(y)=−f(2−y). Thus both A and D are necessarily true although the official single-choice key is A. This is a literal nonunique-answer discrepancy, not an assumed symmetry of f.
